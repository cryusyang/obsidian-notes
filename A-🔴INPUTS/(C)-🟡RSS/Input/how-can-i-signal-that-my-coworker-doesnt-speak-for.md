A reader writes:

一位读者来信写道：

<blockquote>My coworker, Chuckie, has concerns. A lot of concerns. They aren’t necessarily unfounded — I would say about 50% are completely justified, 40% have some foundation but are overblown, either mildly or significantly, and 10% are ridiculous — but he tends to bring them up with the attitude of a beleaguered martyr airing grievances rather than a professional colleague addressing work issues. He often talks at length about his own stress and frustration and implies (or even outright states) that no one outside of our department cares about the work we do or the people we serve.</blockquote>

我的同事查基（Chuckie）有很多顾虑——数量相当多。这些顾虑未必毫无根据：我估计其中约50%完全合理，40%有一定依据，但被夸大了（程度或轻或重），另有10%则纯属荒谬。然而，他提出这些问题时，往往摆出一副受尽委屈的殉道者姿态，仿佛在倾诉个人冤屈，而非以专业同事的身份讨论工作事项。他常常长篇大论地讲述自己的压力与挫败感，并暗示（甚至直接声称）：除了我们部门之外，再没人关心我们所做的工作，也不在意我们所服务的对象。

<blockquote>My main problem is that sometimes Chuckie raises issues in a way that implies he is speaking on behalf of me and our other five coworkers as well. Often, I agree with some of what he says — like, say Chuckie asks if I think that the bells on the new llama harnesses jangle too loudly (made-up example for anonymity’s sake), and I agree that they’re pretty annoying. But he thinks they’re loud enough that no one in the audience of the afternoon llama show will be able to hear the handler speaking. He also thinks the fact that the handling team didn’t consult our team indicates a serious communication breakdown between the two departments and has written up a 1,000-word email detailing “our” concerns and sent it to everyone in my department and both managers.</blockquote>

我最困扰的是：查基有时提出问题的方式，会让人误以为他是在代表我和另外五位同事发声。很多时候，我确实认同他部分观点——比如，他问我是否觉得新羊驼马具上的铃铛响得太刺耳（为保护隐私而虚构的例子），我承认那确实挺烦人。但他却认为铃声如此之响，以至于下午羊驼表演时观众根本听不清驯养员的讲解；他还断定，驯养团队未事先征询我们意见，说明两个部门间存在严重沟通断层，并为此撰写了一封长达千字的邮件，罗列“我们”的顾虑，发给了我们整个部门的所有同事及两位主管。

<blockquote>I try to be more solutions-oriented at work, and when I can, I’ll steer Chuckie’s complaints in that direction, which seems to be taken positively by our manager. But sometimes I don’t think there’s anything we can/should do. Sure, I would have liked the handling team to have consulted us before they made the purchase and would have brought up the bell issue, but I don’t think it’s my place to argue a fait accompli unless I have evidence of a serious problem in my area of expertise — like that the llamas are experiencing acute distress.</blockquote>

我在工作中努力保持“解决方案导向”，只要可能，我就会把查基的抱怨引向建设性方向——这似乎也得到了主管的正面反馈。但有时我觉得，这件事确实没什么可做、也不该由我们出手干预。诚然，我本希望驯养团队在采购前能先咨询我们，并就铃铛问题征询意见；但既然事已至此，若无确凿证据表明我的专业领域内存在严重问题（例如羊驼正经历急性应激反应），我认为自己并无立场去质疑既成事实。

<blockquote>He’ll use “our concerns” and “we feel” pretty consistently both in writing and in person, but when it’s in person the problems are usually smaller, and he’ll turn to us at some point for confirmation, at which point I can pivot to solutions and use softer language. It is still very awkward and I would love to not have to do it, but it’s a low-level tension. (I often feel particular pressure to respond because Chuckie and I have more experience and are generally more proactive than our other colleagues, who tend to be quiet in meetings. I am probably the person who brings the second-highest number of concerns to the table, and I couldn’t swear that my tone or word choice has been 100% perfect, either. I think my lapses are milder and rarer than Chuckie’s but I’m wary of being lumped in as The Two Who Complain.)</blockquote>

无论书面还是当面交流，他都频繁使用“我们的顾虑”和“我们觉得”这类表述。不过当面沟通时，涉及的问题通常较小，且他常会在某个节点转向我们寻求确认——这时我便有机会转向解决方案，并采用更温和的措辞。尽管如此，整个过程依然非常尴尬，我真心不想每次都这么应对，但这种张力确实只是低强度的。（我常常感到特别有压力要做出回应，因为查基和我资历更深、主动性更强，而其他同事在会议上往往比较沉默。我大概是提出顾虑第二多的人，我也不能保证自己的语气或措辞每次都100%得体。我认为自己的失误比查基更轻微、更少见，但我很担心被一并归入“爱抱怨二人组”。）

<blockquote>His snippy emails only happen a few times a year but I typically find them harder to respond to, both due to the medium and due to the fact that the problems either have no easy solutions or aren’t ours to solve. (I think he saves the tough problems for email so he can plan out the language he wants to use.) Sometimes he will raise an issue with me first, sort of taking my temperature, and I’ll express mild agreement, only to be taken by surprise when an email goes out soon after. I usually just don’t reply, if I think I can get away with it, and mostly a manager will respond to the substance of the email without commenting on the tone. Chuckie might grumble a bit to me and our coworkers in person, but not for very long, until the next problem arises.</blockquote>

他那些尖刻的邮件一年只出现几次，但我通常更难应对——既因媒介本身（文字缺乏语境），也因这些问题要么没有简单解法，要么根本不在我们的职责范围内。（我想他特意把棘手问题留到邮件里处理，以便精心措辞。）有时他会先就某件事跟我沟通，类似试探我的态度，而我仅表示轻微认同，结果却在他随后发出的邮件中猝不及防地被代表了。如果自认可以不回复，我通常就干脆沉默；多数时候主管会就邮件内容实质作答，却并不评论其语气。查基可能会当面跟我和其他同事略作牢骚，但不会持续太久，很快又会有下一个问题冒出来。

<blockquote>I should also mention that, due to some internal reorganization, our day-to-day supervision has changed hands a couple of times in the five years we’ve been working together, so this pattern is probably more obvious to me than some of our supervisors.</blockquote>

我还想补充一点：由于公司内部架构调整，过去五年间，我们日常工作的直属主管已更换过几次，因此这种模式在我眼中可能比某些主管看得更清楚。

<blockquote>What do I do? If I keep silent, that feels like I’m endorsing Chuckie’s overreaction, which reflects poorly on me. If I say “I don’t agree with his concerns at all,” that feels dishonest — and I don’t want to endorse the handing team’s decision either, because I do think it was a bad call, just not a disastrous one. What I really want is a professional way to say, “I basically agree with Chuckie but without all the histrionics.” Does that even exist?</blockquote>

我该怎么办？若保持沉默，感觉就像默许了查基的过度反应，这会损害我的职业形象；若说“我完全不认同他的顾虑”，又显得不诚实——而且我也不想支持驯养团队的决定，毕竟我确实认为那是次糟糕的决策，只是尚未达到灾难性程度。我真正需要的，是一种专业得体的表达方式，来传递这样的意思：“我基本认同查基的观点，但不接受他那种戏剧化的渲染。”这种表达方式真的存在吗？

It does exist!

它确实存在！

When it happens in person and Chuckie is using “our concerns” and “we feel,” you can correct that! For example:

当面交流中，若查基使用“我们的顾虑”或“我们觉得”，你完全可以礼貌纠正！例如：

* &#8220;I agree the new harness bells are annoying, but I don’t feel that strongly about it. I’m okay with deferring to the llama handling team on this.&#8221;

* “我同意新马具上的铃铛确实烦人，但我对此感受没那么强烈。这件事上，我愿意尊重驯养团队的决定。”

* “I hear the concern, but I don’t think Chuckie is speaking for the whole group on this. I don’t disagree in principle, but I also don’t feel that strongly about it.”

* “我理解这个顾虑，但我不认为查基在此事上能代表整个团队。原则上我并不反对，但我的感受也没那么强烈。”

* “I hear the concern, but I also don’t think Chuckie is speaking for the group on this. I don’t disagree in principle — and I told him I agreed the bells were annoying when we talked about it — but I should have made it clearer that I don’t feel that strongly about it.&#8221;

* “我理解这个顾虑，但同样不认为查基能代表团队发声。原则上我不反对——之前聊起这事时，我也告诉过他铃铛确实烦人——只是我当时没明确强调：我对这个问题的感受其实没那么强烈。”

* “Eh, I agree the bells are annoying and I wish they would have consulted us, but I don’t think there’s anything we need to do about it now.&#8221;

* “嗯，我同意铃铛是挺烦人，也希望他们采购前能先跟我们商量；但眼下我觉得我们没必要再采取什么行动了。”

You can also talk to him after the next meeting where he does this and say something like, “You’ve been presenting things as ‘our concerns’ and ‘we feel’ but I would rather you not speak on behalf of the group without our explicit agreement beforehand. Sometimes it ends up not accurately representing my stance — often because I don’t feel as strongly as you do — and I don’t want to end up distracting from what you’re saying if I have to interject to clarify that.” Or even just, “Hey, you made it sound like I fully agreed with you on this, but I don’t actually share your take in the way you explained it. I would rather you just speak for yourself when you’re raising this stuff, and I will speak for myself as well.&#8221;

你也可以在他下次会议中再次这样操作后，单独找他沟通，例如：“你最近常把事情表述为‘我们的顾虑’‘我们觉得’，但我希望你在未经我们明确同意前，不要代表整个团队发言。有时这并不能准确反映我的立场——往往是因为我的感受不如你强烈——而如果我不得不中途插话澄清，反而会分散大家对你观点的注意力。” 或者更直白些：“嘿，你刚才听起来好像我完全赞同你的看法，但实际上，我并不认同你阐述此事的方式。今后你提这类问题时，不妨只代表你自己；而我若有不同想法，也会主动为自己发声。”

With the emails, you might be able to use a similar format — “I understand where Chuckie is coming from, but now that they’ve ordered the bells, it’s probably easiest to just live with it. We could talk to them about checking with us before they place their next order though.” In other words, a mild correction about where you stand, and a pivot to a solution.

对于邮件，你也可采用类似策略——“我理解查基的出发点，但既然铃铛已经下单，眼下最省事的做法或许是接受现状。不过，我们倒是可以跟他们沟通一下，建议下次采购前先征求我们的意见。” 换言之，这是对你自身立场的一次温和澄清，同时迅速转向解决方案。

You can also try warding all of this off more preemptively, when Chuckie first raises issues with you. You know from experience that if you express mild agreement, there’s a good chance he’ll relay that as strong agreement later. So instead, you could try changing the responses you’re giving him — leaning more on things like, “Eh, I don’t feel that strongly about it” or “I think it’s probably fine/not worth the capital/something we shouldn’t bother pursuing.&#8221;

你还可以更主动地预防此类情况，在查基第一次向你提出问题时就加以引导。你凭经验知道：一旦你表达轻微认同，他极有可能后续将其转述为强烈共识。因此，你可以尝试调整自己的回应方式——更多采用类似“嗯，我对这事感受没那么强烈”或“我觉得大概没问题／不值得投入资源／没必要继续追究”这类表达。

Also, though, if you have a decent relationship with your current manager, you might just address it directly with her: “I’ve noticed Chuckie will sometimes word things as if he’s speaking for the group when he raises concerns, but I don’t always agree with him or at least don’t feel as strongly, so I wanted to clarify that. I’ll always speak up myself if I do feel strongly about something.&#8221;

此外，如果你与现任主管关系尚可，也可直接向她说明：“我注意到查基在提出顾虑时，有时措辞会让人误以为他在代表整个团队发声；但事实上我并非总与他意见一致，至少我的感受没那么强烈——所以我想提前澄清这一点。今后若我真对某事感受强烈，一定会亲自发声。”
