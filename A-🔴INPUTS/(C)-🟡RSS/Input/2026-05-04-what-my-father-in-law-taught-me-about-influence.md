<p style="font-weight: 400;">Last Saturday we said goodbye to my father-in-law, Charles “Chuck” Baily. For 38 years, he and I shared a relationship filled with plenty of back-and-forth good natured teasing. For example:</p>
<ul>
<li>After finishing a biography on Abraham Lincoln that I suggested, I asked him if he voted for Lincoln or Douglass.</li>
<li>He once asked about the Bible passage that says it’s hard for a rich man to enter the kingdom of heaven. I told him, “Chuck, don’t worry. You don’t have <em>that much</em>”</li>
<li>One Thanksgiving, when I walked in the door with Jane, he thanked her for bringing the turkey.</li>
</ul>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">But underneath our banter was something far more meaningful—deep respect and gratitude. As I reflect on his 98 years, I’m struck by a simple truth: <strong>A well-lived life doesn’t just end—it multiplies through the lives impacted.</strong> And Chuck’s life is a powerful example of that.</p>
<h3 style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>The Gift That Changed Everything</strong></h3>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">First and foremost, I’m thankful to Chuck because of the daughter he gave me.</p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">Anyone who knows Jane knows her warmth, strength, and caring heart. Those qualities didn’t appear out of nowhere. They were shaped, in large part, by the man who raised her.</p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">This is something we often overlook when we think about influence. We tend to focus on what we say or do in the moment. But one of the most powerful forms of influence is <strong>how we shape others over time simply by being who we are</strong>.</p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">Chuck didn’t just raise children. He helped form people who now impact others in more ways than he realized. That’s influence at its best—quiet, steady, and enduring.</p>
<h3 style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>Generosity That Didn’t Keep Score</strong></h3>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">Chuck also taught our family what generosity really looks like.</p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">He wasn’t generous only when it was convenient or when it benefited him. He gave freely—his time, his resources, his attention—to family and to anyone who needed help.</p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">In the science of influence, we often talk about reciprocity—the natural human desire to give back when we receive. But Chuck lived something deeper. He gave without expecting anything in return.</p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">Ironically, that kind of generosity tends to create the strongest bonds. People remember it. They’re shaped by it. And often, they pass it on. That’s how influence multiplies.</p>
<h3 style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>Modeling Love</strong></h3>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">Perhaps the most powerful lesson Chuck gave us was about love.</p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">He made a promise to his wife, Elinor, early in their marriage: no matter what happened, he would never leave her alone in a nursing home. And he kept that promise.</p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">When her health declined, he didn’t look for options because he knew what would be done—he converted their apartment and arranged for round-the-clock care so she could remain in her home.</p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">On their 72nd anniversary, she passed away—holding his hand.</p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">Think about that for a moment. In a world where commitments are often conditional, Chuck showed us what unconditional loved looked like. He was <strong>consistent </strong>in word and deed—one of the most powerful principles of influence. His actions aligned with his words for decades. Not weeks, months, or years. Decades!</p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">That kind of consistency doesn’t just build credibility—it builds legacy.</p>
<h3 style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>The Legacy of a Life Well Lived</strong></h3>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">Chuck was a father to five children, a grandfather to eleven, a great-grandfather to thirty-seven, and even a great-great-grandfather. Think about that, 55 people came into the world, and it all started with Chuck and Elinor Baily.</p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">He was born in Carmichaels Pennsylvania, population less than 600, in 1927. He served in the Navy in the Pacific after joining in 1945, right at the close of WWII.</p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">Those facts are impressive. But they’re not what define him.</p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">What defines him is the way he lived: His generosity. His commitment. His love.</p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">And here’s the part I find most meaningful: <strong>For those who were paying attention, his influence didn’t stop with him.</strong> It continues through his children, grandchildren, and everyone who experienced his life.</p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">That’s the real measure of influence—not what happens while we’re here, but what continues because we were here.</p>
<h3 style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>A Question Worth Asking</strong></h3>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">As I think about Chuck’s life, I can’t help but turn the lens inward.</p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">What kind of influence am I having? Not just today. But over time.</p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">Am I living in a way that multiplies good in others? Because in the end, what lasts are the people we impact and the relationships that are built.</p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">Chuck will be deeply missed. But his legacy is far from over. It’s living—and growing—in all of us who knew him and everyone we come in contact with.</p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">Who has influenced your life in a way that still shapes you today?</p>

## 中文译文

上周六，我们送别了我的岳父查尔斯·“查克”·贝利（Charles “Chuck” Baily）。38年来，我们之间始终充满善意而轻松的相互调侃。例如：

在我推荐他读完一本亚伯拉罕·林肯的传记后，我问他：“您当年是投了林肯的票，还是道格拉斯的票？”

他曾问起《圣经》中那句“财主进天国是难的”。我笑着答道：“查克，别担心，您可没那么‘富有’。”

有一年感恩节，当我与简（Jane）一起进门时，他向她道谢，感谢她带来了火鸡。

然而，在这些玩笑话之下，却蕴藏着远为深刻的东西——深切的尊重与由衷的感激。当我回望他长达98年的人生时，一个朴素的真理愈发清晰：一段充实丰盈的人生，并非戛然而止，而是通过所影响的生命不断延展、繁衍。而查克的一生，正是这一真理最有力的印证。

改变一切的馈赠  

首先，我要感谢查克，因为他将女儿简赐予了我。

凡认识简的人，无不感受到她的温暖、坚韧与仁爱之心。这些品质并非凭空而来，而是在很大程度上，由抚养她长大的那个人——查克——所塑造。

当我们思考影响力时，常常忽略这一点：我们往往只关注当下说了什么、做了什么；但最具力量的影响力，恰恰在于我们以自身存在的方式，日复一日、潜移默化地塑造他人。

查克不只是养育了孩子，更参与塑造了一批人——他们如今正以查克自己都未曾意识到的方式，持续影响着更多人。这，便是影响力最本真、最恒久的模样：静默、坚定、绵长。

从不计较得失的慷慨  

查克也向我们全家诠释了何谓真正的慷慨。

他的慷慨，从不取决于是否方便，也不取决于是否对自己有利。他总是慷慨地付出时间、资源与专注——既给予家人，也给予任何需要帮助的人。

在影响力科学中，我们常谈及“互惠原则”——即人们在受惠后自然萌生回报的意愿。但查克践行的，是一种更深沉的境界：他给予，却不期待任何回馈。

颇具反讽意味的是，这种不求回报的慷慨，反而最能缔结牢固的情感纽带。人们铭记它，被它塑造，并常常将它传递下去。这，正是影响力得以倍增的方式。

爱的榜样  

或许，查克教给我们最深刻的一课，是关于爱。

早在与妻子艾莉诺（Elinor）新婚之初，他就郑重承诺：无论发生什么，他绝不会让她独自一人住在养老院。他信守了这个承诺。

当艾莉诺健康状况恶化时，他并未四处寻找替代方案——因为他早已清楚自己会怎么做：他将自家公寓改造为适老居所，并安排全天候照护，只为让她能安住于熟悉而温暖的家中。

在他们结婚72周年纪念日那天，艾莉诺在他手中安详离世。

请稍作停顿，细细体会这一刻。在这个承诺常附带条件的世界里，查克为我们展现了何谓无条件的爱。他言行如一，数十年如一日——不是数周、数月，甚至也不是数年，而是整整数十年！这是影响力中最强大的原则之一。

这样的持守，所建立的不只是可信度，更是不朽的遗产。

丰盛人生的传承  

查克是五个孩子的父亲、十一位孙辈的祖父、三十七位曾孙辈的曾祖父，甚至已是玄孙辈的高祖父。试想一下：55个人来到这个世界，其源头，正是查克与艾莉诺·贝利夫妇。

他1927年出生于宾夕法尼亚州卡迈克尔镇（Carmichaels），全镇人口不足600人；1945年二战尾声之际加入美国海军，赴太平洋服役。

这些事实令人钦佩，却并非定义他的核心。

真正定义他的，是他如何生活：他的慷慨、他的信守、他的爱。

而最令我动容的是：对那些用心观察的人来说，查克的影响力并未随他离去而终结——它正借由他的子女、孙辈，以及所有曾与他生命交汇过的人，持续流淌、生长。

这才是衡量影响力的真正标尺：不在于我们尚在之时发生了什么，而在于我们曾经存在，为世界留下了何种延续。

一个值得自问的问题  

当我思索查克的一生，我不禁将目光转向自身。

我正在产生怎样的影响力？不仅限于今天，而是长久以来？

我的生活方式，是否正在让善在他人身上不断繁衍？因为归根结底，真正留下的，是我们所影响的人，以及由此缔结的关系。

查克将被深深怀念。但他的遗产远未终结——它正活在每一个认识他的人心中，并通过我们，继续延伸至所有与我们相遇的生命之中。

那么，谁曾以至今仍在塑造你的方式，影响过你的人生？
