<h3>&#8220;The mind narrates what the nervous system knows. Story follows state.&#8221;</h3>
<hr />
<p><a href="https://www.amazon.com/Polyvagal-Theory-Therapy-Interpersonal-Neurobiology/dp/0393712370/?tag=braipick-20" target="_blank"><img alt="Polyvagal Theory and the Neurobiology of Connection: The Science of Rupture, Repair, and Reciprocity" class="cover alignright size-medium" height="487" src="https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/polyvagal_dana.jpg?fit=320%2C487&amp;ssl=1" width="320" /></a></p><p>&#8220;A purely disembodied human emotion is a nonentity,&#8221; William James wrote in his <a href="https://www.themarginalian.org/2016/01/11/what-is-an-emotion-william-james/">pioneering 1884 theory</a> of how our bodies affect our feelings &#8212; the first great gauntlet thrown at the Cartesian dualism of body versus mind. In the century and a half since, we have come to see <a href="https://www.themarginalian.org/2016/06/20/the-body-keeps-the-score-van-der-kolk/">how the body and the mind converge in the healing of trauma</a>; we have come to see consciousness itself <a href="https://www.themarginalian.org/2021/12/24/feeling-knowing-damasio/">as a full-body phenomenon</a>. </p>
<p>Beyond the brain, no portion of the body shapes our mental and emotional landscape more profoundly than the tenth cranial nerve &#8212; the longest nerve of the autonomic nervous system that unconsciously governs the inner workings of the body. Known as the vagus nerve &#8212; from the Latin for &#8220;wandering,&#8221; a root shared with <em>vagabond</em> and <em>vague</em> &#8212; it meanders from the brain to the gut, touching every organ along the way with its tendrils, controlling everything from our heart rate and digestion to our reflexes and moods. </p>
<figure class="wp-caption aligncenter" id="attachment_60067"><a href="https://www.themarginalian.org/2017/02/23/beautiful-brain-santiago-ramon-y-cajal/"><img alt="" class="size-full wp-image-60067" height="887" src="https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/02/BeautifulBrain_p064.jpg?resize=680%2C887&#038;ssl=1" width="680" /></a><figcaption class="wp-caption-text">One of Santiago Ramón y Cajal&#8217;s <a href="https://www.themarginalian.org/2017/02/23/beautiful-brain-santiago-ramon-y-cajal/">little-known drawings of the brain</a>.</figcaption></figure>
<p>In James&#8217;s lifetime, it was believed that synaptic communication within the brain was electrical. But when neuroscience founding father <a href="https://www.themarginalian.org/2017/03/17/diseases-of-the-will-cajal-advice-for-a-young-investigator/">Santiago Ramón y Cajal</a> discovered a gap between neurons &#8212; a miniature abyss electricity could not cross &#8212; it became clear that something else must be transmitting the signals between neurons. In 1921, the German pharmacologist Otto Loewi confirmed the existence of these theorized chemical messengers by stimulating the vagus nerve of a frog and discovering in the secreted substance the first known neurotransmitter. Every thought, feeling, and mood that has ever swept across the sky of your mind was forecast by your neurotransmitters and executed by your vagus nerve.</p>
<p>A century after James, while working with premature babies, the psychiatrist Stephen Porges uncovered two distinct vagal pathways in the nervous system &#8212; the much older dorsal vagus, which evolved around 500 million years ago in a fish now extinct to regulate fear response and activate shutdown, and the ventral vagus, a uniquely mammalian development about 200 million old, controlling our capacity for connection and communication. This research became the foundation of polyvagal theory &#8212; the science of how the interplay of these two systems shapes our sense of safety and danger, shapes our attachment styles and relationship patterns, shapes our very ability to tolerate the risks of living necessary for being in love with life. </p>
<p>In the decades since, no one has championed polyvagal theory more ardently than the clinical psychologist Deb Dana. In her book <a href="https://www.amazon.com/Polyvagal-Theory-Therapy-Interpersonal-Neurobiology/dp/0393712370/?tag=braipick-20" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><strong><em>The Polyvagal Theory in Therapy: Engaging the Rhythm of Regulation</em></strong></a> (<a href="https://search.worldcat.org/title/988292557" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><em>public library</em></a>), written for therapists, she explores how trauma automates our adaptive responses in a survival story that puts the fear-based dorsal vagus in command to induce collapse and dissociation, and how we can rewire our neural pathways toward the emotional safety of the ventral vagal state, where our capacity for curiosity, connection, and change flourishes. </p>
<figure class="wp-caption aligncenter" id="attachment_76837"><a href="https://www.themarginalian.org/2022/05/12/sophie-blackall-things-to-look-forward-to/"><img alt="" class="size-full wp-image-76837" height="811" src="https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/sophieblackall_thingstolookforwardto_FallinginLove.jpg?resize=680%2C811&#038;ssl=1" width="680" /></a><figcaption class="wp-caption-text">Art by Sophie Blackall from <a href="https://www.themarginalian.org/2022/05/12/sophie-blackall-things-to-look-forward-to/"><em>Things to Look Forward to</em></a></figcaption></figure>
<p>Dana writes:</p>
<blockquote><p>Connectedness is a biological imperative, and at the top of the autonomic hierarchy is the ventral vagal pathway that supports feelings of safety and connection. The ventral vagus (sometimes called the “smart vagus” or “social vagus”) provides the neurobiological foundation for health, growth, and restoration. When the ventral vagus is active, our attention is toward connection. We seek opportunities for co-regulation. The ability to soothe and be soothed, to talk and listen, to offer and receive, to fluidly move in and out of connection is centered in this newest part of the autonomic nervous system. Reciprocity, the mutual ebb and flow that defines nourishing relationships, is a function of the ventral vagus. As a result of its myelinated pathways, the ventral vagus provides rapid and organized responses. In a ventral vagal state, we have access to a range of responses including calm, happy, meditative, engaged, attentive, active, interested, excited, passionate, alert, ready, relaxed, savoring, and joyful.</p></blockquote>
<p>This biological need for co-regulation with others is not dissimilar to the concept of <a href="https://www.themarginalian.org/2021/12/04/general-theory-of-love-music-emotion/"><em>limbic revision</em></a> &#8212; &#8220;the power to remodel the emotional parts of the people we love,&#8221; and to have our own emotional pathways remodeled by the people who love us. This is only possible in safe relationships, and it is the vagus system that governs our sense of safety. </p>
<p>Central to polyvagal theory is the distinction between conscious perception and what Porges termed neuroception &#8212; the conditioned way the autonomic nervous system responds from within the body, without our awareness, to cues of safety and danger in the outside world. Because our vagal pathways are shaped by our earliest experiences of co-regulation in the infant-parent dyad, ruptures in that co-regulation &#8212; whether by abuse or neglect &#8212; condition the dorsal vagus to become dominant and make a neuroception of danger the default response, storying reality away from safety, nowhere more perilously than in intimate relationships. Dana writes: </p>
<blockquote><p>Co-regulation is at the heart of positive relationships&#8230; If we miss opportunities to co-regulate in childhood, we feel that loss in our adult relationships. Trauma, either in experiences of commission (acts of harm) or omission (absence of care), makes co-regulation dangerous and interrupts the development of our co-regulatory skills. Out of necessity, the autonomic nervous system is shaped to independently regulate. Clients will often say that they needed connection but there was no one in their life who was safe, so after a while they stopped looking. Through a polyvagal perspective, we know that although they stopped explicitly looking and found ways to navigate on their own, their autonomic nervous system never stopped needing, and longing for, co-regulation.</p></blockquote>
<figure class="wp-caption alignnone"><a href="https://www.themarginalian.org/2017/10/12/big-wolf-little-wolf/"><img src="https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/bigwolflittlewolf8.jpg" /></a><figcaption class="wp-caption-text">Art by Olivier Tallec from <a href="https://www.themarginalian.org/2017/10/12/big-wolf-little-wolf/"><em>Big Wolf &#038; Little Wolf</em></a></figcaption></figure>
<p>Because we are physiologies first and psychologies second, but we are also storytelling and sensemaking creatures, our minds naturally create emotional narratives out of these unconscious vagal states &#8212; stories that, if we are not careful enough and conscious enough, may come to subsume reality. Dana observes:</p>
<blockquote><p>The mind narrates what the nervous system knows. Story follows state.</p></blockquote>
<p>Our early adaptive survival responses of trauma train the autonomic nervous system on a default neuroception of danger, replacing patterns of connection with patterns of protection in a fear-based narrative. And yet these reflexes can be recalibrated by retraining our regulatory pathways. </p>
<p>Because the feeling of reciprocity is one of the most powerful regulators of the autonomic nervous system, a great deal of repair and rewiring can happen in relationships winged with true reciprocity. Dana writes:</p>
<blockquote><p>Reciprocity is a connection between people that is created in the back-and-forth communication between two autonomic nervous systems. It is the experience of heartfelt listening and responding. We are nourished in experiences of reciprocity, feeling the ebb and flow, giving and receiving, attunement, and resonance.</p></blockquote>
<figure class="wp-caption aligncenter" id="attachment_82570"><a href="https://www.themarginalian.org/2012/04/27/the-human-body-1959/"><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-82575" height="357" src="https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/thehumanbody_TheMarginalian_copyright.jpg?resize=680%2C357&#038;ssl=1" width="680" /></a><figcaption class="wp-caption-text">Art from <a href="https://www.themarginalian.org/2012/04/27/the-human-body-1959/"><em>The Human Body</em></a>, 1959.</figcaption></figure>
<p>But the great paradox is that if our earliest template of connection is marked by rupture and deficient co-regulation, our very notion of reciprocity may be warped, leading us to tolerate immense asymmetries of affection and attention, to mistake deeply imbalanced relationships for reciprocal. The grounds for optimism lie in the very real possibility of changing the template through safe and nourishing relationships &#8212; ones we may not so much choose at first, for trauma can taint our choices with unhealthy patterns, as chance into and only then choose to nurture. The payoff is a gradual transition from the dorsal vagal state into the ventral vagal, a gradual willingness to release the patterns of protection in favor of connection, allowing the kinds of relationships Adrienne Rich <a href="https://www.themarginalian.org/2013/07/02/adrienne-rich-honorable-human-relationship/">celebrated</a> as ones &#8220;in which two people have the right to use the word &#8216;love.'&#8221;</p>
<p>Complement with the science of <a href="https://www.themarginalian.org/2024/02/23/how-emotions-are-made/">how emotion are made</a> and <a href="https://www.themarginalian.org/2021/12/04/general-theory-of-love-music-emotion/">how love rewires the brain</a>, then revisit Toni Morrison on <a href="https://www.themarginalian.org/2021/01/03/toni-morrison-beloved-heart/">reclaiming the body as an instrument of joy, sanity, and self-love</a>.</p>

## 中文译文

“心灵讲述着神经系统所知晓的一切。故事紧随状态之后。”

威廉·詹姆斯（William James）在其开创性的1884年情感理论中写道：“纯粹脱离身体的人类情绪根本不存在。”——这是对笛卡尔式身心二元论（即身体与心灵彼此割裂）投出的第一支重要挑战之矛。一个半世纪以来，我们逐渐认识到：身体与心灵如何在创伤疗愈中交汇融合；我们甚至开始将意识本身视作一种全身心的现象。

除大脑之外，没有任何身体部位比第十对脑神经——即迷走神经——更能深刻地塑造我们的心理与情绪图景。迷走神经是自主神经系统中最长的一条神经，它在无意识中调控着人体内部运作。其名源自拉丁语“vagus”，意为“游荡”，与“流浪者”（vagabond）和“模糊的”（vague）同源；它自脑部蜿蜒而下，直抵肠道，沿途以细密分支轻触每一处器官，调控着从心率、消化到反射乃至情绪的一切功能。

这是圣地亚哥·拉蒙-卡哈尔（Santiago Ramón y Cajal）鲜为人知的一幅大脑素描。

在詹姆斯生活的年代，人们普遍认为大脑内的突触传递纯属电传导。然而，当神经科学奠基人拉蒙-卡哈尔发现神经元之间存在一道间隙——一道电流无法跨越的微小深渊——人们才意识到，必有某种其他物质在神经元之间传递信号。1921年，德国药理学家奥托·洛伊（Otto Loewi）通过刺激青蛙的迷走神经，并在所分泌物质中确认了首个已知神经递质的存在，从而证实了这类假想化学信使的真实存在。你脑海中曾掠过的每一个念头、每一种感受、每一份情绪，皆由你的神经递质预先“预报”，并由你的迷走神经最终执行。

詹姆斯逝世一个世纪后，精神科医生斯蒂芬·波尔格斯（Stephen Porges）在早产儿临床工作中，发现了神经系统中两条截然不同的迷走神经通路：一条是更为古老的背侧迷走神经，约5亿年前在一种现已灭绝的鱼类中演化而成，负责调节恐惧反应并触发“宕机”（shutdown）；另一条则是腹侧迷走神经，为哺乳动物所独有，约2亿岁高龄，主管我们的联结能力与沟通能力。这项研究构成了“多迷走神经理论”（polyvagal theory）的基础——这门科学揭示了这两套系统如何相互作用，塑造我们对安全与危险的感知，塑造我们的依恋类型与关系模式，甚至塑造我们承受生活风险的能力——而这正是热爱生命所必需的前提。

此后数十年间，临床心理学家黛布·达娜（Deb Dana）成为多迷走神经理论最坚定有力的倡导者。她在面向治疗师撰写的著作《多迷走神经理论在治疗中的应用：参与调节的节律》（The Polyvagal Theory in Therapy: Engaging the Rhythm of Regulation，公共图书馆藏书）中探讨道：创伤会自动化我们的适应性反应，编织出一则以生存为核心的叙事，令基于恐惧的背侧迷走神经占据主导，诱发崩溃与解离；而我们则可通过神经通路重塑，转向腹侧迷走神经所支撑的情绪安全状态，在此状态下，我们的好奇心、联结力与改变力方能蓬勃生长。

索菲·布莱克尔（Sophie Blackall）绘《值得期待的事》（Things to Look Forward to）

达娜写道：

联结是一种生物学上的必然需求；而在自主神经系统的层级结构顶端，正是支持安全感与联结感的腹侧迷走神经通路。腹侧迷走神经（有时被称为“智慧迷走神经”或“社会迷走神经”）为健康、成长与修复提供了神经生物学基础。当腹侧迷走神经活跃时，我们的注意力自然朝向联结；我们主动寻求共调节（co-regulation）的机会。安抚与被安抚、言说与倾听、给予与接纳、自如进出联结状态的能力，皆根植于这一自主神经系统中最晚近演化的部分。互惠性——定义滋养型人际关系的那种双向流动、彼此呼应——正是腹侧迷走神经的功能。得益于其髓鞘化通路，腹侧迷走神经可提供快速而有序的反应。处于腹侧迷走神经状态时，我们能调用一系列丰富反应：平静、愉悦、冥想、投入、专注、活跃、好奇、兴奋、热忱、警觉、预备、放松、品味与欢欣。

这种与他人共调节的生物学需求，与“边缘系统重写”（limbic revision）概念并无本质不同——即“重塑我们所爱之人情感部分的力量”，以及让爱我们的人反过来重塑我们自身的情感通路。而这一切，唯有在安全的关系中才可能发生；而主宰我们安全感的，正是迷走神经系统。

多迷走神经理论的核心，在于区分有意识的知觉与波尔格斯所称的“神经知觉”（neuroception）——即自主神经系统在我们毫无察觉的情况下，依据身体内部状态，对外界安全或危险线索所做出的条件化反应。由于我们的迷走神经通路是在婴儿期与父母构成的二元关系中，通过早期共调节经验塑形而成，因此，若该阶段的共调节出现断裂——无论是源于虐待抑或忽视——都会导致背侧迷走神经趋于主导，使“神经知觉”将危险设为默认反应，进而将现实叙事不断推向远离安全的方向，尤其在亲密关系中，这种偏移尤为危险。达娜写道：

共调节是积极关系的核心……倘若我们在童年时期错失了共调节的机会，成年后的关系中便会感受到这种缺失。无论是因“作为型创伤”（commission，即施加伤害的行为），还是“不作为型创伤”（omission，即关爱的缺席），创伤都会令共调节变得危险，并中断我们共调节能力的发展。出于生存所需，自主神经系统被迫发展出独立调节的能力。来访者常表示：“我需要联结，但生命中却无人让我感到安全”，久而久之，他们便停止了寻找。而从多迷走神经视角来看，我们深知：尽管他们已不再主动寻求，也找到了独自应对的方式，但其自主神经系统从未停止对共调节的渴求与呼唤。

创伤所塑造的早期适应性生存反应，会训练自主神经系统将“危险”的神经知觉设为默认模式，以保护性模式取代联结性模式，构建起一种基于恐惧的叙事。然而，这些反射仍可通过重新训练我们的调节通路而得以校准。

由于互惠感是自主神经系统最强大的调节器之一，真正具备互惠性的关系中，便蕴藏着大量修复与神经重塑的可能。达娜写道：

互惠性，是人际联结的一种形式，诞生于两个自主神经系统之间往复交流的过程。它是发自内心的倾听与回应的体验。我们在互惠性体验中获得滋养，感受其中的潮起潮落、给予与接纳、协调一致与共鸣共振。

但最大的悖论在于：倘若我们最早关于联结的模板，已被断裂与匮乏的共调节所标记，那么我们对“互惠性”本身的认知便可能已然扭曲——以致容忍情感与关注的巨大不对等，将严重失衡的关系误判为互惠关系。希望的根基，则在于一种切实可行的可能性：通过安全而滋养的关系，去改写这一原始模板。这类关系，我们或许并非一开始就能主动选择——因为创伤可能污染我们的判断，使选择陷入不健康的模式；而是往往经由偶然际遇进入，继而才主动选择去培育与深化。其回报，是逐步从背侧迷走神经状态过渡至腹侧迷走神经状态，逐步愿意放下保护性模式，转向联结；由此，我们终将能够拥有阿德里安娜·里奇（Adrienne Rich）所礼赞的那种关系——“两个人皆有权使用‘爱’这个词的关系”。

延伸阅读：情绪如何生成的科学，以及爱如何重塑大脑；另请重读托妮·莫里森（Toni Morrison）关于重拾身体作为喜悦、理智与自爱之工具的论述。
