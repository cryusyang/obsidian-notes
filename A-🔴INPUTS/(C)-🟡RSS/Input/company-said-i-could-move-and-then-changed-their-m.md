It’s five answers to five questions. Here we go…

这是五个问题的五个回答。我们开始吧……

<strong>1. Company said I could move after my husband took a new job and now they won’t let me</strong>

<strong>1. 公司曾说我丈夫换工作后我可以随迁，如今却反悔不让我搬</strong>

<blockquote><p>I work for a large corporation with a Remote First policy, and compensation is location-based with three tiers. While their policy says permanent relocations must be approved, there is no employee-facing material that says relocation is not allowed up a tier. For example, there is nothing saying someone in a lower cost of living city (Tier 3) who needs to move to a high cost of living city (Tier 1) will not be approved.</p></blockquote>

<blockquote><p>我在一家实行“远程优先”政策的大型企业工作，薪酬按所在地分为三个等级。虽然公司政策规定永久性异地办公需经批准，但面向员工的所有公开材料中均未说明“不得升档搬迁”。例如，没有任何条款指出：一位原本居住在生活成本较低城市（三级地区）的员工，若因家庭原因需迁往生活成本高昂城市（一级地区），其申请将不予批准。</p></blockquote>

<blockquote><p>My husband applied for and got a job in a Tier 1 location. He flew out to start the job (his start date was four days after the offer). I immediately told my managers, and they said they didn’t anticipate an issue so we prepared to move to the new location.   I’ve been with the company for five years, am a top performer, and have won several awards for performance. My direct managers and the org manager approved the move, but last week I learned that the upper leadership have not, due to a policy of not approving moves up location-based pay tiers. My managers had never even heard of this policy. My husband had already accepted the job, started work, came back to help me pack, and the day before all our items were to be shipped across the country we got this news.</p></blockquote>

<blockquote><p>我丈夫应聘并成功获得了一级地区的一份工作。他飞赴当地入职（录用通知发出四天后即开始上班）。我立刻向直属经理汇报，他们表示“应该不会有问题”，于是我们着手准备搬迁至新城市。   我已在该公司工作五年，是公认的高绩效员工，多次荣获绩效类奖项。我的直属经理及部门负责人均批准了此次搬迁；但上周我才得知，公司高层以“禁止跨薪酬等级搬迁”为由否决了申请——而我的经理们此前甚至从未听说过这项政策。此时，我丈夫已正式接受职位、开始上班，并专程返家协助我打包；就在我们所有物品即将发往新城市的前一天，我们收到了这一消息。</p></blockquote>

<blockquote><p>I have appealed the decision and my managers have outlined the business cases for me staying to leadership. But as it stands right now, I have 90 days where I can work from a non-home location but after that I’ll have to quit or be terminated. I asked HR if I could remain at my Tier 3 salary through the next review cycle (as I realize that budgets have been allocated, etc.) but that was denied since remaining at a different salary tier in a new location is not “company policy.”</p></blockquote>

<blockquote><p>我已就此决定提出申诉，我的经理们也向高层详细阐述了我留任的业务合理性。但目前的现实是：我仅有90天宽限期，可暂在非注册办公地远程工作；期满后，我将被迫辞职或被解雇。我曾询问HR：能否在下一个薪酬评审周期内继续维持三级地区的薪资标准（我理解预算早已分配完毕等客观限制）？但该请求被拒绝，理由是“在新地点沿用原薪酬等级不符合公司政策”。</p></blockquote>

<blockquote><p>I’m at my wits’ end. My husband has been out of work since Covid, this is an amazing opportunity, and he has already accepted the job. We can’t live in the new area without both of our salaries. I was definitely not expecting to lose my job because I moved, and I&#8217;m terrified of looking for a job in this market.</p></blockquote>

<blockquote><p>我已濒临崩溃。自疫情以来，我丈夫一直失业，这份工作是他难得的绝佳机会，且他已正式签约。若没有双方收入，我们根本无法在新城市生活。我万万没想到，仅仅因为一次搬迁就会丢掉工作；而当前就业市场如此严峻，我更对重新求职充满恐惧。</p></blockquote>

<blockquote><p>I’m currently going back home alone to try to buy as much time before my 90 days starts. My husband wants me to get a lawyer, but I don’t know if I have a case or if a lawyer could do anything since I’ve not been fired yet. My job is extremely specialized and I’m very happy in my current position and I just don’t know what to do.</p></blockquote>

<blockquote><p>目前，我独自返回原住地，试图在90天宽限期启动前争取更多缓冲时间。我丈夫建议我咨询律师，但我尚不确定自己是否具备诉讼基础——毕竟我尚未被解雇，律师也未必能有所作为。我的岗位高度专业化，我对当前工作也十分满意，眼下真的不知该如何是好。</p></blockquote>

I’m sorry, this is a mess. If your company promotes itself as “remote first” organization, they need to be much clearer with employees about what restrictions they have on that. If you can’t move to a location with a higher pay tier than your current one, it’s ridiculous that they haven’t proactively told people; their lack of transparency is what led to this, and it would have been so easy for them to avoid it.

很抱歉，这确实是一团乱麻。如果贵公司自诩为“远程优先”型组织，就必须向员工清晰阐明相关限制条件。倘若员工不得迁往高于当前薪酬等级的地区，公司却从未主动告知，那就极其荒谬；正是这种缺乏透明度的做法导致了今日困局——而本可以轻而易举地避免。

Are you able to tell your company that you’re going to stay where you are? Whether or not you really do plan to stay there long-term, telling them that would presumably stop the 90-day clock from ticking and buy you and your husband some time to decide what you want to do, which could include living in separate locations until one of you can move to the other, you looking for a job out there, or him coming back (basically returning to the situation from before he got the job, which would be brutal but is an option). But your company sucks for putting you in this position and not being willing to make an exception considering the circumstances, and particularly when you’d been told by multiple managers that it would be fine.

你能否告知公司：你将暂时留在现居地？无论你是否真打算长期留下，此举至少能暂停90天倒计时，为你和丈夫争取决策时间——例如分居两地，直至一方能迁就另一方；或你赴新城市求职；或他重返原城市（即回到他获聘前的状态，虽艰难但确为可行选项）。然而，将你置于这般境地、又拒绝根据实际情况破例的公司，实在令人失望——尤其当多位管理者都曾明确告知你“一切没问题”之时。

<strong>2. How do I lean out of my company’s DEI work?</strong>

<strong>2. 我该如何退出公司内的多元化、公平与包容（DEI）相关工作？</strong>

<blockquote><p>I&#8217;m a boomerang at my current company (meaning I left but have now returned). When I was previously employed here, I ended up leading our women&#8217;s ERG, as well as leading or being a critical stakeholder in a variety of DEI-related groups and activities. Ultimately, despite doing this work for several years, and passing the baton to capable passionate folks when I left, many of the key metrics related to increasing diversity at all levels but particularly in management have not changed or have changed for the worse. Ultimately, I have come to believe that the many extra hours of unpaid labor my colleagues and I contributed did little more than create good press for the firm. I think that if the firm is committed to the goals it ostensibly signed on to, then such efforts need to come from the top and include real numeric goals in hiring and promoting, along with resources for professional development, none of which were ever really forthcoming.</p></blockquote>

<blockquote><p>我是这家公司的“回流员工”（即曾离职、如今重返）。上一次任职期间，我曾牵头负责公司女性员工资源组（ERG），并主导或深度参与多项与多元化、公平与包容（DEI）相关的团体活动与项目。然而，尽管持续投入数年精力，且离职时已将工作交接给能力出众、热情饱满的同事，但公司在各级别（尤其是管理层）提升多元化的关键指标却毫无进展，甚至出现倒退。最终我意识到：我和同事们无偿投入的大量额外工时，几乎仅为企业博取了良好公关形象。我认为，若公司真心致力于其公开承诺的DEI目标，则相关举措必须自上而下推动，包括设定切实可衡量的招聘与晋升量化目标，并配套提供职业发展资源——而这些，始终未曾真正落地。</p></blockquote>

<blockquote><p>Now that I&#8217;m returning, I&#8217;m more interested in pursuing social justice goals outside the firm with organizations that demonstrate real commitment and effectiveness in their efforts. I know as part of our upcoming goal-setting conversation, I&#8217;m going to be encouraged to take up some of my old work and I absolutely won&#8217;t. We have volunteer PTO hours available, which I am happy to use for outside-the-office work. How do I thread this needle in conversations with my grandboss, who also happened to be the exec sponsor of the ERG I used to lead? (Honestly, I think my company has no business or claim on this stuff but I need to check a box, so…)</p></blockquote>

<blockquote><p>如今重返公司，我更希望投身于外部社会组织的公益事业——这些组织在推动社会公正方面展现出真实承诺与切实成效。我预见到，在即将开展的年度目标设定沟通中，我必然会被鼓励重新接手部分原有DEI工作；但这一点我绝不会答应。公司提供带薪志愿服务假（PTO），我乐于将其用于职场外的公益事务。那么，面对我的隔级主管（恰好也是我曾领导的女性ERG的高管赞助人），我该如何得体而坚定地表达这一立场？（坦白说，我认为公司对此类事务本无权干涉或索取，但流程上我又不得不“走个过场”，所以……）</p></blockquote>

If you’re asked to pick up that work again: “Oh, thanks for offering, but I’m not interested in stepping back into it again.” If you want, you can add, &#8220;I’d like to leave it with whoever has been handling it or give someone new a chance to take it on.” If you’re pressed about why, feel free to say, “I’ve realized that work needs to come from the top of a firm and people at lower levels aren’t well positioned to do it.” If pressed anyway: “I feel strongly about it, so I’m going to pass.” If you want you can add, “It’s something I work on a lot outside of work and I don’t want it to become part of my job here as well.”

若被再次邀请承担该项工作，你可回应：“哦，谢谢您的提议，但我无意重新接手。” 如需补充，可加上：“我希望将此项工作交由现任负责人继续推进，或让新人获得锻炼机会。” 若对方追问原因，可坦率表示：“我逐渐意识到，此类工作必须由公司高层发起并推动，基层员工实难有效承担。” 若仍被进一步施压，可坚定表态：“我对此事立场非常明确，因此只能婉拒。” 如有必要，还可补充：“这类事务我已在工作之外大量投入，不希望它再成为我本职工作的组成部分。”

<strong>3. Can I suggest my difficult boss get more emotional support?</strong>

<strong>3. 我能否建议那位令我困扰的上司寻求更多情绪支持？</strong>

<blockquote><p>I have a question about a boss who I don&#8217;t really like, but I also think he&#8217;s not a bad person so I don&#8217;t want anything bad to happen to him.</p></blockquote>

<blockquote><p>我想请教一个关于我上司的问题：我其实并不喜欢他，但同时我也认为他并非坏人，因此不希望他遭遇什么不幸。</p></blockquote>

<blockquote><p>I work in a law firm, and this guy is one of the salaried partners. He has bullied a couple of people out of the team, but always been very respectful to me. Even so, because of his behavior to others, I&#8217;m not a fan. He has always been a bit unreliable &#8212; there is not a single time he has gone on a business trip or holiday when I have been told in advance and had a plan. Every time, he just disappears, and then there are timezone issues that neither I nor the client were prepared for. But, by and large he has kept up with work and I&#8217;ve had a decent time learning from him.</p></blockquote>

<blockquote><p>我在一家律师事务所工作，这位上司是领薪合伙人之一。他曾排挤走团队中的几位同事，但对我始终非常尊重。即便如此，鉴于他对他人表现出的行为，我仍对他好感全无。他向来有些不可靠——每次出差或休假，我从未提前获知并做好相应安排；他总是突然失联，导致时区差异问题频发，令我与客户均措手不及。不过总体而言，他仍能跟进工作进度，我也从他身上学到了不少东西。</p></blockquote>

<blockquote><p>Recently, he has dropped the ball SO BADLY. Clients are complaining and he is continuing to disappear without warning, but he&#8217;s managing it even worse than he previously did. He is failing to turn up to calls that he&#8217;ll said he&#8217;ll attend. I know he is going through a divorce, although that&#8217;s been going on for eight months now, and it&#8217;s only recently that he&#8217;s gotten really bad.</p></blockquote>

<blockquote><p>近来，他的工作表现急剧恶化。客户频频投诉，他仍持续无预警失联，且应对方式比以往更加糟糕——连自己承诺出席的会议也屡屡缺席。我知道他正经历离婚，此事已持续八个月，但直到最近才真正变得严重。</p></blockquote>

<blockquote><p>I hate the way that he is affecting junior staff, and I have been communicating with management accordingly. However, I am also worried about him. I might not like him, but I don&#8217;t think he&#8217;s a bad person, and I think this really AWOL behavior is pretty worrisome, even taking into account his usual behavior. I think he is a typically manly man who has not considered the resources available for mental health issues. Is there any way I can check in with him, and offer him some judgment free support, without being inappropriate?</p></blockquote>

<blockquote><p>我极度反感他给初级员工带来的负面影响，也已就此向管理层如实反馈。但与此同时，我也为他本人感到担忧。或许我不喜欢他，但我并不认为他本质恶劣；而这种近乎“彻底失联”的行为，即使考虑到他一贯的表现，也着实令人忧心。我认为他属于典型的传统男性，尚未意识到心理健康支持资源的存在与价值。那么，是否存在一种得体的方式，让我向他表达关切、提供不带评判的支持？</p></blockquote>

You’re not the person best positioned to do that. His partners or other senior members of the firm are. You are positioned to do the piece that you’re already doing (communicating with management about what’s happening). You could certainly suggest to one of <i>them</i> (perhaps that most empathetic-seeming among them) that he might benefit from a nudge toward some support, but beyond that you’re just not well placed to do it. (It’s not that it would always be impossible to suggest that to someone with more power than you, but typically if you had that kind of rapport that would make it natural, you’d already know it.)

你并非最适合承担此事的人选——他的合伙人或其他律所高层才更合适。而你当前的角色，恰恰是你正在做的这件事：向管理层如实反映现状。你当然可以向其中某位高层（比如看起来最具同理心的那位）委婉建议：“他或许需要一些温和的提醒，引导其关注可获取的支持资源。”但除此之外，你确实难以再进一步介入。（这并非绝对意味着你永远不该向职权高于你的人提出此类建议；但通常而言，若你们之间本就存在足够信任与默契，让你觉得此举自然妥当，那你也早该意识到了。）

<strong>4. When employers say “we’ll keep your resume on file,” will they actually look at it again?</strong>

<strong>4. 当雇主说“我们会将你的简历存档”时，他们真的会再次查阅吗？</strong>

<blockquote><p>Do employers look at past resume submissions when a new job opens, or do they just look through the new submissions? Does &#8220;having a resume on file&#8221; actually mean anything?</p></blockquote>

<blockquote><p>当新职位开放时，雇主会查阅过往投递的简历，还是只审阅本轮新提交的申请？所谓“简历已存档”，究竟有何实际意义？</p></blockquote>

It varies. Some companies say it as part of their boilerplate rejection letters when the chances of them ever contacting you again are low. But other companies do look through past applicants when they have new openings, particularly for hard-to-fill roles and particularly when they need to hire again soon after a similar role just closed. People do get contacted by companies for openings they might be well matched with after previously being rejected. You just can’t really tell from the outside how likely it is. Either way, there’s no point in reading much into it, and if you see an opening there in the future that you’re interested in, proactively apply — don’t assume they’ll contact you.

情况因公司而异。部分企业仅在标准拒信中例行公事般使用此话术，实则再度联系你的可能性极低；但也有不少公司确会在新岗位开放时翻阅过往申请人档案——尤其针对难以填补的职位，或当类似岗位刚结束招聘、急需再次启动时。的确有人在先前被拒后，又因匹配度高而收到企业的主动邀约。但从外部视角，你很难判断具体概率。无论如何，对此类话术过度解读并无意义；若未来你看到该公司有心仪职位开放，请务必主动投递——切勿假设他们会主动联系你。

<strong>5. Can I ask for more pay in lieu of benefits?</strong>

<strong>5. 我能否要求以更高薪资替代部分福利待遇？</strong>

<blockquote><p>When I was looking for my first job out of college, a mentor suggested I negotiate for higher pay since I was under 26 and could still be on my parents’ health insurance. I actually did need health insurance, and in fact it was the primary reason I was looking for a job, so I did not take this advice, but I’ve always had it in the back of my head.</p></blockquote>

<blockquote><p>大学毕业后初入职场求职时，一位导师曾建议我争取更高薪资——因我当时未满26岁，仍可享受父母的医疗保险。但事实上，我本人亟需医保，而这恰恰是我求职的首要动因，因此并未采纳该建议；不过，这个想法始终在我脑海中挥之不去。</p></blockquote>

<blockquote><p>I got married in the past year and am now on my spouse’s (far superior) benefits. I’m wondering if it would be wildly out of touch to try to negotiate a raise in lieu of benefits now, or if that’s something that can only be done when starting a job. (Or is it even something one can reasonably do when starting a job?)</p></blockquote>

<blockquote><p>过去一年我已结婚，目前加入配偶（保障远优于公司方案）的保险计划。我正犹豫：此时尝试协商以加薪替代福利，是否显得极不合时宜？抑或此类谈判仅适用于入职阶段？（又或者，即便在入职时，这本身是否就是一项合理可行的诉求？）</p></blockquote>

<blockquote><p>Factors to consider: This is a relatively small company (fewer than 50 people). Our industry is having a tough time, largely due to current administration issues. Raises last year were paltry, though honestly I was surprised to be getting one at all. I’ve never negotiated salary before. Our raises are calculated at one (fairly arbitrary) point in the year, and they’ve always been presented as, “This is what you’re getting this year.” Maybe some people negotiate within that, but I never have felt like that was an option. I’m a high performer and fairly senior, and have been here for more than five years.</p></blockquote>

<blockquote><p>需考量的因素包括：这是一家规模较小的公司（员工不足50人）；受当前政策环境影响，我们整个行业正面临严峻挑战；去年的调薪幅度微乎其微，坦白说，我能拿到任何涨幅都已属意外之喜。我此前从未进行过薪资谈判；公司每年仅在某个（相当随意的）时间节点统一核算调薪，并一贯以“今年你的涨幅即为此数”方式直接告知。或许有人会在该框架内协商，但我从未觉得这对我是可行选项。作为高绩效员工及资深成员，我已在此服务五年以上。</p></blockquote>

You can ask! Some companies will do this and some won’t. Typically if they do, it’s done as a separate line item in your benefits, not just added to your salary (because if your situation changes in the future and you do need to start using their insurance, they don’t want you to feel like you’re getting a pay cut), so you wouldn’t frame it as a raise — just something like, “Would the company consider offering a stipend or credit for not using the company health insurance, since that saves us money?”

你可以提出！部分公司愿意接受此类安排，部分则不会。通常情况下，若公司同意，会将其列为福利体系中的独立条目，而非直接计入基本薪资（原因在于：若未来你的情况发生变化、需启用公司医保，公司不希望你产生“薪资被削减”的错觉）。因此，你不应将其表述为“加薪”，而可委婉提出：“鉴于我不使用公司医疗保险可为公司节省开支，公司是否考虑提供专项津贴或福利抵扣额度？”
