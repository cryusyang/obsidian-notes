<h3>The boring method that got me paid in bug bounty (while others chased hype)</h3><p>Most bug hunters jump from one program to another.</p><p>I did the opposite.</p><p>For months, I stayed on just one target.</p><p>No fancy recon.<br />No endless tool stack.<br />Just one application… and patience.</p><h3>The mistake most hunters make</h3><p>Every time someone posts a big bounty or a critical bug, there’s a wave of people rushing into that program.</p><p>I’ve done it too.</p><p>But what you don’t see is the work behind those findings — hours (sometimes months) spent understanding how that application actually behaves.</p><p>Not scanning it.<br />Not fuzzing it blindly.<br />Understanding it.</p><h3>What I did differently</h3><p>I picked a single program and stayed with it.</p><ul><li>No jumping between targets</li><li>No chasing hype</li><li>No complex toolchains</li></ul><p>I used <strong>Burp Suite with InQL</strong> — and that’s it.</p><p>No recon phase.<br />No automation overload.</p><p>Just manually exploring the application like a real user.</p><h3>The boring phase (that everyone avoids)</h3><p>At the start, it was painful.</p><p>Long GraphQL mutations.<br />Confusing flows.<br />Nothing interesting.</p><p>Most people quit here.</p><p>I didn’t.</p><p>After my 9–6 job, I’d still sit at night and go through the same functionality again and again — trying to understand <em>why</em> things worked the way they did.</p><p>Not just <em>what</em> worked.</p><h3>The shift</h3><p>After a few weeks, something changed.</p><p>The same endpoints that looked confusing earlier… started making sense.</p><p>Patterns became visible.<br />Assumptions became obvious.<br />Trust boundaries started breaking.</p><p>That’s when things clicked.</p><h3>The results</h3><p>I started finding:</p><ul><li>IDOR vulnerabilities</li><li>Broken Access Control issues</li><li>Business logic flaws</li></ul><p>Some were accepted.<br />Some were marked as N/A (valid from their business perspective).<br />Several were rewarded.</p><p>But the biggest takeaway wasn’t the payouts.</p><p>It was realizing this:</p><blockquote><em>I had tested some of these functionalities before… but I wasn’t seeing them properly.</em></blockquote><p>After taking a short break and coming back, I found issues in the same areas I had already explored.</p><p>That’s not luck.</p><p>That’s depth.</p><p>If you look at my payout timeline, one thing becomes obvious — this wasn’t a one-time hit.</p><p>The rewards are spread across months.</p><p>There are gaps. Sometimes weeks with nothing. Then suddenly, multiple findings.</p><p>That’s how this actually works.</p><p>It’s not about getting lucky in a day or two. It’s about staying long enough on a target that you start seeing what others miss.</p><p>Most people leave too early.</p><p>That’s why they never reach this phase.</p><figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/1024/1*NgpolCBrsUzLe12ke1sWig.png" /></figure><h3>What actually works</h3><p>If you’re serious about finding high-impact bugs:</p><ul><li>Stop jumping between programs</li><li>Pick one target and go deep</li><li>Think like a user, not a scanner</li><li>Revisit the same functionality multiple times</li><li>Take breaks — they reset your perspective</li></ul><h3>Final thought</h3><p>Tools help.</p><p>But understanding the application is what gets you criticals.</p><p>Most people won’t do this because it’s slow and boring.</p><p>That’s exactly why it works.</p><p>Thanks for reading.</p><img alt="" height="1" src="https://medium.com/_/stat?event=post.clientViewed&amp;referrerSource=full_rss&amp;postId=3581d3e32e38" width="1" />
