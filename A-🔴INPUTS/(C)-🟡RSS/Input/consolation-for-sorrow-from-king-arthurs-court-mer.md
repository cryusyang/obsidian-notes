> “Learn why the world wags and what wags it. That is the only thing which the mind can never exhaust, never alienate, never be tortured by, never fear or distrust, and never dream of regretting.”

> “弄懂世界为何运转，以及是什么在驱动它运转——这乃是心灵永不枯竭、永不疏离、永不受其折磨、永不畏惧或怀疑、也绝不会追悔莫及的唯一事物。”

![Consolation for Sorrow from King Arthur’s Court: Merlyn’s Advice on What to Do When the World Gets You Down](https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/TheOnceAndFutureKing_white.jpg?fit=320%2C516&ssl=1)

In his wonderful contribution to *[A Velocity of Being: Letters to a Young Reader](https://www.themarginalian.org/2018/11/20/a-velocity-of-being-letters-to-a-young-reader/)*, Yo-Yo Ma tells children about how books helped him survive his own childhood, listing King Arthur among his three great heroes; as a young boy born in France to Chinese parents, trying to find his mooring as an immigrant in America, he reaped great consolation and inspiration from the tales of the legendary medieval leader — stories of “adventure, heroism, human frailty and accidental destiny” that emboldened him to believe in the power of the quest for holy grails and improbable dreams — dreams as improbable as a small boy with no homeland growing up to be the world’s greatest cellist.

在他为《[一种存在之速度：致年轻读者的信](https://www.themarginalian.org/2018/11/20/a-velocity-of-being-letters-to-a-young-reader/)》所撰写的精彩篇章中，马友友向孩子们讲述书籍如何助他度过自己的童年岁月，并将亚瑟王列为他三大英雄之一；这位出生于法国、父母是华人的少年，初抵美国时身为移民苦苦寻找自我定位，却从这位传奇中世纪领袖的故事中获得了巨大的慰藉与启迪——那些关于“冒险、英雄主义、人类脆弱性与偶然命运”的故事，赋予他信念：追寻圣杯与不可能的梦想确有力量——而这样的梦想，其不可思议的程度，不亚于一个无根无籍的小男孩最终成长为世界最伟大的大提琴家。

And, indeed, buried inside the adventure-thrill of these Arthurian tales are treasure troves of wisdom on fortitude, courage, and the art of honorable living, nowhere richer than in the novels by **T.H. White** (May 29, 1906–January 17, 1964), one particular passage in which offers a meta-testament to the potency of reading in the character-formation of King Arthur himself.

事实上，这些亚瑟王传奇所裹挟的冒险快感之下，深藏着关于坚韧、勇气与高尚处世之道的智慧宝藏；其中尤以**T.H.怀特**（1906年5月29日–1964年1月17日）的小说最为丰饶——他笔下某一段文字，更堪称对阅读之力塑造亚瑟王人格的一则元见证。

![Art by Cindy Derby from *A Velocity of Being: Letters to a Young Reader*. Available as a print, benefitting the New York public library system.](https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/12/CindyDerby.jpg?resize=680%2C918&ssl=1)

In White’s 1958 Arthurian classic *[The Once and Future King](https://www.amazon.com/Once-Future-King-T-White/dp/0441627404/?tag=braipick-20)* (*[public library](https://www.worldcat.org/title/once-and-future-king/oclc/1037236469&referer=brief_results)*) — one of Ursula K. Le Guin’s lifelong favorite books — the mystic-magician Merlyn, aware of the young not-yet-king Arthur’s destiny, endeavors to sculpt the boy’s moral fiber and to teach him what it means to be a strong, kindly leader through a series of lessons from the animal kingdom, transforming him by turns into a fish, a hawk, an ant, a goose, and a badger. One day, the young Arthur comes to Merlyn in his ordinary human incarnation, sulking with an ordinary human disappointment — that small, merciless mallet for our fragility. Merlyn offers his advice on the mightiest antidote to disappointment and sorrow:

在怀特1958年的亚瑟王经典巨著《[永恒之王](https://www.amazon.com/Once-Future-King-T-White/dp/0441627404/?tag=braipick-20)》（[公共图书馆藏本](https://www.worldcat.org/title/once-and-future-king/oclc/1037236469&referer=brief_results)）——亦是厄休拉·K·勒古恩毕生挚爱的书籍之一——中，通晓玄秘的魔法师梅林，早已洞悉少年亚瑟（尚未成王）的命运，遂致力于雕琢他的道德品格，借动物王国的一系列教诲，向他阐释何谓强大而仁慈的领袖：他先后将亚瑟化身为鱼、鹰、蚁、鹅与獾。某日，年轻的亚瑟以寻常人类之躯来到梅林面前，因一次寻常的人类失望而闷闷不乐——那柄微小却冷酷无情的锤子，专敲打我们脆弱的内心。梅林于是道出对抗失望与悲恸最强大的解药：

> The best thing for being sad… is to learn something. That is the only thing that never fails. You may grow old and trembling in your anatomies, you may lie awake at night listening to the disorder of your veins, you may miss your only love, you may see the world about you devastated by evil lunatics, or know your honor trampled in the sewers of baser minds. There is only one thing for it then — to learn. Learn why the world wags and what wags it. That is the only thing which the mind can never exhaust, never alienate, never be tortured by, never fear or distrust, and never dream of regretting.

> 消解悲伤……最好的办法，便是去学习某样东西。这是唯一永不失效的良方。你或许会衰老颤抖于血肉之躯，或许会彻夜难眠，耳畔只闻血脉紊乱之声；或许会痛失此生挚爱，或许目睹周遭世界被邪恶疯子摧残殆尽，又或许眼见自身荣光被卑劣心智的阴沟污水所践踏。此时，唯有一法可解——学习。弄懂世界为何运转，以及是什么在驱动它运转。这乃是心灵永不枯竭、永不疏离、永不受其折磨、永不畏惧或怀疑、也绝不会追悔莫及的唯一事物。

In a sentiment evocative of trailblazing astronomer Maria Mitchell’s observation that “[we have a hunger of the mind which asks for knowledge of all around us, and the more we gain, the more is our desire,](https://www.themarginalian.org/2017/06/05/maria-mitchell-science-religion/)” Merlyn adds:

怀揣着先驱天文学家玛丽亚·米切尔那句名言所唤起的共鸣——“我们心灵深处怀有一种饥渴，渴求知晓周遭一切；而我们所得愈多，渴求便愈甚”——梅林进一步说道：

> Look at what a lot of things there are to learn — pure science, the only purity there is. You can learn astronomy in a lifetime, natural history in three, literature in six. And then, after you have exhausted a million lifetimes in biology and medicine and theocriticism and geography and history and economics — why, you can start to make a cartwheel out of the appropriate wood, or spend fifty years learning to begin to learn to beat your adversary at fencing. After that you can start again on mathematics, until it is time to learn to plough.

> 且看这世间有多少学问可学——纯粹科学，乃唯一之纯粹。你可用一生习得天文学，用三生掌握自然史，用六世通晓文学。而后，纵使你在生物学、医学、神学批评、地理学、历史学与经济学中耗尽百万生，仍可转而学习如何择木制轮；或倾五十年光阴，只为初窥击剑之道，继而习得克敌之术。再之后，你又能重拾数学，直至该学耕田之时。

Complement with Rebecca Solnit — a modern-day magician of storytelling — on [how books solace, empower, and transform us](https://www.themarginalian.org/2019/01/03/a-velocity-of-being-rebecca-solnit/), philosopher Martha Nussbaum on [how to live with our human fragility](https://www.themarginalian.org/2014/03/14/martha-nussbaum-bill-moyers-world-of-ideas/), and poet Mary Oliver on [the greatest antidote to sorrow](https://www.themarginalian.org/2016/11/02/mary-oliver-upstream-staying-alive-reading/), then revisit Bruce Lee’s [philosophy of learning](https://www.themarginalian.org/2016/08/26/bruce-lee-learning/), Lewis Carroll’s [four rules of learning](https://www.themarginalian.org/2014/06/13/how-to-learn-lewis-carroll/), and Albert Einstein’s advice to his own young son on [the secret to learning anything](https://www.themarginalian.org/2013/06/14/einstein-letter-to-son/).

还可参阅当代叙事魔法师丽贝卡·索尔尼特论[书籍如何抚慰、赋能并转化我们](https://www.themarginalian.org/2019/01/03/a-velocity-of-being-rebecca-solnit/)，哲学家玛莎·努斯鲍姆论[如何与人类的脆弱共处](https://www.themarginalian.org/2014/03/14/martha-nussbaum-bill-moyers-world-of-ideas/)，以及诗人玛丽·奥利弗论[对抗悲伤最伟大的解药](https://www.themarginalian.org/2016/11/02/mary-oliver-upstream-staying-alive-reading/)；继而重温李小龙的[学习哲学](https://www.themarginalian.org/2016/08/26/bruce-lee-learning/)、刘易斯·卡罗尔的[学习四法则](https://www.themarginalian.org/2014/06/13/how-to-learn-lewis-carroll/)，以及阿尔伯特·爱因斯坦致幼子的信中所揭示的[掌握任何事物的秘密](https://www.themarginalian.org/2013/06/14/einstein-letter-to-son/)。
