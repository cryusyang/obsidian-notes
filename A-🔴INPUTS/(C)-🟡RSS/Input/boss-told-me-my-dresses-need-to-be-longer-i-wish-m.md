It’s five answers to five questions. Here we go…

这是五个问题的五个回答。我们开始吧……

<strong>1. My boss told me my dresses need to be longer</strong>

<strong>1. 我的老板告诉我，我的裙子需要更长一些</strong>

<blockquote><p>My line manager told me yesterday that there had been &#8220;comments made” about how short / inappropriate my dresses are in the office, as a member of the team who is front-facing for clients. I was asked to not wear these outfits in the office any longer.</p>
<p>These comments have utterly humiliated me, and I spent about an hour crying on my way home. I have always dressed fairly modestly at work and am deeply uncomfortable with my body being perceived as being &#8220;on display.&#8221; My dresses are long-sleeved, with skirts that stop just above my knee. They are conventional office wear. I prefer dresses and skirts over trousers, as the medications I take have made my stomach quite bloated, and I find tight waistbands uncomfortable. But I was told they need to be longer as I&#8217;m greeting clients (as my work wear was just above the knee already, my presumption is that longer means to the knee or below).</p>
<p>It&#8217;s a very male heavy office, so what other women wear is split between trousers and skirts that are above the knee or to the knee. My line manager was wearing an above-the-knee dress the day after telling me my outfits were too short.</p>
<p>After looking at my work wardrobe, I estimate I&#8217;m going to have to get replace nearly 80% to get to these new standards &#8212; of dresses and skirts to the knee or lower — while also managing the other restrictions that are placed on women&#8217;s wear at our office. For instance, I&#8217;m not allowed to wear a sleeveless blouse because our male directors decided they are not professional for women. This is while the men in our office can meet with clients in hoodies or polo shirts.</p>
<p>I simply don&#8217;t understand why my clothing is an issue 16 months into working here. Am I being unreasonable or is this unfair? Is this something I should speak to my union about?</p></blockquote>

<blockquote><p>我的直属经理昨天告诉我，作为面向客户的团队成员，有人对我的裙子“太短/不恰当”提出了“意见”。我被要求今后不得再在办公室穿着这类服装。</p>
<p>这些评论让我深感羞辱，回家路上我哭了将近一个小时。我在工作中一向穿着相当保守，也极度反感自己的身体被视作“公开展示”。我的裙子都是长袖款，裙摆止于膝盖上方一点，属于常规的职场着装。比起裤子，我更喜欢穿裙子或半身裙——因为我所服用的药物导致腹部明显胀气，而紧身腰带令我非常不适。但经理却告诉我，由于我要接待客户，裙子必须更长（既然我目前的职场着装已刚好在膝上，我推测所谓“更长”即指及膝或过膝）。</p>
<p>我们办公室男性员工占绝大多数，其他女性同事的着装则分为两类：穿裤子，或穿膝上裙/及膝裙。而在我被告知着装“过短”的次日，我的直属经理自己就穿着一条膝上裙来上班了。</p>
<p>我翻看了自己的职场衣橱后估算，为达到这些新标准——即所有裙子和半身裙均须及膝或更长——我将不得不更换近80%的现有衣物；与此同时，我还需应对办公室施加于女性着装的其他诸多限制。例如，我不能穿无袖上衣，因为公司男董事们认定这对女性而言“不够专业”；而办公室里的男性员工却可以穿着连帽衫或Polo衫去见客户。</p>
<p>我完全无法理解，为何入职16个月后，我的着装突然成了问题？是我太敏感、不合理，还是这本就不公平？我是否该就此向工会寻求帮助？</p></blockquote>

Yes, you should absolutely speak to your union. Something here doesn’t make sense — skirts and dresses to just above the knee aren’t unprofessional or inappropriate work wear, and that goes triple in an office where a bunch of other people are wearing them, including the manager who told you that you couldn’t. “You’re client-facing” doesn’t make sense as an explanation. Is there anything else that could explain why you're getting this feedback and others aren't? Sometimes this happens when you’re the only one in your office with a <a href="https://www.askamanager.org/2013/05/my-boss-wants-to-ban-skirts-leggings-and-v-necks-for-curvier-women-but-not-for-others.html">particular body shape</a> (which doesn’t make it okay), and I wonder if that’s in play here.

是的，你绝对应该联系工会。此事明显不合常理——裙摆仅略高于膝盖的裙子和连衣裙，并非不专业或不合适的职场着装；尤其当办公室里还有许多人（包括那位告诉你“不能这么穿”的经理本人）正穿着同类款式时，这种说法就更站不住脚。“你负责对接客户”根本不足以成为合理解释。是否还有其他因素，能说明为何唯独你收到此类反馈，而他人没有？有时这种情况会发生在你是办公室里唯一拥有某种特定体型的人身上（参见<a href="https://www.askamanager.org/2013/05/my-boss-wants-to-ban-skirts-leggings-and-v-necks-for-curvier-women-but-not-for-others.html">此处链接</a>），尽管这绝不代表其做法正当，但我怀疑这或许正是当前状况的成因。

Ideally you’d go back to your manager and ask for clarification — including explicitly asking if she is telling you that your skirts must be below the knee, and pointing out that all your skirts are currently the same length as the ones you see other women in your office wearing. But since you have a union, pull them in for advice too.

理想情况下，你应回到经理处寻求明确澄清——包括直接询问她是否确指你的裙子必须“低于膝盖”，并指出你目前所有裙子的长度，与你在办公室所见其他女性同事穿着的完全一致。但既然你已有工会支持，也务必请他们介入提供建议。

<strong>2. CEO sends a delusional AI-generated image of himself with every email</strong>

<strong>2. 首席执行官每封邮件都附上一张由AI生成的、脱离现实的自画像</strong>

<blockquote><p>As a mere lower-level staffer, I am certain there is nothing I can do about this issue, but perhaps you have some ideas.</p>
<p>The CEO has begun to attach an AI-generated image of himself to every email he sends out. The images are universally more handsome than the real thing. No more receding hairline, or stomach fat. Plenty of bicep muscles. Not a wrinkle in sight.</p>
<p>This is cringe behaviour, and staff mock him for it behind his back. While I am not personally invested in helping him save face, I do want to stop being forced to look at these unprofessional, inaccurate portraits. Especially since the workplace is a public library, where one would hope to avoid misinformation.</p></blockquote>

<blockquote><p>作为一名普通基层职员，我确信自己对此事无能为力，但或许您有些想法？</p>
<p>首席执行官已开始在每一封发出的邮件中附上一张AI生成的自画像。这些图像无一例外都比本人英俊得多：发际线不再后移，腹部不再有赘肉，肱二头肌饱满发达，脸上不见一丝皱纹。</p>
<p>此举令人尴尬至极，员工们纷纷在背后嘲笑他。虽然我个人并不在意帮他保全面子，但我确实希望停止被迫观看这些不专业、失实的肖像照——尤其考虑到我们的工作场所是一家公共图书馆，在此更应避免传播错误信息。</p></blockquote>

No, this is amazing and you must not try to stop it!

不，这简直太棒了，你万万不可试图阻止它！

And that’s fortunate, because there’s almost certainly nothing you can do about it anyway. If you were, say, a senior communications staffer or his right-hand person or otherwise a trusted confidant, you could attempt to diplomatically address it, but assuming you are none of those things and therefore have no real standing or obligation to take this on, the only correct response is to sit back and bask in the utter absurdity of it.

幸运的是，反正你也几乎什么都做不了。倘若你是资深公关人员、他的得力助手，或他信任的密友，或许尚可尝试以外交方式委婉沟通；但假设你并非上述任何一类人，因而既无实际话语权，也无真正责任去处理此事，那么唯一正确的反应便是静观其变，尽情沉浸于这场荒诞大戏之中。

Is it a problem for his credibility? Yes! Is it <em>your</em> problem? No.

这损害他的公信力吗？是的！但这是否是<em>你的</em>问题？不是。

You can just enjoy the spectacle.

你只需欣然观赏这场奇观即可。

<strong>3. I wish my board would just fire me already</strong>

<strong>3. 我只盼董事会干脆解雇我算了</strong>

<blockquote><p>I am the chief executive of a small nonprofit and I report to a board, and I have been on a performance improvement plan (PIP) for the past four months. The PIP is full of things that are untrue and half true, along with some things that could legitimately be improved. The PIP was my first notice of any of those gripes that the board (or rather, a few members of the executive committee) had about me, my work, and, more pointedly, my personality.</p>
<p>The first PIP was supposed to be 60 days. They had no objective success measure in it and missed over half the weekly check-ins we had scheduled. They are having a lawyer handle everything for them, so I didn&#8217;t receive a determination about the PIP until a couple weeks ago when they gave me another PIP with a 60-day extension. This document, even more than the first one, has things in it explaining where I am failing to meet expectations that I was unaware of and were not part of the job previously.</p>
<p>At this point, it is clear that at least two of the board members just don&#8217;t like me and want to fire me, which is completely within their power to do. I have sincerely done what I can to meet their expectations, but I can&#8217;t and won&#8217;t change my personality or pretend to be someone I am not. And this job has turned into something different than what I was hired for.</p>
<p>I have been looking for a new job since the process started, but it is not easy at this level and I can&#8217;t afford to be without an income or I would have quit already. How do I have the conversation with them expressing my desire to leave along with my need to be eligible for unemployment benefits?</p></blockquote>

<blockquote><p>我是某小型非营利组织的首席执行官，向董事会汇报工作；过去四个月，我一直处于绩效改进计划（PIP）中。该PIP内容充斥着不实与半真半假之词，夹杂着一些确实有待提升之处。而这份PIP，竟是我首次得知董事会（或更准确地说，是执行委员会中的几位成员）对我本人、我的工作，乃至更尖锐地——对我的性格，抱有诸般不满。</p>
<p>首份PIP原定为期60天，其中却未设任何客观的达标衡量标准，且漏掉了我们原定每周例会的一半以上。他们已聘请律师全权代理一切事务，因此直到几周前，我才收到关于首份PIP的最终决定——结果却是又一份延长60天的新PIP。这份文件甚至比第一份更甚：其中列出的“未达预期之处”，不仅此前我全然不知，更从未被列为该岗位的原有职责。</p>
<p>事已至此，至少两位董事会成员纯粹出于个人好恶而欲将我解雇，已是显而易见；而他们的确拥有完全合法的权力这么做。我已真诚竭尽所能满足他们的期望，但我既无法、也绝不打算改变自己的性格，或伪装成另一个自己。此外，这份工作早已异化为与我当初受聘时截然不同的模样。</p>
<p>自PIP启动以来，我一直在积极求职，但这一层级的职位本就难觅，而我又无力承受失业断收之困——否则我早已主动辞职。那么，我该如何与他们沟通，既表达离开意愿，又确保自己有资格申领失业救济金？</p></blockquote>

Frame it this way: “It’s clear to me that you’re unhappy with my work and I want to be realistic about my chances for success here and not drag out the process, so I’d like to propose a managed separation with a transition that will be as smooth as possible for both of us. I’d ask that you not contest my unemployment benefits since it sounds like I was likely to be let go at the end of this process anyway, but beyond that I’m flexible about what this could look like in terms of timing and messaging.” They are likely to hear this with relief.

请如此措辞：“我清楚地意识到，各位对我的工作表现并不满意；为务实起见，我也希望坦诚评估自己在此继续成功的可能性，而不让这一过程无谓拖延。因此，我想提议双方协商达成有序离职，并确保过渡期对彼此都尽可能平稳顺畅。我恳请各位不要对我的失业救济金申请提出异议——毕竟，按当前态势，我最终被解雇似乎已成定局；至于具体时间安排与对外口径等细节，我持开放态度，愿积极配合。” 他们听到这番话，很可能会如释重负。

You might also consider whether you have an argument to request <a href="https://www.askamanager.org/2019/08/severance-pay-who-gets-it-and-how-it-works.html">severance</a>, if they’re now defining success in the role differently than what you were brought on to do.

你还可以进一步考虑：若他们如今对岗位“成功”的定义，已与当初聘用你时的标准大相径庭，你是否具备主张获得遣散费的合理依据？（参见<a href="https://www.askamanager.org/2019/08/severance-pay-who-gets-it-and-how-it-works.html">此处链接</a>）

<strong>4. Requiring 15+ hours of outside reading per week</strong>

<strong>4. 要求每周额外阅读15小时以上</strong>

<blockquote><p>I am curious to your take on a job listing I recently came across. There is an indie bookshop in my city that is looking for booksellers &#8212; basically part-time retail work, $12/hour starting wage, nothing atypical for the area.</p>
<p>Amongst the qualifications and job duties listed, alongside needing 3+ years experience as a bookseller and &#8220;associates or better&#8221; degree, I noticed something that seemed super wild. &#8220;Booksellers are required to spend an additional 15+ hours a week reading recent releases and bestsellers to stay up to date on merchandise and better assist customers.&#8221;</p>
<p>(I am assuming the 15+ hours of reading homework is unpaid, but I could be wrong; this is a very hipster bookshop that I like to visit now and then but would never work at personally, so I haven&#8217;t inquired further or anything.)</p>
<p>Is this as bonkers as it sounds to me? Or does this sound more like &#8220;continuing education&#8221; and is pretty reasonable to expect?</p></blockquote>

<blockquote><p>我对最近看到的一则招聘启事颇感好奇。本市一家独立书店正在招聘图书销售员——本质上属兼职零售岗位，起薪每小时12美元，薪资水平在当地并无异常。</p>
<p>在所列任职资格与岗位职责中，除要求应聘者具备三年以上图书销售经验及“副学士或更高学位”外，我还注意到一项看似极其离谱的要求：“图书销售员须每周额外投入15小时以上，阅读最新出版物与畅销书，以及时掌握商品信息，从而更好地服务顾客。”</p>
<p>（我推测这15小时以上的“阅读作业”属无偿性质，但也可能猜错；这家书店风格极为小众，我偶尔会去逛逛，但绝不会考虑入职，因此并未进一步询问核实。）</p>
<p>这听起来是否真的如此荒谬？抑或这更接近于“持续进修”，因而其实颇为合理？</p></blockquote>

As a general rule, if outside reading is required for non-exempt employees, they need to be paid for that time. There are exceptions for things like continuing education required to maintain a license, but booksellers aren’t licensed.

一般而言，若雇主强制要求非豁免雇员进行工作之外的阅读，则必须为此类时间支付薪酬。虽有例外情形（例如为维持执业许可而必需的持续教育），但图书销售员并不属于持证上岗职业。

They’d be better off saying that they’re looking for employees who already maintain a deep knowledge of recent releases and bestsellers and who will maintain that knowledge going forward — and then screening for dedicated readers <i>of recent releases</i> (which is different from just being a voracious reader in general) in their interview process — instead of presenting it like a job duty with a specific number of hours attached.

他们更明智的做法是：在招聘中说明“我们希望寻找那些已对近期出版物与畅销书拥有深厚了解、并将在未来持续保持这一知识储备的员工”，继而在面试环节重点考察应聘者是否真正专注于阅读<i>近期出版物</i>（这与泛泛而谈的“阅读爱好者”截然不同）——而非将其包装成一项附带明确工时要求的岗位职责。

<strong>5. I was fired from my last job, then didn’t work for several years — how do I explain it in interviews?</strong>

<strong>5. 我上一份工作被解雇，之后数年未就业——面试时该如何解释？</strong>

<blockquote><p>I was fired from my job several years ago. Due to a combination of burnout and undiagnosed depression, I effectively went AWOL and didn&#8217;t do anything about anything until it was too late, and I&#8217;m trying to re-enter the job market now. I have a resume gap of several years, my previous job loss was entirely my fault, and it&#8217;s been a very long time since I had to do any kind of job searching.</p>
<p>How do I write a resume to cover this particular ground? And, in the event of an interview, any advice on how to answer the inevitable question of what I was doing while unemployed? (The honest answer is &#8220;nothing, while trying to claw my way out of a mental health hole.”)</p></blockquote>

<blockquote><p>几年前我遭上一家公司解雇。当时身心俱疲，又尚未确诊抑郁症，我实质上进入了“失踪”状态，对一切事务置之不理，直至为时已晚。如今，我正努力重返职场。我的简历存在数年空白期，上次离职完全由我个人原因导致，而距离我上次求职，也已过去太久太久。</p>
<p>我该如何撰写简历来应对这一特殊情况？若进入面试环节，对于那个不可避免的问题——“失业期间您在做什么？”——又该如何作答？（如实回答是：“什么也没做，只是在努力挣扎着爬出心理健康的深渊。”）</p></blockquote>

You don’t address it on a resume at all; that’s the place to highlight your work history and accomplishments. In an interview, the language you want is: “I’ve been dealing with a health issue that is now resolved and I’m excited to return to work.” You don’t need to say more than that; they’re not supposed to ask for details, and it explains why you left the last job as well as what you’ve been doing since then.

简历上完全无需提及此事；简历的作用，本就是凸显你的职业履历与工作成就。而在面试中，你只需这样表述：“我此前经历了一段健康问题，现已痊愈，非常期待重返工作岗位。” 你不必多言，对方本也不应追问细节；这句话既解释了你上一份工作的离职原因，也说明了你失业期间的状态。
