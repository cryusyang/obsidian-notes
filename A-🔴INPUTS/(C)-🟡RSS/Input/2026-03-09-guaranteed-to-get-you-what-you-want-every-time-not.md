<p style="font-weight: 400;">If you read this post, it’s guaranteed you’ll get everything you want every time, all the time.</p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">Of course, that’s not true.</p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">But if you spend any time online, you’ll quickly discover there are plenty of people willing to imply exactly that. Read their book. Listen to their podcast. Follow their formula. Use their script. Suddenly life becomes one long series of “yes” responses.</p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">People love the idea of an easy way out. Unfortunately, persuasion doesn’t work that way.</p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">Understanding the science of influence is <strong>no guarantee</strong> you’ll always get what you want—just like understanding how to live a healthy lifestyle doesn’t guarantee you’ll never get sick. But just as healthy habits dramatically improve your chances of living longer with a better quality of life, learning the science of ethical influence increases the odds that people will say “yes” to you sooner and more often.</p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">Nothing is guaranteed, but smart people tend to follow what science shows actually works.</p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">That reality came to mind after I wrote about an experience with Southwest Airlines where I did not get the outcome I wanted. A reader emailed me with this comment:</p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">“I thought you were an expert on persuasion? How come you couldn’t use your ability, knowledge, and experience to persuade the airline otherwise? Not a very good advert for your talents, I’d suggest.”</p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">I replied, and in his follow-up message he apologized, saying his comment was meant to be tongue-in-cheek. We ended up having a good exchange. But it did get me thinking about something important: failure.</p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">Even people who understand influence sometimes fail to persuade.</p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">And that’s not necessarily a bad thing.</p>
<h3>Why Failure Can Strengthen Your Influence</h3>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">One of the principles of persuasion is <strong>authority</strong>, and authority rests on two key elements: expertise and credibility.</p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">You need expertise to be seen as knowledgeable. But credibility often comes from something surprising: <strong>honesty about limitations</strong>.</p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">Research shows that when people admit weaknesses or acknowledge when something didn’t work, their credibility can actually increase. Why? Because honesty builds trust.</p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">In other words, people tend to trust experts who are realistic more than those who claim perfection.</p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">That’s why it’s worth stating plainly: <strong>No one gets what they want all the time.</strong></p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">If someone tells you they do or you can… be skeptical!</p>
<h3>Even the Best Fall Short Sometimes</h3>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">Consider Dr. Robert Cialdini, author of <em>Influence</em> and the most cited living social psychologist in the world on the topic of persuasion.</p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">On one of his audio programs, he shares a story about failing to persuade someone at his gym. He had accidentally locked his keys in his car and simply asked the person folding towels if he could briefly use the phone.</p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">A small request. A reasonable request. And yet, on that particular day, the answer was no.</p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">If the world’s leading authority on persuasion sometimes fails to persuade someone folding towels at a gym, then the rest of us shouldn’t be surprised when we occasionally hear “no” as well.</p>
<h3>What the Science Actually Promises</h3>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">Here’s what the science of influence <strong>does</strong> promise:</p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">If you understand the principles and apply them ethically and correctly, you will become more persuasive than the average communicator.</p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">Not perfect. Not unstoppable. But more effective.</p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">Think about it this way. Two people can go to the same doctor, trainer, or coach and follow the exact same advice. Their results may differ. But it’s still far better to follow proven guidance than to ignore it.</p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">The science of influence is built on nine decades of research, not someone’s clever marketing pitch. When you understand the principles and apply them thoughtfully, you dramatically increase the likelihood that people will respond positively.</p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">And over time, those small advantages add up.</p>
<h3>Turning Failure into Value</h3>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">As for my Southwest Airlines experience, I didn’t get what I wanted. But I still got something valuable from it.</p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">In fact, I got several blog posts, a few good lessons, and a nice conversation with a reader who challenged me. Sometimes the situations that don’t go our way turn out to be the most useful ones for learning and reflection.</p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">So, if you’re studying persuasion and hoping it will guarantee success every time, let me save you some disappointment. It won’t.</p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">But if you apply the science ethically and consistently, you’ll find yourself hearing “yes” far more often than someone relying on guesswork alone. After all, how much better off would you be if people said “yes” to you 20% more? How about 30%? What if people said “yes” 40% more than they do now? I think that would make a dramatic difference for your career and personal life.</p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">And that’s a result worth pursuing!</p>

## 中文译文

如果你读了这篇文章，就能保证每次、永远都能得到你想要的一切。

当然，这并不属实。

但只要你花点时间上网浏览，很快就会发现，网上大把的人乐于暗示你：事情就是这么简单。读他们的书，听他们的播客，照搬他们的套路，套用他们的脚本——顷刻间，生活就变成了一连串响亮的“同意”。

人们总是热衷于寻找捷径。遗憾的是，说服这件事偏偏没有捷径可走。

掌握影响力的科学原理，并不能确保你每次都能如愿以偿；就像懂得如何健康生活，并不等于你永远不会生病。但正如坚持健康习惯能显著提升你的预期寿命与生活质量一样，学习合乎伦理的影响力科学，也能大幅提高他人更快、更频繁对你说“是”的概率。

没有任何事是百分之百有保障的，但聪明人往往选择相信并践行那些被科学研究证实真正有效的方法。

这个想法在我写完一篇关于西南航空（Southwest Airlines）的经历后浮上心头——那次，我恰恰没能达成自己期望的结果。一位读者随后给我发来邮件，留言写道：

“我还以为您是说服领域的专家呢？那为什么您没能运用自己的能力、知识和经验，成功说服航空公司改变决定？恕我直言，这似乎不太能体现您的专业水准。”

我回信作了回应。他在后续邮件中向我致歉，坦言自己原意只是半开玩笑。我们最终展开了一场愉快而深入的交流。但这件事确实让我开始认真思考一个关键问题：失败。

即便是深谙影响力之道的人，有时也会在说服他人时遭遇失败。

而这未必是件坏事。

为何失败反而可能增强你的影响力？

说服原则之一是“权威性”，而权威性建立在两个核心要素之上：专业能力与可信度。

你需要具备专业能力，才能被视为行家；但可信度却常常源于一件出人意料的事：坦诚承认自身的局限。

研究表明，当人们主动承认自身弱点，或坦然指出某项策略并未奏效时，其可信度反而可能上升。原因何在？因为诚实构筑信任。

换言之，人们更愿意信任那些实事求是的专家，而非标榜完美无缺者。

因此，有必要直截了当地指出：没有人能永远心想事成。

若有人声称自己总能如愿，或承诺你也能做到……请务必保持警惕！

就连最杰出的专家，有时也会失手

让我们看看罗伯特·西奥迪尼博士（Dr. Robert Cialdini）——《影响力》（Influence）一书作者，也是全球在说服领域被引用次数最多的在世社会心理学家。

在他的一档音频节目里，他分享了一个自己在健身房说服失败的真实故事：他不小心把车钥匙锁在了车里，于是向一位正在叠毛巾的工作人员提出一个微小而合理的请求——能否借用一下电话？  
结果，就在那一天，对方拒绝了。

如果这位全球公认的说服学泰斗，偶尔都无法说服一位在健身房叠毛巾的普通人，那么我们这些普通人偶尔听到一句“不”，也就完全不必惊讶了。

科学真正承诺的是什么？

影响力科学所承诺的，其实是这样一件事：

只要你理解相关原理，并以合乎伦理且正确的方式加以应用，你将比普通沟通者更具说服力。

不是完美无缺，也非战无不胜，但确实更有效。

不妨这样想：两位患者去看同一位医生，两位学员跟随同一位教练，两人严格遵照完全相同的建议行事，结果仍可能不同。但即便如此，遵循已被验证有效的指导，终究远胜于凭空猜测、盲目行动。

影响力科学植根于长达九十年的严谨研究，而非某位营销高手的巧舌如簧。当你真正理解其原理，并审慎加以运用，他人积极回应你的可能性便会显著提升。

日积月累，这些看似微小的优势终将汇聚成巨大的成效。

将失败转化为价值

至于我在西南航空的那次经历——我确实未能如愿。但我依然从中收获了宝贵的东西。

事实上，我收获了几篇博客文章、数条实用经验，以及一次与那位敢于质疑我的读者之间颇有启发的对话。有时候，那些偏离我们预期的情境，反而成为最富价值的学习契机与反思素材。

因此，如果你正研习说服术，又寄望它能为你带来百分之百的成功保障，那我想提前帮你卸下这份期待——它做不到。

但只要你秉持伦理准则，持之以恒地运用这门科学，你听到“是”的频率，必将远远高于仅靠直觉与猜测行事的人。试想一下：倘若他人对你的请求答应率提升了20%，甚至30%、40%，你的职业生涯与个人生活会发生怎样的巨变？我相信，这种改变将是颠覆性的。

而这，正是值得你全力以赴的目标！
