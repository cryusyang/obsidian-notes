![The Wanting Monster: A Tender Modern Fable about the Difficult Art of Resting at the Still Point of Enough](https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/thewantingmonster_cover.jpg?fit=320%2C415&ssl=1)

Wanting is the menacing margin of error between desire and need. It is the blade that vivisects your serenity, the hammer that shatters your wholeness — to want anything is to deem your life incomplete without it. It is a perpetual motion machine that keeps you restlessly spinning around the still point of enough. “Enough is so vast a sweetness, I suppose it never occurs, only pathetic counterfeits,” Emily Dickinson lamented in [a love letter](https://www.themarginalian.org/2018/12/10/emily-dickinson-love-letters-susan-gilbert/) a century before Kurt Vonnegut, in his [shortest and most poignant poem](https://www.themarginalian.org/2014/01/16/kurt-vonnegut-joe-heller-having-enough/), located the secret of happiness in the sense of enough. Wanting is a story of scarcity writing itself on the scroll of the mind, masquerading as an equation read from the blackboard of reality. That story is the history of the world. But it need not be its future, or yours.

“想要”是欲望与需求之间那道险恶的误差边界。“想要”是一把解剖你内心安宁的利刃，一柄击碎你生命完整性的铁锤——只要“想要”某物，便等于判定自己的人生若无此物便残缺不全。“想要”是一台永动机，令你焦灼地绕着“足够”这一静止支点不停旋转。“足够是如此浩瀚的甘甜，我猜想它从不真正降临，出现的只是可悲的赝品。”艾米莉·狄金森在一个世纪前的一封[情书](https://www.themarginalian.org/2018/12/10/emily-dickinson-love-letters-susan-gilbert/)中如此哀叹；而库尔特·冯内古特则在其[最短却最动人的诗](https://www.themarginalian.org/2014/01/16/kurt-vonnegut-joe-heller-having-enough/)中，将幸福的秘密锚定于“足够”的感受之上。“想要”是在心灵卷轴上自行书写的匮乏叙事，伪装成从现实黑板上抄录下来的一道等式。这个故事，便是世界的历史。但它不必成为它的未来，也不必成为你的未来。

An epoch after [*John J. Plenty and Fiddler Dan*](https://www.themarginalian.org/2023/07/29/john-j-plenty-fiddler-dan/) — John Ciardi’s magnificent 1963 spell against the cult of more — author Martine Murray and artist Anna Read, living parallel lives close to nature in rural Australia, offer a mighty new counter-myth in [***The Wanting Monster***](https://www.amazon.com/Wanting-Monster-Martine-Murray/dp/1592704190/?tag=braipick-20) ([public library](https://search.worldcat.org/title/1467887179)) — an almost unbearably wonderful modern fable about who we would be and what this world would be like if we finally arrived, exhausted and relieved, at the still point of enough. Having always felt that great children’s books are works of philosophy in disguise, speaking great truth in the language of tenderness, I hold this one among my all-time favorites.

继约翰·齐亚迪（John Ciardi）于1963年创作的、对抗“越多越好”狂热崇拜的恢弘寓言《约翰·J·富足与小提琴手丹》（[*John J. Plenty and Fiddler Dan*](https://www.themarginalian.org/2023/07/29/john-j-plenty-fiddler-dan/)）一个时代之后，作家马丁·默里（Martine Murray）与插画家安娜·里德（Anna Read）——两位在澳大利亚乡野中贴近自然、平行生活的人——在[《想要怪兽》（***The Wanting Monster***）](https://www.amazon.com/Wanting-Monster-Martine-Murray/dp/1592704190/?tag=braipick-20)（[公共图书馆版](https://search.worldcat.org/title/1467887179)）中献上一则磅礴崭新的反神话——一则几乎令人难以承受其美好的现代寓言，讲述倘若我们终于精疲力竭而又如释重负地抵达“足够”的静止支点，我们将成为怎样的人，这个世界又将变成怎样的模样。我向来认为，伟大的儿童读物实为披着温柔语言外衣的哲学著作，在柔声细语中诉说至深真理；而这本书，是我毕生挚爱之一。

![ ](https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/thewantingmonster21.jpg?resize=680%2C472&ssl=1)

![ ](https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/thewantingmonster1.jpg?resize=680%2C457&ssl=1)

The story begins in a town so tranquil and content that no one notices the Wanting Monster, who stands sulking on the edge of the scene, part ghost out of a Norse myth, part Sendakian Wild Thing.

故事始于一座宁静而满足的小镇，镇上的人们甚至未曾察觉“想要怪兽”的存在——它正闷闷不乐地伫立于画面边缘，一半是北欧神话中的幽灵，一半是桑达克笔下狂野不羁的生灵。

And so the Wanting Monster stomps over to the next village, “bellowing and crashing about as monsters do,” but still the magpie keeps singing, the bees keep laboring at the flowers, and the children keep playing in the square. The Wanting Monster redoubles the growling and the howling, but not even Billie Ray, “the littlest child of the village,” pays heed.

于是，“想要怪兽”大步踏向下一个村庄，“像所有怪物那样咆哮怒吼、横冲直撞”，但喜鹊依旧欢唱，蜜蜂仍在花间辛勤劳作，孩子们照旧在广场上嬉戏。“想要怪兽”加倍发出低吼与嚎叫，可就连村里“最小的孩子”比莉·蕾（Billie Ray）也毫不理会。

![ ](https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/thewantingmonster22.jpg?resize=680%2C453&ssl=1)

![ ](https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/thewantingmonster3.jpg?resize=680%2C457&ssl=1)

This inflicts no small identity crisis:

这引发了一场不小的认同危机：

> What good was a monster if it couldn’t raise any trouble? If it couldn’t even raise the eyebrow of a small, curly-headed child? The Wanting Monster had its head in shame.

> 若一个怪物无法掀起任何风波，甚至连一个卷发小童的眉毛都挑不动，那它又有何用？“想要怪兽”羞愧地垂下了头。

But then it comes upon Mr. Banks, napping serenely by the stream. With that “terrible compulsion” that turns the insecure monstrous, the Wanting Monster moans its siren growl of want into the sleeping man’s ear.

但随后，它遇见了班克斯先生——他正悠然地在溪畔小憩。怀着那种将不安者扭曲为怪物的“可怕冲动”，“想要怪兽”将它那海妖般蛊惑人心的渴望低吼，呻吟着灌入沉睡者的耳中。

![ ](https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/thewantingmonster2.jpg?resize=680%2C457&ssl=1)

![ ](https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/thewantingmonster5.jpg?w=680&ssl=1)

![ ](https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/thewantingmonster23.jpg?resize=680%2C453&ssl=1)

> Mr. Banks began to wriggle. His heart began to jiggle.  
> A little note of misery sounded in his mind.  
> What could possibly be wrong?  
> It was a perfect day for a snooze by the stream. But now he wanted something else, something more.

> 班克斯先生开始扭动起来，心跳也开始怦怦乱跳。  
> 一丝苦闷的音符在他脑海中响起。  
> 到底出了什么问题？  
> 溪畔小憩本是再完美不过的一天。可此刻，他却渴望别的东西，更多一点的东西。

Suddenly, he wants the stream itself, shimmering so seductively in the sunlight that it has to be had.

突然间，他渴望起那条溪流本身——它在阳光下粼粼闪烁，诱惑得令他非拥有不可。

As soon as Mr. Banks builds a swimming pool at his house and fills it with the stream’s water, Mr. Bishop perches to peek over the fence and begins “to twitch and prickle and hop around” with the restless desire for a pool of his own.

班克斯先生刚在家建起一座游泳池，并灌满溪水，毕晓普先生便立刻攀上篱笆探头张望，随即因对属于自己的泳池那焦躁难耐的渴望而“坐立不安、浑身刺痒、来回蹦跳”。

So goes the cascade of envy, that handmaiden of wanting, until pool by pool the streams begins to run dry.

于是，嫉妒——这“想要”的侍女——便如瀑布般倾泻而下，直至一座座泳池建成，溪流终至干涸。

![ ](https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/thewantingmonster6.jpg?resize=680%2C457&ssl=1)

> Soon it was only a trickle.  
> The fish gasped and flapped, the frogs jumped away, and the reeds withered and died.

> 不久，溪流只剩涓滴细流。  
> 鱼儿喘息扑腾，青蛙纷纷跳离，芦苇枯萎死去。

![ ](https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/thewantingmonster7.jpg?resize=680%2C457&ssl=1)

Triumphant and drunk on its own power, the Wanting Monster now wonders how much more damage it can do to these peaceful people. So it turns to Mrs. Walton next, who is gathering flowers in the field for her dear friend Maria, and whispers into her ear.

“想要怪兽”志得意满，沉醉于自身力量，不禁思忖：它还能给这些平和的人们造成多大的伤害？于是，它转向正在田野里为挚友玛丽亚采摘鲜花的沃尔顿太太，凑近她的耳畔低语。

![ ](https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/thewantingmonster8.jpg?resize=680%2C457&ssl=1)

> Mrs. Walton began to frown and fret.  
> She was irritated. Why was she picking flowers for Maria when it was really she herself who deserved them?  
> She should fill her own house with flowers.  
> Yes, she should have the most fragrant, the most colorful, the most stylish house in the whole village.  
> Everyone would admire it. Everyone would envy her.

> 沃尔顿太太开始皱眉、焦躁。  
> 她感到恼火：为何要为玛丽亚采花？明明自己才最配拥有这些花！  
> 她该用鲜花装点自己的家。  
> 是的，她该拥有整个村庄里最芬芳、最绚丽、最时髦的宅邸。  
> 所有人都会钦羡它，所有人都会嫉妒她。

The other women watch Mrs. Walton pick all the flowers she can carry, and suddenly they too are aflame with the mania for owning the flowers. Soon, no flowers are left and the bees are bereft of pollen, the butterflies fly away, and the wrens and finches have nowhere to nest.

其他妇女目睹沃尔顿太太采走所有能捧起的鲜花，霎时间，她们也燃起一股占有鲜花的狂热。很快，野花被采撷殆尽，蜜蜂失去花粉，蝴蝶飞离，鹪鹩与朱雀再无筑巢之所。

![ ](https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/thewantingmonster9.jpg?resize=680%2C457&ssl=1)

![ ](https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/thewantingmonster24.jpg?resize=680%2C453&ssl=1)

The Wanting Monster stomps across the flowerless fields, gloating.

“想要怪兽”踏过寸草不生的旷野，洋洋自得。

That night, it visits Mr. Newton — the town’s most passionate stargazer — and whispers into Mr. Newton’s ear.

当夜，它造访牛顿先生——镇上最热忱的观星者——并将低语送入他的耳中。

![ ](https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/thewantingmonster10.jpg?resize=680%2C457&ssl=1)

![ ](https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/thewantingmonster27.jpg?w=680&ssl=1)

Suddenly possessed with the desire to own the stars, he heads to the forest and cuts down a great old tree to build himself a ladder, then climbs into the night and takes a star.

他骤然被占有星辰的欲望攫住，奔向森林砍倒一棵苍劲古树，造出一架长梯，随即攀入夜空，摘下一颗星辰。

![ ](https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/thewantingmonster11.jpg?resize=680%2C457&ssl=1)

I am reminded here of this miniature etching by William Blake, which I suspect might have inspired Read’s art:

此刻，我不禁想起威廉·布莱克（William Blake）的这幅微型蚀刻画，我怀疑它或许曾启发里德的创作：

![I Want! I Want! by William Blake, 1793. (Available as a print and as stationery cards.)](https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Iwant_blake.jpg?resize=680%2C988&ssl=1)  
*I Want! I Want!* by William Blake, 1793. (Available as [a print](https://society6.com/product/i-want-i-want-by-william-blake-1793_print?curator=brainpicker) and as [stationery cards](https://society6.com/product/i-want-i-want-by-william-blake-1793_cards?curator=brainpicker).)

《我想要！我想要！》，威廉·布莱克，1793年。（可购[印刷画](https://society6.com/product/i-want-i-want-by-william-blake-1793_print?curator=brainpicker)及[文具卡片](https://society6.com/product/i-want-i-want-by-william-blake-1793_cards?curator=brainpicker)。）

Ms. Grimehart watches Mr. Newton and, unable to bear possessing no stars herself, she cuts down not one tree but two to make an even bigger ladder and snatches not one star but five.

格里姆哈特女士目睹牛顿先生之举，无法容忍自己一颗星辰也没有，便砍倒两棵而非一棵树，造出更长的梯子，夺走的也不止一颗，而是五颗星辰。

![ ](https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/thewantingmonster12.jpg?resize=680%2C457&ssl=1)

More and more ladders rise up and the sky soon grows starless. With the stream gone and the flowers gone and the forest gone, with the birds silent and the bees still, this tranquil little world finds itself unworlded.

越来越多的梯子拔地而起，天空迅即变得群星寂灭。溪流干涸、鲜花凋零、森林消失，鸟儿噤声、蜜蜂停驻——这方宁静的小世界，竟彻底失却了自身的世界性。

![ ](https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/thewantingmonster13.jpg?resize=680%2C457&ssl=1)

> The village was quiet and colorless and gloomy. The children wept. They had loved their forest and their little stream. They missed the singing birds, the sunlit flowers, the shining stars.

> 村庄一片死寂、褪色、阴郁。孩子们啜泣不止。他们深爱着自己的森林与那条小小的溪流。他们怀念歌唱的鸟儿、沐浴阳光的花朵、熠熠生辉的星辰。

People, unable to console the children, begin to leave. The Wanting Monster roars with self-congratulation.

人们无力抚慰孩子，开始陆续离去。“想要怪兽”则发出自我嘉许的咆哮。

![ ](https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/thewantingmonster25.jpg?resize=680%2C453&ssl=1)

![ ](https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/thewantingmonster14.jpg?resize=680%2C457&ssl=1)

This time, everyone hears the roar and begins to wonder about the menacing presence. It is Billie Ray who first sees it and, pointing, tells the townsfolk that there is a monster in their midst. Naming a hurt has a way of opening up the space for healing — as soon as the little girl names the menace, everyone sees it clear as daylight. Suddenly, the Wanting Monster grows “no bigger than a beetle.” It is only those things of which we are not fully conscious that have the power to possess us.

这一次，所有人都听见了那声咆哮，并开始疑虑这威胁性存在的真容。最先发现它的，是比莉·蕾——她指着它，告诉镇民：我们中间藏着一只怪兽。“为伤痛命名”，自有其开启疗愈空间的力量——小女孩甫一说出“怪兽”之名，众人便如见天光般清晰看见它。“想要怪兽”骤然缩至“甲虫大小”。唯有那些我们尚未全然觉察之物，才拥有支配我们的力量。

But when the grownups lurch to stomp the tiny monster, Billie Ray stops them, leans down and asks the suddenly helpless creature if it needs a cuddle.

但当大人们猛然扑上前欲踩扁这微小的怪兽时，比莉·蕾却拦住了他们，俯身轻问这忽然无助的生灵：它需要一个拥抱吗？

![ ](https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/thewantingmonster15.jpg?resize=680%2C457&ssl=1)

![ ](https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/thewantingmonster16.jpg?w=680&ssl=1)

> The Wanting Monster climbed into the palm of her hand. It was tired, after all, and the hand was soft and warm. It lay down. Billie Ray cupped her other hand to make a roof, and then she wandered toward the dry river bed, where she sat on its banks and began to rock her hand and sing the lullaby her mother had once sung to her.  
> No one had ever sung to the Wanting Monster before. Nor had it ever been cared for. And the Wanting Monster didn’t know quite how those things felt — not really.

> “想要怪兽”爬进了她摊开的掌心。毕竟它已疲惫不堪，而那只小手柔软又温暖。它躺了下来。比莉·蕾用另一只手轻轻覆住，搭成一个小小屋顶，而后缓步走向干涸的河床，在岸边坐下，轻轻摇晃手掌，哼起母亲曾为自己唱过的摇篮曲。  
> 从未有人为“想要怪兽”唱过歌，也从未有人照料过它。而“想要怪兽”并不真正懂得——那些感受究竟是怎样的。

Listening to the lullaby, the Wanting Monster begins to weep. “There, there,” Billie Ray comforts it, “Oh, dearest heart.” The Wanting Monster doesn’t know how to bear all this tenderness — how many of us really do — and so it goes on weeping “sorrowful, endless tears” that begin replenishing the stream.

听着摇篮曲，“想要怪兽”开始啜泣。“好了，好了，”比莉·蕾安慰它，“哦，最亲爱的心啊。”“想要怪兽”不知该如何承受这般温柔——我们中有多少人真正懂得？——于是它继续流淌“悲伤而无尽的泪水”，而泪水，竟开始重新充盈溪流。

Everyone else, listening and watching, begins to weep too.

其他人听着、看着，也纷纷落泪。

![ ](https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/thewantingmonster17.jpg?w=680&ssl=1)

> A great mournful lament filled the valley.  
> Tears swelled the little stream, and it rushed like a river…  
> What had been withheld was released; what had dried up, flowed.  
> What had hardened was becoming soft again.

> 一声巨大而悲恸的哀叹回荡山谷。  
> 泪水涨满了小溪，它奔涌如江河……  
> 曾被压抑的，得以释放；曾干涸的，重新流淌。  
> 曾僵硬的，正渐渐变软。

People unpack their suitcases, take the stars out of their pockets, and set about collecting seeds, tilling the ground, and filling watering cans to replant the trees and flowers.

人们打开行李箱，将星辰从口袋中取出，随即着手收集种子、翻耕土地、灌满喷壶，重新栽种树木与鲜花。

![ ](https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/thewantingmonster18.jpg?w=680&ssl=1)

![ ](https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/thewantingmonster19.jpg?w=680&ssl=1)

![ ](https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/thewantingmonster20.jpg?w=680&ssl=1)

As the birds return and the night reconstellates, the Wanting Monster finally stops weeping and, looking up wonder-smitten at the stars lavishing the world with all that abundant beauty, feels, finally, slaked of want.

随着鸟儿归来、夜空重聚星辰，“想要怪兽”终于停止哭泣，仰起头，以充满惊奇的目光凝望星空——那浩瀚丰盛之美正慷慨洒向人间——它终于真切地感到，“想要”之渴，已然消解。

donating = loving

捐赠 = 爱

For seventeen years, I have been spending hundreds of hours and thousands of dollars each month composing *The Marginalian* (which [bore the outgrown name *Brain Pickings*](https://www.themarginalian.org/2021/10/22/brain-pickings-becoming-the-marginalian/) for its first fifteen years). It has remained free and ad-free and alive thanks to patronage from readers. I have no staff, no interns, no assistant — a thoroughly one-woman labor of love that is also my life and my livelihood. If this labor makes your own life more livable in any way, please consider lending a helping hand with a [donation](https://www.themarginalian.org/donate/). Your support makes all the difference.

十七年来，我每月耗费数百小时、数千美元，独自撰写《边际主义者》（*The Marginalian*）（其前十五年曾使用已不合时宜的名称《大脑拾穗者》[*Brain Pickings*](https://www.themarginalian.org/2021/10/22/brain-pickings-becoming-the-marginalian/)）。它之所以始终免费、无广告、生生不息，全赖读者们的慷慨资助。我没有员工、没有实习生、没有助理——这是一场彻头彻尾由我一人完成的爱之劳作，亦是我的生命与生计所在。若这份劳作以任何方式让你的生活更易栖居，请考虑通过[捐赠](https://www.themarginalian.org/donate/)伸出援手。你的支持，意义非凡。
