<p>Most advice about consistency sounds the same: try harder, be more disciplined, push through resistance. Discipline is often seen as the difference between people who succeed and people who don’t. And if you fall off, the explanation is usually moralized: not enough willpower, lack of grit, laziness.</p>



<p>But people don’t fail because they don’t try hard enough. They fail because the system they’re operating in makes sustained effort too costly.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading">From discipline to devotion</h2>



<p>From a scientific perspective, discipline is the ability to apply self-control to override impulses in service of longer-term goals.</p>



<p>Decades of research <a href="https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/19485506251385007">suggest</a> that self-control <em>does</em> predict positive outcomes. But it also shows something more subtle: self-control works best when it’s used sparingly. When people rely on constant “effortful inhibition” (forcing themselves to act) their performance degrades over time.</p>



<p>That’s because this kind of effortful inhibition activates brain networks that are metabolically expensive and sensitive to stress and fatigue.</p>



<p>Instead, research <a href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352250X24000952">finds</a> that people who appear highly disciplined are not constantly exerting more willpower. Rather, they tend to rely on <a href="https://nesslabs.com/habits-routines-rituals-intentionality-curve">habits, routines, rituals</a> to maintain their wellbeing, and ways to design their environment that reduce the need for active control.</p>



<p>This is where devotion becomes a more useful tool than discipline. Etymologically, devotion comes from the Latin <em>devovēre</em>: to dedicate by a vow, to promise solemnly. Devotion implies commitment rooted in meaning and identity rather than forceful compliance with a rule.</p>



<p>When you’re devoted to an action, you don’t force yourself to act; the action expresses something you value.</p>



<p>Instead of effortful inihibition, this maps onto what researchers <a href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/psychology/harmonious-passion">call</a> “harmonious passion” – engagement that is freely chosen and integrated into your identity. Harmonious passion has been linked to greater persistence, better well-being, and being more likely to get in the flow.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading">How to design for devoted action</h2>



<p>You’ve probably noticed that caring deeply about something doesn’t guarantee that you can act on it consistently. That’s because devotion doesn’t exist in a vacuum, and its impact depends heavily on friction – the total resistance between intention and action.</p>



<p>Friction can come from your environment, from a lack of skills or routines, or from the activation energy required to get started.</p>



<p>Two people can be equally devoted and have radically different outcomes because one is operating in a low-friction system and the other in a high-friction one.</p>



<figure class="wp-block-image size-large"><img alt="Devotion-Friction Matrix of Devoted Action by Anne-Laure Le Cunff (Ness Labs)" class="wp-image-17824" height="576" src="https://nesslabs.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/devotion-friction-matrix-1024x576.png" width="1024" /></figure>



<p>The Devotion-Friction Matrix of devoted action has four states:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Flow</strong> (high devotion, low friction) where action feels natural and repeatable;</li>



<li><strong>Strain</strong> (high devotion, high friction) where caring is high but the cost of doing it is high too;</li>



<li><strong>Coasting</strong> (low devotion, low friction) where you keep going mostly because it’s easy;</li>



<li><strong>Avoidance</strong> (low devotion, high friction) where the task feels both unrewarding and hard to start, so it gets postponed or completely dropped.</li>
</ul>



<p>There’s no point optimizing for maximal devotion or minimal friction in isolation. To get in the flow, you need to optimize for devoted action, where what you care about deeply is also easy enough to do repeatedly.</p>



<p>Here are three evidence-backed ways to do that:</p>



<p><strong>1. Lower your activation energy.</strong> You might care a lot about working out or writing, but find yourself procrastinating. When that happens, stop focusing on finishing and focus on starting instead. For example, don’t say you’ll do a work out. Just put on your running shoes and step outside. Don’t say you’ll write the piece. Just open the document and write one sentence.</p>



<p><strong>2. Design your environment. </strong>Make the desired action the default and the distractions slightly inconvenient. For instance, eave your book on your pillow and charge your phone in another room, or make unhealthy ones high-friction by putting them out of reach.</p>



<p><strong>3. Run tiny experiments</strong>: Turn curiosity itself into an act of devotion. <a href="https://nesslabs.com/personal-experiments">Pick one action to test for a specific duration</a>, playing with variables such as timing, location or accountability, then observe what reduces effort and increases intrinsic reward.</p>



<p>If you want to show up reliably, don’t ask how to force yourself to work harder. Ask what you’re devoted to and what unnecessary friction is standing in the way. This way, consistency becomes a property of the system itself.</p>

## 中文译文

关于保持一致性的大多数建议听起来都差不多：再努力一点，更有纪律性一点，硬着头皮克服阻力。纪律常被视为成功者与失败者之间的分水岭。而一旦你中途放弃，通常会被赋予道德评判色彩：意志力不足、缺乏毅力、懒惰。

但人们之所以失败，并非因为不够努力；而是因为他们所处的系统让持续付出变得成本过高。

从“纪律”到“热忱”

从科学角度看，纪律指的是运用自我控制能力，压制即时冲动以服务于长远目标的能力。

数十年的研究表明，自我控制确实能预示积极结果。但更微妙的发现是：自我控制最有效的时候，恰恰是它被审慎、节制地使用之时。当人们长期依赖持续的“费力抑制”（即强迫自己行动），其表现会随时间推移而逐渐下降。

这是因为这种费力抑制会激活大脑中代谢成本高昂、且对压力和疲劳高度敏感的神经网络。

相反，研究发现，那些看似高度自律的人，并非始终在调动更强的意志力；他们往往依靠习惯、常规与仪式来维系自身福祉，并通过精心设计环境，大幅降低对主动控制的需求。

此时，“热忱”便成为比“纪律”更有力的工具。“热忱”（devotion）一词源自拉丁语 devovēre，意为“以誓约献身”“郑重承诺”。它所体现的是一种根植于意义感与身份认同的承诺，而非对规则的强制服从。

当你对某件事怀有热忱时，你并非强迫自己去做；你的行动本身，就是你所珍视之物的自然流露。

这与研究者所称的“和谐型热情”（harmonious passion）相对应——一种自由选择、并已融入自我认同的投入状态。研究表明，和谐型热情与更强的坚持力、更好的心理福祉，以及更易进入心流状态密切相关。

如何设计支持热忱行动的系统

你或许已经注意到：深切关心某件事，并不意味着你就能持续付诸行动。这是因为热忱无法凭空存在；它的实际效力，极大程度取决于“阻力”——即从意图到行动之间所面临的全部阻碍。

阻力可能来自外部环境，可能源于技能或常规的缺失，也可能来自启动一项行动所需的初始能量（即“激活能”）。

两位同样满怀热忱的人，最终结果却可能天差地别——只因一人身处低阻力系统，另一人则困于高阻力系统。

由此可构建“热忱–阻力矩阵”，用以描述热忱行动的四种状态：

• 心流（高热忱、低阻力）：行动自然流畅，易于重复；  
• 紧绷（高热忱、高阻力）：虽高度在意，但执行成本同样高昂；  
• 惰行（低热忱、低阻力）：仅因事情容易而勉强维持；  
• 回避（低热忱、高阻力）：任务既无吸引力，又难以启动，因而一再拖延甚至彻底放弃。

孤立地追求最高热忱或最低阻力并无意义。要真正进入心流状态，你需要优化的是“热忱行动”本身——即你真正珍视之事，同时也足够容易，足以反复践行。

以下是三种经实证支持的具体方法：

1. 降低启动门槛。你可能非常重视锻炼或写作，却总在拖延。此时，请停止关注“完成”，转而聚焦于“开始”。例如，不要告诉自己“我要去锻炼”，只需穿上跑鞋、踏出家门；不要说“我要写完这篇文章”，只需打开文档，写下第一句话。

2. 设计你的环境。让理想行动成为默认选项，同时让干扰源略显不便。比如，把书放在枕头上，把手机拿到另一个房间充电；又或者，将不健康的食物放在难以取到的地方，人为提高其获取难度。

3. 开展微小实验：把好奇心本身转化为一种热忱行动。选定一个具体行为，在限定时间内进行测试，尝试调整时间、地点或问责机制等变量，然后观察哪些改变降低了行动阻力、提升了内在回报感。

若你想稳定可靠地投入行动，就别再问“怎样才能逼自己更努力”；而应问：“我真正热忱的是什么？又有哪些本不必要的阻力横亘其间？”如此一来，一致性便不再是对你个人的苛求，而成为系统自身的一种属性。
