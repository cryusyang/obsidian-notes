<p>Bug hunting is a mix of technical skills, persistence, and curiosity. Sometimes, the simplest bugs are overlooked because of one thing — <strong>assumptions</strong>. This story isn’t about a groundbreaking exploit; it’s about patience and why you should always follow through.</p><h3><strong>The Target: A Simple OTP-Based Login</strong></h3><p>The program I was testing — let’s call it <em>redacted.com</em> — was well-established and heavily tested. Here’s how the web app worked:</p><ol><li>Users log in using their email address.</li><li>A 6-digit numeric OTP is sent to their email.</li><li>The OTP is entered to access the account — no passwords involved.</li></ol><p>It was a straightforward mechanism, making it an ideal candidate for testing OTP brute force vulnerabilities.</p><h3><strong>The Approach: Starting the Test</strong></h3><p>Like every bug hunter, I opened Burp Suite, captured the OTP request, and sent it to Intruder for brute-forcing. With a 6-digit OTP, there were 1,000,000 possible combinations (000000 to 999999).</p><p>At first, everything seemed normal. The requests returned <strong>200 OK</strong> for invalid OTPs, indicating that the server was processing them correctly.</p><h3><strong>The Roadblock: Status Code 401</strong></h3><p>After 20 attempts, the server’s response changed. I started receiving <strong>401 Unauthorized </strong>for all subsequent OTP guesses.</p><p>Here’s where most bug hunters stop. It’s easy to assume that the system has implemented a rate limit or lockout mechanism. Even triagers often make this assumption.</p><p>But I didn’t stop. I had a habit of letting Intruder run until it either completed the range or hit a valid OTP. My curiosity pushed me to see how the server would handle a correct OTP, even when I get 401/429 Status code.</p><h3>Validation: Proving the Bug</h3><p>The triager initially doubted the severity of the issue, assuming it was protected by rate-limiting. And he asked me to takeover his account</p><figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/1024/1*UOM7W7L_fKBHV-Hf27rWSw.png" /></figure><h3>The Breakthrough: 2 Hours Later</h3><p>I let Intruder run. It was a test of patience — request after request, all I saw were <strong>401 Unauthorized</strong> responses.</p><p>After nearly <strong>200,000 requests</strong> and two hours, something incredible happened. Among the flood of 401 responses, I noticed a <strong>200 OK</strong> status code.</p><figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/438/1*78zjRgMEaiCzxh27TmbShQ.png" /></figure><p>When I inspected the response, I found that it contained the <strong>access token</strong> — the key to account access. The server had no real rate-limiting mechanism; it was merely returning 401 for invalid OTPs. A valid OTP still bypassed this pseudo-protection.</p><p>The program rewarded me with <strong>$2,200</strong> for uncovering this issue.</p><figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/856/1*KtQ6g0rkIl-Z8x_6O23Ukw.png" /></figure><h3>The Lesson: Why Most Hunters Fail to Find This Bug</h3><p>This bug wasn’t technically complex. It was simple — anyone could have found it. But what set me apart was <strong>persistence</strong>.</p><p>Here’s why most bug hunters miss bugs like this:</p><ul><li>They see <strong>401 Unauthorized</strong> responses or 429 and assume the system has implemented effective rate limiting. Let it run till a valid OTP and see how it responds.</li><li>They stop the Intruder before completing the range.</li><li>They lack the patience to let a brute-force attack run its course.</li></ul><h3>Why Persistence Matters</h3><p>This experience taught me a valuable lesson: <strong>don’t assume until it’s done.</strong> Bugs like these hide behind assumptions, and only those willing to push past them will uncover the gold.</p><p>Bug hunting isn’t always about advanced skills — it’s about perseverance. So the next time you see a 401/429 or hit what seems like a dead end, remember this story. Let the test run, follow through, and stay curious.</p><p>Happy hunting, and may your patience lead to great rewards!</p><img alt="" height="1" src="https://medium.com/_/stat?event=post.clientViewed&amp;referrerSource=full_rss&amp;postId=505f21d56732" width="1" />
