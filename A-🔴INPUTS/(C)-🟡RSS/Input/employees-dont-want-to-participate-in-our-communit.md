It’s four answers to four questions. Here we go…

这是四个问题的四个答案。我们开始吧……

<strong>1. Employees don’t participate in our community outreach activities</strong>

<strong>1. 员工不参与我们的社区外展活动</strong>

<blockquote><p>I’m a part of the “good will” committee for my office location, as well as the large committee for the whole company. Lately I’ve been feeling a struggle of getting other employees actively involved in our initiatives. Good will is a named tenet of our company, and we have a pretty healthy annual budget to go along with it.</p>
<p>We try and have one or two initiatives per month that have varying degrees of involvement. Sometimes it involved volunteering onsite (during work hours or directly after). Sometimes it’s asking for donations (gently used books or unexpired canned goods/shelf stable foods). We’re not asking people to go and spend their own money on things. The most successful ones are in-office, during office hours, but there’s only so many opportunities for those.</p>
<p>We also try and cover a variety of different areas — unhoused people, kids/schools, food scarcity, women, LGBTQIA+, community gardens and book depositories, animals, BIPOCs groups. We also ask employees about groups they would like to see us support.</p>
<p>I just feel like it’s been a struggle lately to get people involved. People never seem to have issues finding time for sporting events or happy hours, but no one seems to want to do a shift at the food bank. Shocking, I know. Any ideas on how to get people motivated?</p></blockquote>

<blockquote><p>我是所在办公地点“善意行动”委员会成员，同时也是全公司层面大型委员会的一员。近来，我深感难以推动其他员工积极投身于我们的各项倡议。“善意”是我们公司明确倡导的核心价值观之一，且公司为此配备了相当充裕的年度预算。</p>
<p>我们每月尝试开展一至两项参与程度各异的倡议活动：有时是组织员工在工作场所内现场志愿服务（可在工作时间内或下班后立即进行）；有时则是发起温和的物资捐赠倡议（如八成新图书、未过期的罐头食品或常温耐储食品）。我们从不要求员工自掏腰包购买物品。成效最显著的活动均安排在办公场所内、工作时间中进行——但这类机会毕竟有限。</p>
<p>我们亦努力覆盖多元议题领域：无家可归者、儿童/学校、粮食短缺、女性、LGBTQIA+群体、社区花园与图书角、动物保护、有色人种（BIPOC）团体等。同时，我们也主动征询员工意见，了解他们希望公司支持哪些具体组织。</p>
<p>我最近愈发感到动员员工参与实属不易：大家总能轻松抽出时间观看体育赛事或参加欢乐时光，却无人愿赴食物银行轮值服务——我知道，这听起来确实令人震惊。请问，有何建议可提升员工积极性？</p></blockquote>

They may not want to. Some people want to spend their limited downtime relaxing, and that’s okay. I’m not sure it’s appropriate for an employer to try to change that.

他们或许就是不想参与。有些人希望将本就有限的闲暇时间用于放松休憩，这完全合理。我不确定雇主是否有权或有义务去改变这种个人选择。

People are exhausted right now, and they’re at work to earn money, not to volunteer. A lot of people who are charity-minded do their charity work on their own time, and don’t feel their employer needs to <a href="https://www.askamanager.org/2019/07/my-employer-pressures-us-to-volunteer-for-its-charitable-events.html">claim the credit</a> for those efforts. If your <i>company</i> wants to do good in the community, that should mean it’s coming from <i>your company’s</i> resources — its money and its time (meaning that this should all happen on work time, not after hours, and <a href="https://www.askamanager.org/2026/04/my-offices-wellness-week-just-adds-to-our-stress.html">other work needs to be moved aside</a> to create space for it; the expectation shouldn’t be that people’s regular workloads don’t change at all to make room for it). If the company isn’t willing to do that, then this is just a value they’ve stuck on a list, not a genuine value they hold.

当前人们普遍精疲力竭，他们上班是为了挣钱，而非志愿奉献。许多富有慈善精神的人士，其公益行动均发生于个人时间，也并不认为雇主有必要为这些自发行为“邀功请赏”。若贵公司真心希望回馈社区，那这份善举理应源自公司自身的资源——即公司的资金与公司的时间（这意味着所有活动都应在工作时间内开展，而非下班后进行；同时，其他工作任务须相应让位，为公益留出空间；不应指望员工在保持原有工作量不变的前提下，额外承担这些事务）。倘若公司不愿为此投入真实资源，那么“善意”便仅是一纸空文、清单上一个虚设条目，而非真正践行的价值观。

All that said, I think you have the answer when you say, “The most successful ones are in-office, during office hours, but there’s only so many opportunities for those.” If people aren’t interested in ones outside of that category, that’s feedback worth listening to! And if there are only so many opportunities for those, then maybe you stick to those because that’s what employees are up for.

话虽如此，您自己其实已给出答案：“成效最佳的活动均在办公场所内、工作时间中进行——但此类机会毕竟有限。”倘若员工对非此类活动缺乏兴趣，这本身就是值得认真倾听的反馈！而既然此类机会本就稀缺，或许您就该聚焦于此——因为这恰恰契合员工的真实意愿与接受度。

Beyond that, talk to people! Survey employees on what they are and aren’t interested in participating in — both in terms of specific activities and general categories of activities (during work hours, 5-7 pm, drives where you bring items from home, etc.), and also ask how they’d like to see the company live out this value. That’s what ultimately should shape it.

此外，请务必与员工直接沟通！开展问卷调研，了解他们愿意或不愿参与哪些具体活动及活动类型（如：工作时间内、17:00–19:00、居家捐物等），并询问他们期望公司以何种方式切实践行这一价值观。唯有员工的真实声音，才应最终决定该价值的落地形态。

<strong>2. Should I tell my manager about a recurring issue with a coworker?</strong>

<strong>2. 我是否该向主管反映同事反复出现的问题？</strong>

<blockquote><p>I’m a receptionist at a small healthcare-adjacent company and would appreciate your advice on a recurring issue with a remote customer service representative, “Donna.”</p>
<p>Callers frequently report being hung up on a few minutes after I forward them to the customer service line. Almost every caller who tells me this has mentioned they were speaking with Donna when it happened, which is a problem I don’t encounter with any other representatives.</p>
<p>Because our work involves urgent health-related matters and long wait times, these disconnections can be pretty significant for our clients. Sometimes they’re sitting in the queue listening to hold music for 20 or 30 minutes before having to start over again because upon being transferred to Donna, they get disconnected.</p>
<p>Thing is, I’m hesitant to report this to our supervisor because the guy is a severe micromanager, and I want to avoid subjecting anybody to having to deal with him. I’ve been the subject of his ire before, and it’s not fun when his laser beams get trained on you. However, I’m growing concerned that Donna may be intentionally disconnecting calls, or has an unstable remote connection. How would you suggest I handle this situation?</p></blockquote>

<blockquote><p>我在一家规模较小、业务与医疗健康相关的企业担任前台接待员，现就一位远程客服代表“唐娜”（Donna）反复出现的问题，恳请您给予建议。</p>
<p>来电者经常向我反映：在我将其转接到客服专线几分钟后，即被挂断。几乎所有向我提及此事的来电者均表示，当时正与唐娜通话，而此问题在其他任何客服代表处均未出现。</p>
<p>鉴于我们的工作涉及紧急健康事务且客户等待时间漫长，此类意外断线对客户影响尤为严重。有时，客户已在排队中聆听等待音乐长达二三十分钟，却因转接至唐娜后即遭断线，不得不一切重来。</p>
<p>然而，我犹豫是否向主管上报此事，因其是一位严苛的微观管理者，我希望能避免让任何人被迫面对他。我本人此前曾成为他发泄怒火的对象——当他的“聚光灯”打在你身上时，绝非愉快体验。不过，我日益担忧：唐娜或许是故意挂断电话，抑或其远程网络连接极不稳定。您建议我该如何处理这一状况？</p></blockquote>

You need to tell your manager about it. It’s a significant issue, and it’s got to be incredibly frustrating for your callers — and your position means you’re probably the only person (other than Donna) who’s aware of it. Your position does give you standing to raise it, because you’re partly responsible for the experience that callers have when they contact your company. (That doesn’t mean you’re responsible for whatever is happening once you transfer them, of course — just that when you have info about their experience that no one else has, you do need to make someone higher up aware.)

您必须就此问题向主管汇报。这是一个重大问题，必然令来电客户倍感沮丧——而您的岗位意味着，除唐娜本人外，您很可能是唯一知晓此事的人。您确有充分理由提出此事，因为您对客户联络贵公司时的整体体验负有部分责任。（当然，这并非指您需为转接后的状况担责——而是说，当您掌握着他人所不具备的客户体验信息时，您就有义务向上级通报。）

If that mean Donna gets micromanaged … well, some closer management might be needed here, because either Donna is intentionally disconnecting callers or is aware she’s frequently losing her connection and not bothering to ask for help to get that fixed. Or maybe she has, in which case she’ll presumably explain that to her boss — but either way, this is something you should escalate.

即便这意味着唐娜将面临更严密的管理……也实属必要。因为真相可能有两种：要么唐娜确系故意挂断电话；要么她明知自身网络频繁中断，却未主动寻求协助以解决问题。当然，也可能她已就此求助——届时她自会向主管说明情况。但无论如何，此事均需您及时升级上报。

The subject line of your email to me was, “Is it appropriate to snitch on my coworker for this?” and this is not snitching. This is letting your manager know about a work issue that’s highly relevant to how well your organization is serving clients.

您发给我的邮件主题是：“就此问题向同事‘打小报告’是否恰当？”——但此举绝非“打小报告”，而是向主管通报一项与贵公司服务客户质量高度相关的工作问题。

<strong>3. Disabled and losing access to parking</strong>

<strong>3. 残障人士即将失去停车便利</strong>

<blockquote><p>I work at a university that recently announced a major campus construction project that will eliminate a significant amount of central parking, including areas closest to my building. The announcement framed this as an exciting improvement to campus life, but for some of us, it creates a serious accessibility problem.</p>
<p>I have a disability that affects my mobility. Even now, I arrive over an hour early just to secure one of the limited nearby parking spots, and I still face a several-minute walk to my building, which can be difficult depending on the day. I am not the only one who does this in my building. There’s several of us who do this daily. With these lots closing, we are all extremely concerned that we simply won’t be able to access my workplace in a reliable or sustainable way.</p>
<p>This is the second time in a few years that staff parking has been reduced. In this case, the project will also remove several accessible parking spaces across campus, and they are not being replaced.</p>
<p>The university’s suggestion is to contact parking services for alternatives, but based on past experience, those alternatives are not workable for me. They typically recommend using a shuttle system, which is difficult for me to physically navigate, or parking farther away. Factoring in wait times and travel, that could add close to an hour to my commute each day. I also have religious commitments after work on Fridays, and this added time would make it difficult or impossible to attend. The shuttle isn’t always the most reliable. It’s also small and with more people probably needing to use this, it could add in well over an hour to my commute daily.</p>
<p>Another option is purchasing access to parking at a nearby institution, but that requires an upfront cost of over $700 annually, which is not financially feasible for most people.</p>
<p>My job could be performed remotely, but remote work is not currently offered as an option, and I worry that pushing too hard on that could negatively affect my job security.</p>
<p>I have not reached out to parking services, as I am not optimistic about the response they will give. Historically, the university has been resistant to feedback on parking and accessibility concerns or they ignore emails totally. Another colleague reached out to them with these exact concerns, but she’s certain she’ll not receive a response or they will not care as they have when there were issues with parking spots blocked off earlier last year.</p>
<p>At what point does this become an ADA issue? What are my options for advocating for reasonable accommodations in a situation like this? Would requesting remote work on days when I cannot access parking be reasonable, or am I better off pursuing a formal accommodation through HR or another route such as an anonymous ADA complaint?</p></blockquote>

<blockquote><p>我在一所大学任职，校方近期宣布一项大型校园建设工程，将大幅削减中心区域停车位，包括紧邻我办公大楼的车位。公告将此举描绘为提升校园生活的“激动人心的改进”，但对我们部分人而言，这却构成严峻的无障碍通行障碍。</p>
<p>我患有影响行动能力的残障。即便目前，我也需提前一个多小时抵达，只为抢到为数不多的就近车位；即便如此，前往办公楼仍需步行数分钟，而每日状况不同，这段路程对我而言时常十分艰难。在我所在办公楼内，并非仅我一人如此——每日坚持这样做的同事大有人在。随着这些停车场关闭，我们所有人极度忧虑：今后将无法以可靠且可持续的方式抵达工作岗位。</p>
<p>这已是近几年来第二次缩减教职员工停车位。此次工程还将移除校园内多处无障碍停车位，且并无替代方案。</p>
<p>校方建议我们联系停车服务中心寻求替代方案，但依据过往经验，这些方案对我均不可行：通常推荐使用摆渡车系统，而其物理环境令我难以通行；或建议停至更远位置。综合考虑候车与通勤时间，每日通勤或将额外增加近一小时。此外，我每周五下班后尚有宗教活动安排，新增耗时将使我难以甚至无法出席。摆渡车本身可靠性欠佳，且车厢狭小；随着更多人依赖此系统，每日通勤时间恐将增加逾一小时。</p>
<p>另一选项是购买邻近机构的停车权限，但需预付逾700美元年费，这对多数人而言经济上不可承受。</p>
<p>我的工作本可远程完成，但目前校方并未提供远程办公选项；我亦担心过度主张此事可能危及自身职位安全。</p>
<p>我尚未联系停车服务中心，因对其回应毫无信心。历史表明，校方一贯抗拒关于停车及无障碍通行的反馈，或直接无视相关邮件。另一位同事曾就完全相同的问题致信该部门，但她确信将收不到任何回复——正如去年早些时候停车位被临时占用时的情形一样，对方同样置之不理。</p>
<p>此情形何时构成《美国残障人士法案》（ADA）问题？在此类情况下，我可通过哪些途径争取合理便利？在无法获得停车位的日子申请远程办公是否合理？抑或我更应通过人力资源部正式申请便利措施，或采取其他路径（例如提交匿名ADA投诉）？</p></blockquote>

It’s an ADA issue now. Under the ADA, employers with 15 or more employees are required to provide accessible parking as a reasonable accommodation for workers with disabilities, and they must engage in an interactive process to find solutions if spots are unavailable.

这当下已是ADA问题。根据《美国残障人士法案》，雇员达15人及以上的雇主，有法定义务为残障员工提供无障碍停车位作为合理便利措施；若现有车位不足，雇主必须启动互动式磋商流程，共同探寻可行解决方案。

You should submit a request in writing to HR with the subject line, “official request for accommodations under the Americans with Disabilities Act.” Don’t go through parking services since they’ve been unresponsive; approach it as a legally required medical accommodation with HR.

您应以书面形式向人力资源部提交申请，邮件主题明确标注：“依据《美国残障人士法案》提出的正式便利措施申请”。切勿经由停车服务中心——因其素来不予回应；而应将此事定性为一项受法律保障的医疗相关便利需求，直接对接人力资源部。

<strong>4. Independent contractor vs. employee</strong>

<strong>4. 独立承包商与雇员之辨</strong>

<blockquote><p>I have a question regarding <a href="https://www.askamanager.org/2013/05/what-to-do-when-your-employer-illegally-treats-you-as-a-contractor.html">W2 vs 1099 contractor definitions</a>. I understand one key legal difference is a contractor sets their own hours. Does that mean that <i>no</i> employment where I commit to showing up at a certain time can be a 1099? Like, let’s say I am a tutor. I choose my students and can let them go. But let’s say I sign on to tutor someone intensively for two hours once a week for a semester at a specific time slot, and I sign a contract to that effect, do they now have to give me a W2?</p></blockquote>

<blockquote><p>我有关于<a href="https://www.askamanager.org/2013/05/what-to-do-when-your-employer-illegally-treats-you-as-a-contractor.html">W2雇员与1099独立承包商界定标准</a>的问题。我理解二者一项关键法律区别在于：承包商自主决定工作时间。这是否意味着，只要我承诺在特定时间到岗，就绝不可能被认定为1099承包商？举例而言，我是一名家教：我自主选择学生，也可随时终止合作。但假设我签约为某位学生提供密集辅导——每周固定时段两小时，持续一学期，并签署相应合同——此时对方是否就必须向我发放W2表格？</p></blockquote>

The IRS doesn’t use a black and white test for contractors where if you don’t set your own hours, you can’t be a contractor. Rather, they look at the totality of the circumstances. They look at three factors: (1) behavioral — does the company control or have the right to control what the worker does and how the worker does their job? (2) financial — are the business aspects of the job (like how the worker is paid, whether expenses are reimbursed, and who provides tools/supplies) controlled by the company? (3) type of relationship — are there written contracts or employee-type benefits (insurance, vacation pay, etc.) and is the work a key aspect of the business? The law says, “Businesses must weigh all these factors when determining whether a worker is an employee or independent contractor. Some factors may indicate that the worker is an employee, while other factors indicate that the worker is an independent contractor. There is no ‘magic’ or set number of factors that makes the worker an employee or an independent contractor and no one factor stands alone in making this determination. Also, factors which are relevant in one situation may not be relevant in another. The keys are to look at the entire relationship and consider the extent of the right to direct and control the worker.”

美国国税局（IRS）判定承包商身份时，并非采用“非黑即白”的绝对标准——即并非简单以“是否自主排定工作时间”为唯一依据。相反，IRS考察的是整体事实与具体情况，聚焦三大核心维度：（1）行为控制——公司是否实际控制或有权控制工作者的工作内容及其执行方式？（2）财务控制——工作的商业层面要素（如薪酬支付方式、费用是否报销、工具/耗材由谁提供等）是否由公司掌控？（3）关系性质——是否存在书面合同或雇员式福利（如保险、带薪休假等）？该工作是否构成公司业务的关键环节？法律规定：“企业在判定工作者属于雇员还是独立承包商时，必须全面权衡上述所有因素。某些因素可能指向雇员身份，而另一些则指向承包商身份。不存在某个‘神奇’因素或固定数量的因素可单独决定身份归属；同一因素在不同情境下相关性亦可能不同。关键在于审视整体雇佣关系，并重点评估公司对工作者的指导与管控权范围。”

There <i>are</i> independent contractors who commit to working set hours. Doing that doesn’t on its own make you an employee.

确实存在承诺固定工作时段的独立承包商。单凭这一点，并不自动使您成为雇员。
