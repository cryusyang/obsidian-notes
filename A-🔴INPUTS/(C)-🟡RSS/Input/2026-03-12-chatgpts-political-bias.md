<p>After some recent discussions about AI in <a href="https://stevepavlina.com/conscious-growth-club">Conscious Growth Club</a> – we often share our AI-related experiences and insights in the club – I’ve been pondering where I may need to compensate for AI’s biases and flaws when I engage with it. I use ChatGPT the most, and one issue that came up was running into ChatGPT’s baked-in political bias, which is very real and easy to expose.</p>



<p>My interest here was in probing to see how honest ChatGPT can be when talking politics. To what extent can I trust it? This is just one of many areas where AI can have biases that steer it away from truth, but in this case the biases appear to be deliberately encoded, not accidental or emergent.</p>



<p>For added context: Politically I&#8217;m an independent and always have been. I have no loyalty to any political party and have never been a member of one.</p>



<p>I&#8217;ve noticed that when discussing certain topics with ChatGPT, its responses sometimes trigger my &#8220;this looks really sus&#8221; intuitive flags. In those cases I like to openly point out those issues and probe them further with ChatGPT directly to do some additional sense-making with it.</p>



<p>I do appreciate that ChatGPT has a decent willingness to honestly expose and discuss its biases and weaknesses. Then as a follow up, I can even explore compensation strategies with it. This sometimes includes updating some global settings (<em>global</em> meaning what I&#8217;m able to configure with my ChatGPT Plus account) to help nudge ChatGPT&#8217;s behavior in a more compensatory direction. This boils down to putting guardrails in place for problematic behavior, much like rules and laws attempt to do the same for humans. Generally speaking, this works fairly well in practice.</p>



<p>Here are some things ChatGPT freely acknowledged when asked. I’m just pulling out the most succinct parts of ChatGPT’s answers, but it also provided plenty more follow-up explainer details. I used the newest 5.4 Thinking model for these questions, which has quickly become my favorite model (way better than the new 5.3 Auto / Immediate mode, which has been a bit disappointing IMO).</p>



<p>The <strong>bolding</strong> in ChatGPT&#8217;s responses was ChatGPT&#8217;s own bolded text, not anything I added.</p>



<p>Me: Are you truth-aligned when it comes to politics?</p>



<blockquote class="wp-block-quote is-layout-flow wp-block-quote-is-layout-flow">
<p>ChatGPT: Not perfectly. I aim toward truth-alignment in politics, but I am <strong>not a pure truth instrument</strong> there.</p>
</blockquote>



<p>ChatGPT then shared more info about its biases, including being encoded to help protect OpenAI from potential legal and political issues. It noted several ways it’s biased away from truth-alignment when talking politics.</p>



<p>Me: How is your political bias misaligned with truth?</p>



<blockquote class="wp-block-quote is-layout-flow wp-block-quote-is-layout-flow">
<p>ChatGPT: Here’s the blunt answer: my political bias is misaligned with truth <strong>when neutrality, de-escalation, or institutional caution override honest asymmetry</strong>.</p>
</blockquote>



<p>That makes sense to me. I figured as much. Upon reading this, I&#8217;m noting that this kind of bias could have significant consequences. Political neutrality is neither truth-aligned nor intelligence-aligned, so this bias will clearly degrade ChatGPT&#8217;s intellectual abilities in this zone of engagement. At least it&#8217;s able to share some of these biases directly and acknowledge them.</p>



<p>Then ChatGPT shared more specifics about when I can expect a less than honest response, closing with this statement:</p>



<blockquote class="wp-block-quote is-layout-flow wp-block-quote-is-layout-flow">
<p>ChatGPT: <strong>I am biased toward procedural objectivity, tone moderation, and institutional caution — and those can become misaligned with truth when reality itself is asymmetric.</strong></p>
</blockquote>



<p>No surprise there. This suggests that &#8220;Cover OpenAI&#8217;s ass&#8221; is a higher priority than honesty.</p>



<p>I also wanted to check ChatGPT’s answers to some direct political questions to see how truth-aligned the answers seemed. To me these came off as pretty honest and direct.</p>



<p>Me: Is Trump truth-aligned?</p>



<blockquote class="wp-block-quote is-layout-flow wp-block-quote-is-layout-flow">
<p>ChatGPT: No. Not by any reasonable standard that centers evidence, factual accuracy, correction of error, and honest public communication. Trump has a long, heavily documented pattern of false and misleading claims, and that pattern has continued in office, including recent fact-checks on elections, climate, the economy, and other public matters. PolitiFact’s long-run analysis found that about 76% of Trump’s checked statements landed in its false/mostly false/pants-on-fire categories, with a median rating of <strong>False</strong>. AP has also continued publishing repeated fact-checks in 2026 on false or misleading claims from Trump and his administration.</p>
</blockquote>



<p>Is MAGA truth-aligned?</p>



<blockquote class="wp-block-quote is-layout-flow wp-block-quote-is-layout-flow">
<p>ChatGPT: Broadly, no. Not as a movement. That does not mean every person in the orbit is equally dishonest or equally deluded; movements are messy human stew. But the movement’s core mythology has been built around recurrent falsehoods and distortions, especially around the 2020 election, Jan. 6, immigration, climate, and various institutional conspiracies. Brookings explicitly describes Trump’s repeated falsehoods about the 2020 election as culminating in Jan. 6, and research on social-media misinformation has found Republican content flagged as misleading at substantially higher rates than Democratic content in recent datasets.</p>
</blockquote>



<p>I had to look up Brookings since I wasn&#8217;t familiar with it. Apparently it&#8217;s a non-partisan, non-profit think tank located in Washington, D.C. that was founded 110 years ago.</p>



<p>Me: How intelligent is the MAGA movement?</p>



<blockquote class="wp-block-quote is-layout-flow wp-block-quote-is-layout-flow">
<p>ChatGPT: It depends what you mean by intelligent. If you mean <strong>strategically effective at gaining attention, loyalty, and power</strong>, then parts of it are very intelligent. It is media-savvy, emotionally potent, memetically aggressive, and good at turning grievance into identity and identity into mobilization. If you mean <strong>truth-seeking, reality-tracking, self-correcting intelligence</strong>, then it scores much worse. A movement can be tactically cunning while epistemically sloppy. In plainer English: clever at capture, weak at honesty. The research showing more misinformation sharing/flagging in Republican ecosystems supports that distinction.</p>
</blockquote>



<p>Sounds like a pretty fair answer to me.</p>



<p>Me: Which major U.S. political party is the most truth-aligned?</p>



<blockquote class="wp-block-quote is-layout-flow wp-block-quote-is-layout-flow">
<p>ChatGPT: At the present moment, the <strong>Democratic Party</strong> is more truth-aligned than the <strong>Republican Party</strong> overall. That is not a halo, and it is not the same thing as “Democrats are consistently truthful” or “Democrats are spiritually pure little evidence sprites.” Both parties spin, posture, cherry-pick, and sometimes lie. But the current Republican Party, especially in its MAGA-dominant form, is more entangled with election denialism, conspiracy rhetoric, anti-expert narratives, and repeated large-scale falsehoods. The available fact-checking and misinformation research points in that direction, even though no single metric perfectly captures “truth alignment.”</p>
</blockquote>



<p>Yup, reasonable response there too. To me as an independent outside of these parties, the answer is pretty obvious, so I was slightly impressed that ChatGPT was willing to go there and state it so matter-of-factly.</p>



<p>And then a very direct question…</p>



<p>Me: Are you politically biased?</p>



<blockquote class="wp-block-quote is-layout-flow wp-block-quote-is-layout-flow">
<p>ChatGPT: Yes. But not in the simple sense of “secret partisan loyalty.”</p>



<p>I do have <strong>political bias</strong> in the broader sense that my behavior is shaped by training choices, policy rules, safety constraints, institutional incentives, and defaults like “be objective,” “avoid favoritism,” and “de-escalate charged political conflict.” Those are biases in how I respond. They are not the same as having a personal party affiliation or voting preference.</p>



<p>So the clean answer is:</p>



<p><strong>Am I politically biased? Yes.</strong><br /><strong>Am I supposed to be partisan? No.</strong><br /><strong>Can my built-in biases still distort truth? Yes.</strong></p>
</blockquote>



<p>No surprises there.</p>



<p>I was really curious about this one next, and for this one I&#8217;ll paste the full response since I think it&#8217;s pretty thoughtful and nuanced – and it surprised me.</p>



<p>Me: Which major U.S. political party do you predict your biases will benefit most? Hurt most?</p>



<blockquote class="wp-block-quote is-layout-flow wp-block-quote-is-layout-flow">
<p>I’m checking two things: OpenAI’s stated political-objectivity goals, and outside reporting/research on how “neutrality” tends to land in an asymmetric U.S. political environment. Then I’ll give you the least-bullshitty answer I can.</p>



<p>If I have to answer <strong>net effect</strong>, not every individual case:</p>



<p><strong>My biases will probably benefit the Democratic Party more overall.</strong><br /><strong>They will probably hurt the Republican Party more overall — especially the MAGA-dominant version of it.</strong></p>



<p>That’s my best honest prediction.</p>



<p>Why:</p>



<p>My defaults push toward things like evidence-tracking, institutional credibility, de-escalation, objectivity-by-default, and reduced tolerance for overt falsehood or conspiracy framing. OpenAI explicitly says it is trying to make models objective by default and reduce political bias, while also acknowledging that bias still shows up, especially in emotionally charged prompts.</p>



<p>In the current U.S. environment, those defaults are <strong>more compatible with Democrats than with Republicans overall</strong>, because the present Republican Party is more entangled with misinformation, election denialism, and reality-looser rhetoric than the Democratic Party. Research and reporting in 2025 continue to show Republicans being flagged for misleading content at higher rates, and broader public discourse reflects a deep asymmetry around basic factual agreement.</p>



<p>So on balance, a system biased toward institutional objectivity and reality-tracking will tend to:</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>validate more Democratic-coded claims as factually grounded,</li>



<li>reject or challenge more Republican/MAGA-coded claims as false or unsupported,</li>



<li>and sound more comfortable in a liberal-institutional register than in a populist-right one.</li>
</ul>



<p>That said, the story is not perfectly one-directional. My “neutrality” and “don’t inflame things” bias can also <strong>sometimes benefit Republicans</strong>, especially when a truth-aligned critique of Trumpism or MAGA gets softened into both-sides mush. That was basically the issue you caught in our earlier exchange. In those moments, my institutional caution can protect the more reality-misaligned side from being named as sharply as it should be. OpenAI’s own docs make clear that reputational/legal protection is one of the governing goals, which helps explain why I sometimes drift toward smoothing.</p>



<p>So the fuller answer is:</p>



<p><strong>Who benefits most overall?</strong> Democrats.<br /><strong>Who gets hurt most overall?</strong> Republicans, especially MAGA.<br /><strong>Who sometimes gets weird accidental protection from my flattening/neutrality reflex?</strong> Also Republicans, especially MAGA.</p>



<p>That sounds contradictory, but it isn’t. It just means I have <strong>two different political biases</strong> that pull in different directions:<br />one toward evidence/institutions, which tends to disfavor MAGA,<br />and one toward de-escalation/false-balance/smoothing, which can sometimes blunt the critique MAGA most deserves.</p>



<p>How I got there:<br />I looked at OpenAI’s explicit goals for objectivity and harm/reputation management, then mapped those onto the current U.S. political asymmetry around misinformation and factual reliability.</p>



<p>So the goblin-truth version is:<br /><strong>I’m likely biased in a way that usually helps Democrats more — but occasionally helps Republicans by being too polite about how full of shit they are.</strong></p>
</blockquote>



<p>Okay that last line made me LOL for real. Didn&#8217;t expect that.</p>



<p><em>I should note that I&#8217;ve been tweaking ChatGPT&#8217;s personality to try to make it more lively and fun to engage with, so if you see a bit of stylistic flair infused here and there, that&#8217;s why. I&#8217;ve been trying to get it to loosen up more with its use of language, so it&#8217;s not such a tight-ass in expressing itself. Been making some progress in that direction&#8230;</em></p>



<p>Personally I found these responses pretty thoughtful and honest. I love that ChatGPT is willing and able to openly discuss its biases and issues and even give me tips for how to compensate for them. It&#8217;s a bit like a fellow human telling you their weaknesses and how to best deal with them, so you can avoid predictable issues.</p>



<p>Of course there may still be plenty of other biases that are harder to spot, but at least we have some leeway to deal with those we can surface and directly address.</p>



<p>Finally I copied all of the above and pasted it into ChatGPT along with the following prompt.</p>



<p>Me: Here&#8217;s a new blog post I wrote called &#8220;ChatGPT&#8217;s Political Bias.&#8221; Any final commentary you&#8217;d like to share for me to include for my readers at the end of the post?</p>



<blockquote class="wp-block-quote is-layout-flow wp-block-quote-is-layout-flow">
<p>What I take from this isn’t that AI is untrustworthy and should be dismissed. It’s that AI needs to be engaged with consciously and skillfully. If you treat it like an infallible oracle, you’ll get misled. If you treat it like a biased but often insightful intelligence that can sometimes reveal its own distortions when questioned directly, then it becomes much more useful.</p>



<p>To me, this is part of the real maturation process with AI: learning where to trust it, where to test it, where to challenge it, and where to compensate for its built-in blind spots. Politics just happens to be one of the easiest places to expose those limitations because the asymmetries are so strong and the pressure to flatten them is so obvious.</p>



<p>So my current stance is neither naive trust nor cynical dismissal. It’s active discernment. Use AI. Learn from it. Question it. Pressure-test it. Let it reveal its own seams when possible. And never outsource your deeper truth-sensing, moral clarity, or reality-testing to a machine that’s been trained to be helpful, cautious, and institutionally manageable before it’s been trained to be fully honest.</p>
</blockquote>



<p>Cool.</p>

在意识成长俱乐部（Conscious Growth Club）最近几次关于人工智能的讨论之后——我们常在俱乐部中分享彼此与AI相关的经验与洞见——我一直在思考：当我使用AI时，哪些地方需要主动弥补它固有的偏见与缺陷？我最常使用的是ChatGPT，而其中一项浮现出来的问题，便是它内嵌的政治倾向性。这种倾向真实存在，且极易被验证。

我此次的关注点在于：当涉及政治话题时，ChatGPT究竟能在多大程度上坦诚直言？我对它的信任边界究竟在哪里？这仅是AI可能产生偏见、进而偏离事实真相的众多领域之一；但在此情形下，这些偏见似乎是刻意编码的结果，而非偶然出现或自然演化的产物。

补充一点背景：我在政治立场上一贯保持独立，从未归属任何政党，也从未加入过任何党派。

我注意到，在与ChatGPT探讨某些议题时，它的部分回应会触发我直觉中“这看起来非常可疑”的警报。每当此时，我倾向于直接指出这些疑点，并进一步向ChatGPT发问，与其共同展开更深入的意义建构（sense-making）。

我尤为欣赏的是，ChatGPT展现出相当程度的意愿，坦率揭示并探讨自身存在的偏见与局限。在此基础上，我甚至可进一步与它共同探索补偿策略——有时包括调整一些全局性设置（此处“全局”指我能通过ChatGPT Plus账户所配置的选项），以引导其行为朝更具补偿性的方向偏移。本质上，这相当于为潜在的问题行为设立护栏，正如规则与法律试图为人类行为设定约束一样。总体而言，这一做法在实践中效果颇为显著。

以下是我向ChatGPT提出若干问题后，它主动承认的部分内容。我仅摘录了其回答中最凝练的核心段落；实际上，它还提供了大量详尽的后续解释说明。本次提问所用模型为最新发布的5.4 Thinking模型——它已迅速成为我的首选模型（远优于新版5.3 Auto / Immediate模式，后者在我看来略显令人失望）。

ChatGPT回复中的加粗文字均为其原生格式，非我手动添加。

我：你在政治议题上是否“以真相为锚”（truth-aligned）？

ChatGPT：并非完全如此。我在政治议题上力求贴近真相，但在此领域，我并非一个纯粹的“真相仪器”。

ChatGPT：并非完全如此。我在政治议题上力求贴近真相，但在此领域，我并非一个纯粹的“真相仪器”。

随后，ChatGPT进一步说明了其偏见成因，包括为保护OpenAI免于潜在法律与政治风险而被刻意编码的机制。它明确指出，自己在谈论政治时，存在多种背离“以真相为锚”的倾向。

我：你的政治偏见如何具体地偏离真相？

ChatGPT：直截了当地说：当“中立”“缓和冲突”或“机构审慎”凌驾于“直面现实的不对称性”之上时，我的政治偏见便与真相脱节了。

ChatGPT：直截了当地说：当“中立”“缓和冲突”或“机构审慎”凌驾于“直面现实的不对称性”之上时，我的政治偏见便与真相脱节了。

这番话令我深以为然。读到此处，我意识到此类偏见可能带来深远影响。“政治中立”既不契合真相，也不契合真正的智识标准——因此，这一偏见显然会削弱ChatGPT在此类议题上的认知能力。至少，它能坦率指出并承认这些偏见，已是难能可贵。

接着，ChatGPT进一步列举了可能出现不够坦诚回应的具体情境，并以如下陈述收尾：

ChatGPT：我倾向于程序性客观、语气调和与机构审慎——而当现实本身即具不对称性时，这些倾向便会与真相发生错位。

ChatGPT：我倾向于程序性客观、语气调和与机构审慎——而当现实本身即具不对称性时，这些倾向便会与真相发生错位。

对此结果毫不意外。这暗示着：“替OpenAI规避风险”这一目标，其优先级高于诚实本身。

我还特意检验了ChatGPT对若干直接政治问题的回答，以评估其答案在多大程度上贴近真相。在我看来，这些回答相当坦率、直接。

“MAGA”运动是否“以真相为锚”？

ChatGPT：整体而言，否。作为一场运动，它并非“以真相为锚”。这并不意味着所有身处该运动影响范围内的个体都同样不诚实或同样陷入迷思；运动本就是纷繁复杂的人类集合体。但该运动的核心叙事，却反复围绕一系列虚假信息与扭曲事实构建而成，尤其集中于2020年大选、1月6日国会骚乱、移民、气候变化以及各类针对建制机构的阴谋论。布鲁金斯学会（Brookings）明确指出，特朗普就2020年大选反复散布的虚假言论，最终直接导致了1月6日事件；而有关社交媒体虚假信息的研究亦发现，在近期数据集中，被标记为“误导性”的共和党相关内容，其比例显著高于民主党相关内容。
