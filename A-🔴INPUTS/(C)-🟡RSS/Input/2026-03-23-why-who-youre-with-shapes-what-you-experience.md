<p style="font-weight: 400;">Just over a week ago, I was in Las Vegas speaking at a conference. It was my first time staying at the Venetian, and the experience—on paper—was incredible. I was in a beautiful suite with a 16th-floor view overlooking five pools. The kind of setting most people would love.</p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">And yet, my first thought when I walked in wasn’t about the view or the room. It was, <em>“I wish Jane was here.”</em></p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">Without her, it felt like just a room with a nice view. That surprised me—but it also taught me something important.</p>
<h3>The People You’re With Shape the Experience</h3>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">I often tell people that anything I do is better when Jane is with me. She’s more fun, more adventurous, and more outgoing than I am. But here’s the deeper truth: she <em>influences</em> me to become more of those things.</p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">When I’m with her, I’m more outgoing. More willing to try new things. More present.</p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">In other words, the experience doesn’t just change because she’s there—the <em>version of me</em> in that experience changes.</p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">Think about that for a moment. Who in your life brings out a better version of you?</p>
<h3>Shared Moments Multiply Meaning</h3>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">As I approach my 62nd birthday, I’ve come to value experiences far more than things. But even more than that, I’ve come to realize: Experiences are amplified when they’re shared.</p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">Imagine you’re a golfer playing alone. There’s something peaceful about it. You play quickly and don’t have to worry about other golfers who, for a variety of reasons, could distract you.</p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">But what if you hit a hole-in-one? It wouldn’t feel the same as if friends were there to celebrate with you because joy expands when a feat like a hole-in-one is witnessed and shared.</p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">I’ve seen this firsthand traveling in Europe with close friends, Jud and Deborah. When we get together later and start reminiscing, something interesting happens. One person recalls a moment everyone else forgot. Suddenly, the memory comes rushing back.</p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">With more people, there are more perspectives—and more opportunities to relive the experience. It’s as if the experience continues long after the moment is over.</p>
<h3>The Influence of Liking—and Being Liked</h3>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">This connects directly to one of Cialdini’s principles of persuasion: liking.</p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">We often say people are more likely to say “yes” to those they know and like. That’s true but there’s a deeper layer. We’re even more open when we believe <em>they like us</em>.</p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">Why does that matter? Because when we feel liked, valued, and connected, we relax. We open up. We engage more fully. We become more ourselves—or even better versions of ourselves.</p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">That changes how we experience everything. Think about it:</p>
<ul style="font-weight: 400;">
<li>A dinner with strangers vs. a dinner with close friends</li>
<li>A trip alone vs. a trip with people who know you well</li>
<li>A success achieved quietly vs. one celebrated together</li>
</ul>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">Same events. Completely different experiences.</p>
<h3>Friends Change More Than Your Decisions—They Shape Your World</h3>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">We often think influence is about changing decisions. But in many cases, influence runs deeper. The people closest to you don’t just influence what you do… They influence how you see, feel, and remember your life.</p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">That’s powerful and it raises an important question: Are you intentional about who you share your life with? After all, the “who” may matter more than the “what.”</p>
<h3>One Final Thought</h3>
<p style="font-weight: 400;">I’m sure I’ll be back in Las Vegas someday. And I hope Jane is with me. Because I know this much—no matter how nice the room is, it won’t compare to sharing the experience with her.</p>
<p style="font-weight: 400;"><strong>What about you?</strong> Who makes your experiences better—and how do they influence the way you see the world?</p>

## 中文译文

就在一周多前，我还在拉斯维加斯参加一场会议并发表演讲。这是我第一次入住威尼斯人酒店，单从纸面描述来看，这次体验堪称惊艳：我住进一间美轮美奂的套房，16层高处的落地窗外，五座泳池尽收眼底——这正是大多数人梦寐以求的场景。

然而，当我推门而入时，脑海中浮现的第一个念头，并非窗外风景，也非房间本身，而是：“真希望简（Jane）此刻就在我身边。”

没有她，这里不过是一间视野不错的普通客房而已。这个念头令我意外，却也让我领悟到一个重要的道理：

**与你同行的人，塑造了你的体验**

我常对别人说，只要简在我身边，我做的任何事都会变得更美好。她比我更有趣、更爱冒险、也更外向。但更深层的真相是：她的存在，潜移默化地促使我也朝这些特质靠近。

和她在一起时，我变得更开朗；更愿意尝试新鲜事物；也更能活在当下。

换言之，体验之所以不同，并非仅仅因为她在那里——而是因为，在那个体验中，“我”本身已然不同。

请稍作停顿，想一想：在你的生命里，谁让你成为了一个更好的自己？

**共享的时刻，让意义倍增**

随着我的62岁生日日益临近，我愈发珍视经历，远胜于物质。但比这更进一步的是，我逐渐意识到：**当经历被分享，它便被放大。**

试想你独自打高尔夫球：那是一种宁静的享受——你可以自由挥杆、节奏轻快，也不必担心其他球手因各种原因干扰你。

可如果你一杆进洞呢？若无人见证、无人同庆，那份喜悦便难以真正释放。因为像“一杆进洞”这样的成就，唯有在被看见、被分享时，才真正迸发出它本该拥有的光芒。

这一点，我在欧洲与挚友朱德（Jud）和黛博拉（Deborah）共同旅行时深有体会。后来我们再度相聚、追忆往昔，一件奇妙的事便发生了：某个人突然提起一段大家早已淡忘的细节，刹那之间，那段记忆如潮水般涌回所有人脑海。

人越多，视角就越丰富，重温体验的机会也就越多。仿佛那段经历并未随时间消逝，而是在记忆中持续延展、不断重生。

**喜欢——以及被喜欢——的力量**

这直接关联到西奥迪尼（Cialdini）影响力六大原则之一：“喜好”（Liking）。

我们常说，人们更倾向于答应自己熟悉且喜欢的人。这话没错，但背后还有更深层的逻辑：当我们相信对方也喜欢我们时，我们的开放度会更高。

为什么这一点如此关键？因为当我们感到被喜欢、被重视、被联结时，我们会自然放松，敞开心扉，全情投入，从而更真实地做自己——甚至成为比平时更美好的自己。

这将彻底改变我们体验一切的方式。不妨思考一下：

与陌生人共进晚餐 vs. 与至交好友围坐一桌  
独自踏上旅途 vs. 与真正懂你的人结伴同行  
默默达成一项成就 vs. 与所爱之人共同庆祝胜利  

事件本身相同，体验却天差地别。

**朋友改变的不只是你的选择，更是你感知世界的方式**

我们常以为“影响”只是改变他人的决定。但在许多情况下，影响远比这更深沉：你最亲近的人，不仅影响你做什么，更深刻地影响着你如何**看待**、如何**感受**、以及如何**记住**自己的人生。

这种力量强大而深远，也引出一个至关重要的问题：你是否刻意选择与谁共度人生？毕竟，那个“谁”，或许远比那些“什么”更加重要。

**最后一点思考**

我相信，总有一天我会重返拉斯维加斯。而我希望，简能与我同行。因为我深知一点：无论房间多么华美，都远不及与她共享那一刻来得珍贵。

那么你呢？谁让你的经历变得更丰盈？他们又如何悄然重塑你眼中的世界？
