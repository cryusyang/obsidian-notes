<p>Last week I realized that today&#8217;s rapid cultural evolution, mediated greatly by youth movements, seems encouraged by the common modern norm <a href="https://x.com/robinhanson/status/2035831339790782520">favoring</a> &#8220;authenticity&#8221;. Youths ask their hearts how society should change. So I just read two books on the subject, Lionel Trilling (1972) <em><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sincerity_and_Authenticity">Sincerity and Authenticity</a></em>, and Charles Taylor (1991) <em><a href="https://www.hup.harvard.edu/books/9780674987692">Ethics of Authenticity</a></em>. I also read Rousseau (1755) <em><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discourse_on_Inequality">Discourse on Inequality</a></em>, as many call that the first modern advocacy of authenticity.</p><p>Authenticity having your behaviors driven from within you, instead of letting others influence them. Follow your heart, you do you, go with your gut, that sort of thing. It is such a widely accepted norm that the authors who write books on it don&#8217;t actually argue for it much; they instead use it to argue for other stuff. My reading was a waste.</p><p>But, why exactly is authenticity such a good thing? Yes, there&#8217;s this <a href="https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2016/05/scott-alexander-on-robin-hanson.html">quote</a> about me, &#8220;Robin Hanson is more like himself than anybody else I know.&#8221; And, yes, my <a href="http://hanson.gmu.edu/home.html">webpage</a> has long said: &#8220;I&#8217;m not a joiner; I rebel against groups with &#8216;our beliefs&#8217;.&#8221; So as a matter of practice I seem to be authentic. Yet I still don&#8217;t see why it&#8217;s good, per se.</p><p>The modern world changes faster, and gives us more options, which puts a premium on agency; we can&#8217;t just ride along with our slowly changing peasant village anymore. But that means you need make choices, not that they need to come from within.</p><p>We&#8217;ve long taken controlling more as a sign of status, so others controlling you lowers your status. But what would this effect be stronger in the modern world?</p><p>Maybe in the modern world imitation and social pressures have become easier to see. In the old stable peasant village you acted like everyone else, but so did everyone, and you were not noticeably following any particular other visible models. However, in the modern world choices are more varied and contested, and so we can more easily see who in particular is pressuring or influencing who else in particular.</p><p>That wouldn&#8217;t necessarily be bad, except that looking too obviously &#8220;try hard&#8221;, like you are trying to choose actions to impress and please others, shows an unimpressive lack of confidence. Just as the most impressive dancers make their dancing look &#8220;effortless&#8221;, maybe the most impressive social displays are those that seem to come naturally, with little noticeable effort.</p><p>Cultural evolution says that most everything that comes from inside of you was stuff that went there before, from your prior cultural exposures. But seeing you trying to please and conform looks quite different to observers than your seeming to just do stuff from within, even though all of that stuff inside resulted from your prior efforts to please and conform, perhaps as a child. It is like the difference between a dancer who is visibly struggles to do her dance routine, and one for who the routine looks effortless, enjoyable, and even invented on the spot.</p>

## 中文译文

上周我意识到，当今迅速的文化演化——很大程度上由青年运动所推动——似乎受到一种普遍的现代规范的鼓励，即推崇“本真性”（authenticity）。年轻人向自己的内心发问：社会应当如何改变？因此，我刚刚读了两本探讨这一主题的著作：莱昂内尔·特里林（1972）的《诚与真》（Sincerity and Authenticity），以及查尔斯·泰勒（1991）的《本真性的伦理》（Ethics of Authenticity）。我还读了卢梭（1755）的《论人类不平等的起源和基础》（Discourse on Inequality），因为许多人视其为现代首部倡导本真性的著作。

所谓“本真性”，指的是你的行为源自内在驱动，而非受他人影响。听从内心、做真实的自己、凭直觉行事——诸如此类。它已成为一种被广泛接受的规范，以至于专门著书讨论它的作者们，其实并不怎么为其辩护；他们反而以本真性为前提，去论证其他主张。结果，我的阅读可谓白费功夫。

但，本真性究竟好在何处？诚然，有人这样评价我：“罗宾·汉森比我认识的任何人都更像他自己。”而且，我的个人网页长期写着一句话：“我从不随大流；我反抗那些标榜‘我们的信念’的群体。”就实践而言，我似乎的确算得上本真。然而，我仍看不出它本身何以值得称道。

现代社会变化更快，提供更多的选择，因而格外看重人的能动性（agency）；我们再也无法像从前那样，安于缓慢变迁的乡村生活，被动地随波逐流。但这只意味着你必须做出选择，并不意味着这些选择必须源于内在。

长久以来，我们一直把更多掌控权视为地位的标志，因此他人对你的操控会拉低你的地位。但在现代社会中，这种效应为何会更为显著？

或许，在现代社会中，模仿行为与社会压力变得更容易被察觉。在旧日稳定而封闭的农民村落里，你的一举一动都与众人一致，但所有人皆是如此，你并未明显追随任何特定的、可见的榜样。而在现代社会中，选择更加多样，也更具争议性，因此我们更容易看清：究竟是谁，在对谁施加压力或产生影响。

这本身未必是坏事，除非你表现得过于“用力”，比如刻意选择某些行为来取悦或打动他人——那反而暴露出一种令人印象不佳的自信匮乏。正如最出色的舞者会让舞蹈看起来“毫不费力”，最令人信服的社会表现，或许也正是那些看似自然流露、几乎不见刻意努力痕迹的行为。

文化演化理论指出：你内心所呈现的一切，大多早已通过先前的文化接触进入其中。然而，在旁观者眼中，“努力取悦与顺从”与“仿佛纯然出于本心而行动”，二者观感截然不同——尽管你内心所有这些内容，其实都源于你早年（甚至童年时）为取悦与顺从而付出的努力。这就像一位舞者奋力完成动作、处处显露挣扎，与另一位舞者轻松自如、享受其中、甚至仿佛即兴创编出整套动作之间的差别。
