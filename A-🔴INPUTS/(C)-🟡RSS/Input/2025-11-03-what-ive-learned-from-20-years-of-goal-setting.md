									<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">In the summer of 2005, when I was 20 years old, I sat down and wrote one of my first personal goals:</span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">&#8220;Hold a position in the Associated Student Government of Saddleback College at start of Fall semester (August 22, 2005).&#8221;</span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">I had just dropped out of college on the East Coast after burning through all my college savings in one year. I&#8217;d moved back in with my parents in shame and enrolled in a local community college to try and get my life back on track. Writing down this goal was my first tentative attempt at regaining a sense of control and agency.</span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">I can remember it like it was yesterday – how daunting it felt to commit to this goal, despite the fact that the position was uncontested and there were no real barriers to my winning it in the upcoming student government elections!</span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Even such a small, easy commitment required me to confront limiting beliefs: that I wasn&#8217;t good with numbers or with money. Even entertaining such a small amount of ambition meant I had to shift my identity, since I didn&#8217;t remotely see myself as a leader.</span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">I&#8217;m happy to say that I persevered through those fears, took on the position successfully, and it ended up being a very rewarding part of my college career. More importantly, it was the inception of my relationship with goals.</span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Reveling in this first small victory, I soon after decided to write down a series of other goals I had for myself – for the GPA I would attain, for the girlfriend I wanted, and for the 4-year college I wanted to transfer to.</span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">I discovered SMART goals around that time, and decided to attach a target completion date to each of them. Looking back at that list, the furthest date I assigned to my most ambitious goal was in 2025, when I would turn 40 years old. At the fresh-faced age of 20, I couldn&#8217;t fathom being any older than that. It was the farthest my imagination could reach.</span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Now that I&#8217;ve arrived at that seemingly distant horizon, I want to revisit not only that first batch of goals, but all the goals I&#8217;ve formulated for myself in the 20 years since. I&#8217;m less interested in which ones I accomplished or didn&#8217;t, and more interested in what I discovered and who I became by aiming for them in the first place.</span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Let me take you on a retrospective journey through 20 years of goal-setting to find out what I learned.</span></p><h2><span style="font-weight: 400;">Building the foundation of adulthood</span></h2><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Looking at the list of outcomes I said I wanted in the earliest days, I can see that I was struggling and striving to build the foundation of my adult life.</span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Some goals had to do with seemingly small projects, such as creating a website to promote my first business, fixing people&#8217;s home computers:</span></p><p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">&#8220;Build a professional, full-featured website for Forte Computer Solutions by beginning of Spring semester (January 14, 2006).&#8221;</span></i></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">I remember I didn&#8217;t succeed because there were no user-friendly website builders at the time, and I didn&#8217;t have enough motivation to learn HTML. But it was my first personal experience with the challenge of building things with technology, a subject I&#8217;m still grappling with today.</span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Some goals had to do with deep mindset shifts I underwent, such as diving into the world of Robert Kiyosaki&#8217;s book </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Rich Dad, Poor Dad</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">:</span></p><p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">&#8220;Complete Rich Dad’s curriculum: read and take notes on all Rich Dad books, master Cashflow 101 and 202, and read all books recommended by Robert Kiyosaki by end of Spring semester (May 20, 2007).&#8221;</span></i></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">I&#8217;d been raised by artists, and as useful as that was for my creative integrity, it didn&#8217;t give me any of the financial or business skills I needed. I spent over a year completely immersed, reading all Kiyosaki&#8217;s books, and many others he recommended, and even purchasing his financial education board game and playing it with my friends and family. </span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Looking back, I can see how fundamental that experience was for giving me an abundance mentality toward money – that it could be reliably earned, leveraged, multiplied, and compounded, and in a creative and values-aligned way.</span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Other goals seemed quite random and incidental at the time, such as getting my first English teaching job in Curitiba, Brazil, where I was studying abroad:</span></p><p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">&#8220;Get a job teaching English at an established school in Curitiba by end of March 2008.&#8221;</span></i></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Yet I now realize that experience opened up the door to my entire career in education. I see repeated evidence that some of the biggest endeavors in my life started so small.</span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Other projects were clearly massive from the start, and I have no idea what I was thinking taking on something so ambitious:</span></p><p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">&#8220;Write Beyond the Orange Curtain and have it published by New Year (January 1, 2011).&#8221;</span></i></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">That was my </span><a href="https://www.amazon.com/Beyond-Orange-Curtain-Adventures-America-ebook/dp/B07B6VQD8W/ref=sr_1_1?crid=2WBJ8WLDYFVWJ&amp;dib=eyJ2IjoiMSJ9.PAm8gfAt9EAnatrhr5nMhKo4lH7yPJnFHEnI6YXlk43etaaS0JHLS0-YPkd1Wlpa1gly6FZWG3QugG9I1IKEm-_SvzuZYw0Wh4A6mstnu5B7FHG_oPae-DWUwZmJamYRPAzIQRofWlVgeTj3wUETJfh52FsiXqLYP1WwvcS1630nfC6ZrsIMVsBDmHszWuzNk7UYr6C97vW6wygxMw-NgJbrR9Osr0vdG4xAfix3oBY.9vdzY-_ZqY4wsRTOAJzU3bTuhQzmXNHtBkzOff4PdNs&amp;dib_tag=se&amp;keywords=beyond+the+orange+curtain&amp;qid=1761404051&amp;sprefix=beyond+the+orange+curtain%2Caps%2C202&amp;sr=8-1" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="font-weight: 400;">first book</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, and I took every conceivable shortcut I could find to make it real. The chapters of the book were just unedited posts from my travel blog. I used a service called </span><a href="https://www.blurb.com/?gad_source=1&amp;gad_campaignid=21614324841&amp;gbraid=0AAAAAD9CkIUQG1rMzxp4cTPnJbf8zdfOA&amp;gclid=CjwKCAjw6vHHBhBwEiwAq4zvAz93UWnCn9UBoBX-znkjP9D_OvzpHoUSHGZ36oL8D0hyJpPW91PZbxoCbc0QAvD_BwE" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Blurb</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> to design the front and back covers on my computer, and uploaded it to Kindle Direct Publishing. I remember I couldn&#8217;t bring myself to charge anyone for it, so instead I made it a fundraiser and donated all the proceeds to charity.</span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Fast-forwarding a couple of years to the start of my career, my goals started involving higher stakes. I began making real decisions about where my life would lead, with irreversible tradeoffs.</span></p><h2><span style="font-weight: 400;">Kicking off my professional career</span></h2><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">I returned to the U.S. from my service in the Peace Corps in December 2011, and immediately dedicated myself to finding a job in San Francisco:</span></p><p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">&#8220;Get a job in the technology or non-profit sector and move to San Francisco by April 30, 2012.&#8221;</span></i></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">A few months later, I found myself in that city interviewing for the two jobs I&#8217;d received offers for: a junior analyst job at a French consulting firm called Fabernovel, and a role on the fundraising team at the Wikimedia Foundation (which manages Wikipedia, among other projects).</span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">These paths couldn&#8217;t have been more different: the former represented a sharp turn into the world of business and technology, while the latter would have continued my current trajectory deeper into the world of non-profits. But I was already wary of the non-profit world. I&#8217;d spent much of my 20s in it, and seen a lot of dysfunction, politics, and waste. I decided I wanted to try something new and accepted the consulting job.</span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">At that point, I notice a gap in my running list of goals, and I think it was because for the 18 months I worked at the consulting firm, I wasn&#8217;t responsible for choosing my own goals. They were assigned to me, in line with the broader goals of the company. That was useful, to see what it felt like for my personal goals to be subsumed into a wider mission.</span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">I see my own agency emerge again in early 2013, as I started thinking about leaving consulting. I wanted to work on technology more directly, and started planning how I would apply to a tech company like Google, Coursera, Evernote, Udemy, Autodesk, Uber, IDEO, or Tesla.</span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">I had no real technical skills, however, and knew I would need a stellar resume to have any chance. I signed up for a Squarespace account and built my own website, with a portfolio showcasing my credentials and accomplishments:</span></p><p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">&#8220;Publish an online professional portfolio with documented evidence of my accomplishments, in a beautifully designed format by July 1, 2013.&#8221;</span></i></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">That ended up not being nearly enough, and I failed to secure even one interview for any of the roles I applied for at those companies. </span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">A few months later, in June 2013, after yet another long night at the office banging my head on the keyboard and getting nowhere, I decided to call it quits. I was working too hard, for too little pay, and with too little control of my destiny. I would rather take my chances on my own.</span></p><p><div class="convertkit-form wp-block-convertkit-form"></div><!-- notionvc: ab314af8-f681-457a-8aa9-239570dcbe30 --></p><h2><span style="font-weight: 400;">Embarking on the journey of self-employment</span></h2><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">As soon as my two weeks&#8217; notice was up, I turned my portfolio website into a business website and threw myself into trying to make money any way I could think of. </span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">I worked at random events, helped my friends with their projects as a subcontractor, and made my first online course on the GTD productivity method. That course ended up being an unexpected hit, and paid the bills for a while:</span></p><p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">&#8220;Launch GSD.LAB on Skillshare and get 1,000 paying students signed up by Jan. 1, 2014.&#8221;</span></i></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">With a bit of financial breathing room, I had some freedom to experiment, and experiment I did. Looking back, I can see that many of the seeds of my future endeavors were planted around this time as seemingly low-stakes experiments. </span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">I volunteered to give a talk at a local Quantified Self meetup, which introduced me to the subculture of people using technology for personal development in a systematic way:</span></p><p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">&#8220;Create and deliver an inspiring, moving, cutting edge talk on my QS experiments for the QS SV Meetup on Nov. 18, 2013.&#8221;</span></i></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The Quantified Self community ended up being my gateway to an adjacent field – personal knowledge management – that would become my main focus in the ensuing years. That talk actually took place at the Evernote headquarters in Redwood City, foreshadowing my relationship with the platform that would be my tool of choice for building my &#8220;second brain.&#8221;</span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">After the lucky success of my first online course, I found it very difficult to continue making that income stream work sustainably. It was too hard to build the following I knew I needed, while also creating more courses and other products, while also doing everything else involved in running a business. I saw the potential of that career path, but wasn&#8217;t ready for it, so I turned to corporate training at the invitation of a mentor:</span></p><p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">&#8220;Sign a major corporate training contract for productivity/workflow design by Apr. 1, 2014.&#8221;</span></i></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Those workshops were well paid, and gave me a financial lifeline for a couple of years. More importantly, they happened infrequently, giving me lots of free time to learn new skills and pursue new interests.</span></p><h2><span style="font-weight: 400;">Setting goals for my personal development</span></h2><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The life of a freelancer was quite uncertain and stressful, and I turned to meditation around this time as a basic survival measure after seeing an introductory book on it mentioned on an online forum one night:</span></p><p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">&#8220;Establish a daily mindfulness meditation practice by May 31, 2014.&#8221;</span></i></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">That would eventually lead me to join a 10-day silent meditation retreat:</span></p><p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">&#8220;Attend a 10-day meditation retreat by August 31, 2014.&#8221;</span></i></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">That experience was so profound that I felt compelled to write about it, which became my </span><a href="https://fortelabs.com/blog/10-days-of-vipassana/" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="font-weight: 400;">first blog post</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> on a fledgling site called Medium. The meditation retreat was free, requiring only 10 days of my time. And yet it was a seismic shock to my psychology, kicking off two of the most significant themes that continue to define my life to this day: writing in public and my further explorations of personal growth experiences.</span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">My next such exploration was to join a weekend seminar called the </span><a href="https://www.landmarkworldwide.com/the-landmark-forum" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Landmark Forum</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, which I&#8217;d heard several friends talk about:</span></p><p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">&#8220;Attend a Landmark Forum program by Dec. 31, 2016.&#8221;</span></i></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">I can still remember how daunting it felt to spend $700 of my own money on a seminar. And yet again, the return-on-investment was incalculable. I spent two years immersing myself in the world of Landmark as a result, taking a series of their other courses and seminars, culminating in their 7-month leadership program along with my then-girlfriend Lauren.</span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">I see so many examples of how saying &#8220;yes&#8221; to something that seemed inconsequential at the time opened up entire new worlds for me. As another example, I agreed to write a series of 5 guest articles for an obscure blog I followed called Ribbonfarm. That experience was my training for writing long-form thought pieces, which would deepen my writing and thinking, attract my first consistent following, and give me exposure to a vibrant community that was the perfect testing ground for my own emerging ideas about productivity:</span></p><p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">&#8220;Write a 5-post guest series on Ribbonfarm, laying out my vision for productivity and recruiting a smart audience by Jan. 31, 2017.&#8221;</span></i></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">And sometimes personal growth was less about reaching some mountain peak of experience, but about taking the time to engage in non-goal-oriented activities. I&#8217;m certain, for example, that the time I spent sailing was really important for my mental health:</span></p><p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">&#8220;Receive Junior Skipper certification from Cal Sailing Club by August 31, 2015.&#8221;</span></i></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">I turned to sailing in 2015 when I needed an outlet that had nothing to do with my work, gave me exercise and exposure to nature, and helped me make new friends. The Cal Sailing Club operating out of the Berkeley Marina was my lifeline in all those respects, and I&#8217;m so grateful I set aside the time from my professional pursuits to learn to sail there.</span></p><h2><span style="font-weight: 400;">The rise of Building a Second Brain</span></h2><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">As I saw my ideas start to gain traction, an earlier vision returned: of creating an independent career of reading, researching, writing, and teaching, like a freelance professor. </span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">I remember vividly in the fall of 2016 when a new goal emerged along these lines, which was intimidating, but also the perfect synthesis of everything I&#8217;d learned over the previous few years:</span></p><p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">&#8220;Create a new online course on digital organization and creative execution, and deliver it to 50 people at premium prices, by Feb. 28, 2017&#8221;</span></i></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">As I reentered the online course space, I wanted to approach it differently from the &#8220;self-paced&#8221; course model that hadn&#8217;t worked for me previously. It&#8217;s agonizing to see evidence of how long it took me to arrive at that new approach! </span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Like watching the main character in a movie stumble around and miss all the obvious clues, it&#8217;s all so clear with my 20/20 hindsight vision. I wrote vaguely about creating some kind of &#8220;bootcamp,&#8221; which would eventually give rise to the </span><a href="https://fortelabs.com/blog/the-rise-of-cohort-based-courses/" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="font-weight: 400;">cohort-based course model</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> and a whole new category of virtual education.</span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Around that time, I switched from single-sentence SMART goals to more narrative-style visualization that contained more detail and specificity:</span></p><p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">&#8220;I run a regular virtual bootcamp, enrolling my most engaged followers in a high-quality, engaging, accountable learning experience using the latest ideas and tools. I have a system for updating and delivering these bootcamps with minimal recurring effort, dedicating most of my time to providing coaching, feedback, and support for their projects and businesses. This bootcamp helps me test and refine the key components of my new vision for productivity, generating testimonials, case studies, and examples for a book.&#8221;</span></i></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">My first small beta group ended up being only 15 people, mostly friends or acquaintances who agreed to be part of it for free, in exchange for providing their feedback. We met once a week for 4 weeks, and I recall creating the slides for each call immediately before it took place, based on the feedback from the previous one.</span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">That feedback, thankfully, was excellent, and I went on to deliver a series of cohorts to slowly growing numbers of people, at slowly increasing prices. I can remember reaching the following goal, which finally confirmed that this was a sustainable model for me:</span></p><p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">&#8220;Deliver BASB v3 to 80 people, generating $48k in revenue, by August 31, 2018.&#8221;</span></i></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">It&#8217;s astonishing to me to look back on this fledgling start and see what </span><a href="https://www.buildingasecondbrain.com/" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Building a Second Brain</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> has become. In the 7 years since, it&#8217;s served hundreds of thousands of people via an ecosystem of books, courses, a membership, and a sea of content, created both by me and countless others. Yet it all began with a goal written down on paper.</span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">It&#8217;s also amazing to look back on issues that once occupied a lot of bandwidth and stress, such as putting aside money for taxes and making estimated tax payments, which are now completely automated, and I don&#8217;t spend one second thinking about. Other concerns that once seemed unbelievably far in the future, such as saving for retirement, now feel much more relevant since reducing our tax burden has thankfully become our main problem!</span></p><h2><span style="font-weight: 400;">What I learned from unfulfilled or delayed goals</span></h2><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">I’ve recounted a succession of the goals that worked out and led to something greater, but I can also see many, many others that fizzled out, were off the mark to begin with, or would take far longer to come to fruition than I imagined.</span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">In early 2017, I felt a desire to connect with other creators and entrepreneurs, since that lifestyle could be so solitary so much of the time. I had no idea what that could look like concretely, however, and you can see that lack of clarity in how I worded it:</span></p><p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">&#8220;Establish a group of like-minded, ambitious, engaged people for mutual learning, accountability, community, and growth by June 30, 2017.&#8221;</span></i></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">That desire would persist and only grow over the years, before finally manifesting as the </span><a href="https://fortelabs.com/blog/5-surprising-insights-from-my-first-mastermind/" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Wholesome Mastermind</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> I would start 6 years later, in 2023. In retrospect, I can see that I wasn&#8217;t ready to host an event like that one until I&#8217;d reached a certain level of financial stability and gained enough of a reputation that other entrepreneurs would be willing to join.</span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Other goals seemed to take shape quickly, but would involve many iterations before I figured them out. In 2017, I decided to hire someone for the first time, as the BASB cohorts took off and I needed a course manager to handle the logistics:</span></p><p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">&#8220;Establish a solid working relationship with a collaborator, significantly boosting my productivity and profitability by Dec. 31, 2017.&#8221;</span></i></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">I can still remember how scary that was, what an immense responsibility it felt like, and how hesitant and unclear my expectations were. I hired the first person who offered to work with me, without any semblance of a wider search. Eight years later, and after hiring probably 20 people in the interim, I’m finally starting to feel like I’ve achieved a balance of autonomy and accountability with my team.</span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The narrative style of goal-setting that I adopted around this time led to an unexpected side effect: it revealed a lot more detail about my mistaken assumptions around what would bring me happiness and fulfillment. The results are often insightful or funny.</span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">In early 2014, I wrote: </span></p><p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">&#8220;I wake up after a restful night of sleep, which was tracked by QS devices for later analysis. I record my health metrics using a smart scale, activity tracker wristband, and other QS device</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;">s.&#8221;</span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">LOL! At this time, I was neck-deep in the Quantified Self movement and related ideas, and believed that self-optimization was the path to self-fulfillment. If only I could achieve a high enough sleep score, fitness score, productivity score, etc., then surely I&#8217;d be happy!</span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">That optimization mindset extended to my surroundings and the products I bought:</span></p><p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">&#8220;My chair was chosen after in-depth research and perfectly supports my spine. I’ve also studied and practiced the best sitting techniques and ergonomic positioning, and use them.&#8221;</span></i></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Thankfully, not long after this period, I would realize what a dead-end self-optimization becomes after a certain point. I learned to satisfice instead: to allow most parts of life to be relaxed and somewhat &#8220;mediocre&#8221; at any given time, so I could focus my energies on a few things that truly matter.</span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">In 2014, I invested a lot of time and money launching my second online course, on habits, which would end up being a tremendous flop:</span></p><p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">&#8220;I am teaching an online course on behavior design, teaching newcomers how to make a plan for designing and sustaining new habits, and how to execute the plan using the best ideas, methodologies, and tools out there.&#8221;</span></i></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">My choice of topic was spot on, as the meteoric success of </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Atomic Habits</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;"> a few years later would demonstrate. But in retrospect, I can see exactly what didn&#8217;t work: what I was teaching wasn&#8217;t authentic to me. I&#8217;ve never been particularly committed to habits or a routine of any kind. I don&#8217;t even really believe in them, preferring to flex and adapt my choices to how I feel. That tension, between what I was recommending and how I lived, showed up everywhere and doomed the project from the start.</span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">But even that failure taught me a profound lesson: that I shouldn’t chase the trends or try to do what was popular. I have to follow what is authentic to me, what I&#8217;m passionate and inherently curious about, even if it seems to lead somewhere random. I still follow that principle to this day.</span></p><h2><span style="font-weight: 400;">Goals as a means to self-knowledge</span></h2><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">I notice in my narrative goal-setting that I had a lot of beliefs about what my work &#8220;should&#8221; look like, many of which turned out to be misconceptions. </span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">For example, I kept returning to the idea of &#8220;one-on-one coaching,&#8221; since that has always been such a powerful trend in personal development circles in the San Francisco Bay Area. But I would eventually learn that coaching wasn&#8217;t a good fit for me, and I was much better suited to monetizing my knowledge through courses that could serve many people at once.</span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The same is true for public speaking. I wrote:</span></p><p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">&#8220;I speak regularly at events and conferences around the world, using them as platforms to spread my message and impact people directly.&#8221;</span></i></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">And for consulting:</span></p><p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">&#8220;I consult with the world’s most influential organizations, shaping the development of products and services that manifest human-centered design principles and what I’ve learned about human-centered work.&#8221;</span></i></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">I had other misconceptions around what was important in business. I put way too much emphasis on having a slick brand for Forte Labs in the early days, for example, pouring time into logos, business cards, and beautiful slides:</span></p><p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">&#8220;The Forte Labs brand has been completely redesigned and refreshed, presenting a look and feel equal to the world’s most innovative brands. This brand is reflected in the website, all social media properties, and printed materials, providing a consistent and alluring experience with every interaction.&#8221;</span></i></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">With the benefit of hindsight, the brand didn&#8217;t matter at all and would only become important and clear when BASB came about.</span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Many of the goals I set for myself were most useful not because I successfully achieved them, but because I didn&#8217;t. They taught me what I truly wanted and didn&#8217;t want. That self-knowledge and self-awareness is far more valuable than whatever I would have gained by unquestioningly pursuing those goals to their completion.</span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">I had so many fanciful ideas of what I </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">thought</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;"> I wanted, such as &#8220;having breakfast in a super modern, gorgeous kitchen.&#8221; It turns out that acquiring a &#8220;super modern&#8221; kitchen requires renting a super modern apartment, which is very expensive. I found that I much preferred to live in affordable places that didn&#8217;t stress me out when rent was due.</span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Another example is &#8220;frequent business travel,&#8221; which felt so luxurious and prestigious for a short period, until I quickly realized I wanted to avoid it at all costs. </span></p><p><div class="convertkit-form wp-block-convertkit-form"></div><!-- notionvc: ab314af8-f681-457a-8aa9-239570dcbe30 --></p><h2><span style="font-weight: 400;">The runway is longer than you think, but the plane can soar higher than you imagine</span></h2><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Many goals I set for myself seemed so far out of reach, and </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">were</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;"> far out of reach! In my 2015 goal-setting, I dared to write:</span></p><p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">&#8220;I&#8217;m generating $200,000 per year in pre-tax income.&#8221;</span></i></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">This felt like a totally audacious sum, and it would be 4 years until I achieved it in 2019. A couple of years later, as the pandemic fueled online business growth, our personal income would reach 7 figures, which my 2015 self could scarcely have imagined. I would give anything to travel back in time and see the look on his face as I delivered the news.</span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">This is a case of a common phenomenon I can detect in retrospect: that many goals take longer to accomplish than I first imagined, yet once I do, their potential can greatly exceed my limited imagination. In other words, I tend to underestimate the &#8220;runway&#8221; needed for the plane to take off, but once it achieves liftoff, it can soar far higher than I can imagine.</span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Another example of that principle is my book advance. In 2015, I wrote: </span></p><p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">&#8220;I have a book deal with an upfront advance of at least $50,000.&#8221; </span></i></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">You can sense in my language a kind of pleading tone – I really would have been happy with a book deal of any size.</span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">It would, in fact, take me 6 years to receive the first part of that book advance, but when it came, it was $325,000, a sum that exceeded my combined income from my first 5 years of self-employment. Today, a decade after I wrote those words, I&#8217;ve </span><a href="https://fortelabs.com/blog/3-year-update-a-financial-analysis-of-my-books-unit-economics/" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><span style="font-weight: 400;">made $1.85 million from the book</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> and associated product sales, and project that number should reach $6.8 million in earnings over its lifetime. Astonishing.</span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">It&#8217;s amusing to look back on some of my earliest goals, such as to &#8220;reach financial freedom,&#8221; which I defined as having enough passive income to meet my expenses:</span></p><p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">&#8220;Become financially secure (passive income = expenses) by age 33 (May 4, 2018).&#8221;</span></i></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">I understand now that that goal was based on a misconception that work was something I should try to &#8220;escape.&#8221; From the vantage point of today, I wouldn&#8217;t want to stop working even if I had the choice to, as it&#8217;s one of my greatest sources of learning and fulfillment. And I know that the sense of security I was looking for isn&#8217;t found in a certain size bank balance, but in a holistic life of rich relationships, including a healthy relationship with risk and uncertainty.</span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The same is true for personal goals. Almost every year, I wrote down something along these lines:</span></p><p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">&#8220;I perform some type of exercise every day, and vary my exercises to keep me in shape and support overall health.&#8221;</span></i></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Over time, I tested out a variety of theories about health and fitness, such as Paleo-style diets, CrossFit workouts, high-protein breakfast, etc., that were making the rounds at the time. None of them ever really stuck for long.</span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Regular exercise has always been my &#8220;white whale,&#8221; and it is only over a decade later, in 2025, that I can say I&#8217;ve (more or less) formed that habit. I find that I&#8217;m able to do it now because I&#8217;m in a different life stage with different, more aligned motivations: not to get ripped or impress girls, but to avoid aches and pains and be more active with my children.</span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">I see so many examples of just how long it takes many desires and intentions to come to pass. We tend to think in weeks, months, quarters, and, if we are really long-term thinkers, at the scale of a year. But I can&#8217;t help but conclude, as I look back at 20 years of goals, that most of my most meaningful pursuits took years to manifest.</span></p><h2><span style="font-weight: 400;">Goals for the business</span></h2><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">I see many examples in my business-related goals of a variety of theories and hypotheses I was testing at various points.</span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">In 2020, I tried having &#8220;no meeting days&#8221;:</span></p><p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">&#8220;Each day has a strong unifying intention, either free of appointments so I can focus on producing something, or open and spontaneous to make myself available to people who need me.&#8221;</span></i></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">But I would soon discover that a day was too large a block of time, and it fit my energy cycles much better to reserve all mornings free of meetings, and to spend the afternoons on calls.</span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">I can see I consistently underestimated the impact of the economic and cultural environment on my business growth, thinking it all depended only on me instead of trends in online education and the economy:</span></p><p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">&#8220;Forte Labs is growing explosively, not because of what I’m doing or pushing through, but because of who I’m being.&#8221;</span></i></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">I see other examples of my naivete, such as thinking I could &#8220;open source&#8221; all my content and still have a profitable business:</span></p><p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">&#8220;Everything I know is open-sourced and available to help people create more freedom, pleasure, and impact in their work and lives, whether they ever buy from me or not.&#8221;</span></i></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Some goals represented major initiatives that turned out to be completely misguided.</span></p><p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">&#8220;I oversee an online course incubator, which regularly turns out new courses and other offerings that generate income and value for the people that have developed them. I provide every piece of knowledge, service, and counsel the participants need to develop their idea to fruition, and take a percentage while giving them a worldwide sales and marketing platform.&#8221;</span></i></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">We did pursue trying to build a platform and marketplace for multiple courses from other instructors for about a year, but that failed miserably as it wasn&#8217;t in line with our values or capabilities.</span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">I laugh at how unclear my articulation of what I do has been:</span></p><p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">&#8220;Our community is the world’s best source of conversations, curated resources, tutorials, and experiments on the future of work.&#8221;</span></i></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">I don&#8217;t think it&#8217;s that much better even today!</span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">I&#8217;m also reminded, looking back at goals I&#8217;ve set, of just how diverse and wide-ranging my interests are:</span></p><p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">&#8220;I oversee a portfolio of experiments pushing forward the boundaries of my field, such as group knowledge management, design thinking, science fiction prototyping, behavior change, self-tracking, art and music, crowdsourced collaboration, full-stack freelancing, online marketing, project management, community building, creativity, emergence, history, self-awareness, online learning, time-tracking, visual thinking, and writing.&#8221;</span></i></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">This exercise is a reminder to revisit some of those interests that I didn&#8217;t have time to pursue in the past, and perhaps resurrect them in a new form.</span></p><h2><span style="font-weight: 400;">What I value today</span></h2><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Surveying the long arc of my goal-setting history, it&#8217;s clear that getting married and starting a family was the most consequential shift in my life. It changed every aspect of my life, including goal-setting.</span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">One of the earliest goals I wrote down at 20 years old was this one:</span></p><p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">&#8220;Get married to a beautiful, loving, intelligent, spiritual, sensitive woman who will make a great mother, in a wonderful gathering of our families that creates a community around us, by December 31, 2020.&#8221;</span></i></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">That wedding would take place in April 2019, and I couldn&#8217;t even imagine how meaningful, complex, and fulfilling that marriage would be.</span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">I can see Lauren&#8217;s influence on my goal-setting earlier, however, such as in 2016, when I wrote:</span></p><p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">&#8220;I respect the cycles of my body, mind, spirit, family, friends, community, nature, and nation, leaning into each season as an opportunity to bring balance to a new aspect of my life.&#8221;</span></i></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">She taught me balance, and nuance, and how to savor life in the present rather than only living for the future. I would never have written the following intention if it weren’t for her:</span></p><p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">&#8220;I am ambitious but equanimous, driven but tolerant. I don’t go to unnecessary extremes, and accept moderation in most areas of my life at any given time.&#8221;</span></i></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Some of the most meaningful, precious intentions for me these days are not the business milestones, but rather the mundane details of a calm and peaceful life:</span></p><p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">&#8220;We go to bed early, reading and meditating, and fall asleep with a sense of peace and deep gratitude.&#8221;</span></i></p><h2><span style="font-weight: 400;">How I set goals today</span></h2><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Around the birth of our daughter in 2022, I noticed that the list format for my goals that I&#8217;d kept for over 15 years at that point no longer resonated with me.</span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">It&#8217;s because our lives themselves had become non-linear: it was no longer about the steady march of piling one brick on top of another, or winning each leg of a race. We are now a family of 4, with a lifestyle and business that my younger self couldn&#8217;t have imagined. Life has become more about enjoying our days, finding meaning in them, and squeezing the juice out of everything life has to offer.</span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">So I switched to a mindmap format to try and capture the non-linear, exploratory, and serendipitous way I think about goals today. The mindmap has three clear parts, reflecting how clearly I see the three main priorities in my life: Family, Health, and Work. </span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Here&#8217;s the most recent version, as of the end of 2024:</span></p>								</div>

No one understood or could have predicted how much hard work, courage, determination, and persistence I had inside me, least of all myself. I had to find it all inside.</span></p><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">This thought came to mind as I was driving in the car on the way to pick up my kids, shortly after writing a first draft of this article. ## 中文译文

2005年夏天，我20岁那年，坐下来写下了人生中最早的一批个人目标之一：

“在秋季学期开学时（2005年8月22日），担任萨德尔贝克学院学生自治会（Associated Student Government）职务。”

此前，我刚从美国东海岸一所大学退学——仅用一年时间便花光了全部大学储蓄。我满怀羞愧地搬回父母家，并注册了当地一所社区学院，试图让自己的人生重回正轨。写下这一目标，是我第一次小心翼翼地尝试重拾掌控感与主体性。

那一幕至今历历在目：尽管该职位无人竞争、参选几乎毫无障碍，可承诺实现这一目标仍令我倍感沉重！

就连如此微小、轻易的承诺，也迫使我直面那些根深蒂固的限制性信念：比如“我不擅长数字”“我对金钱一窍不通”。哪怕只是萌生这样一点野心，我也必须重塑自我认同——因为那时的我，完全无法将自己视作一名领导者。

值得欣慰的是，我最终克服了这些恐惧，成功就任该职，这段经历也成为我大学生涯中极具价值的一部分。更重要的是，它标志着我与“目标”之间关系的起点。

沉浸于这第一场小小的胜利之中，我很快又列出一系列其他目标：我想达到的绩点（GPA）、想拥有的女友、以及希望转入的四年制大学。

也正是在那个时期，我接触到了SMART目标法，并决定为每个目标设定明确的完成时限。回看当年那份清单，我为最宏大的目标所设定的截止日期是2025年——彼时我将年满40岁。而20岁的我，风华正茂，根本无法想象自己会活到那么老；那已是当时我想象力所能抵达的最远边界。

如今，当我真正抵达这个曾看似遥不可及的地平线，我想重新审视的，不仅是我最初设定的那批目标，还有此后二十年间为自己立下的所有目标。我已不再执着于哪些目标实现了、哪些落空了，而更关注：在最初瞄准它们的过程中，我发现了什么？我又成为了怎样的人？

请允许我带你踏上一场回溯之旅，穿越二十年的目标设定历程，探寻我究竟学到了什么。

**成年生活的基石**

翻阅我最早期所列的目标成果清单，我能清晰看到：那时的我正挣扎着、奋力着，只为构筑起成年生活的根基。

有些目标关乎看似微小的项目，例如创建一个网站来推广我的第一份事业——为他人维修家用电脑：

“于春季学期初（2006年1月14日）前，为‘福尔泰电脑解决方案公司’（Forte Computer Solutions）建成一个专业、功能完备的网站。”

我记得自己并未成功——当时尚无用户友好的建站工具，而我也没有足够动力去学习HTML。但这却是我首次亲身体验“用技术构建事物”的挑战，而这一课题，直至今日我仍在持续探索。

有些目标则关乎深层的心智转变，例如深入研读罗伯特·清崎（Robert Kiyosaki）的《富爸爸穷爸爸》：

“于春季学期末（2007年5月20日）前，完成‘富爸爸’课程体系：通读并整理全部‘富爸爸’系列图书笔记；精通《现金流101》与《现金流202》；并读完罗伯特·清崎推荐的所有书籍。”

我由艺术家父母抚养长大，这种成长背景虽滋养了我的创意真诚，却未能赋予我任何财务或商业技能。我花了整整一年多时间沉浸其中：读遍清崎所有著作及其推荐书目，甚至购入他的财商教育桌游，与亲友一同玩乐实践。

回望这段经历，我愈发意识到它对我意义深远——它为我植入了一种丰盛的金钱观：金钱可以被稳定赚取、杠杆撬动、倍增放大、复利累积，且能以富有创意、契合价值观的方式实现。

另一些目标在当时看来颇为随机、偶然，例如在巴西库里蒂巴（Curitiba）留学期间，获得我的第一份英语教学工作：

“于2008年3月底前，在库里蒂巴一家成熟英语学校获得英语教师职位。”

然而如今我才明白，正是这次经历为我整个教育事业打开了大门。我反复见证到：生命中一些最宏大的征程，往往始于如此微小的契机。

还有一些项目从一开始便显露出庞然之态，而我竟敢接下，实在不知当初是何等心气：

“于新年（2011年1月1日）前完成并出版《超越橙色帷幕》（Beyond the Orange Curtain）。”

那是我的第一本书，我竭尽所能寻找一切捷径使之成真：书中章节直接取自我旅行博客未经编辑的帖子；我用Blurb服务在电脑上自行设计封面与封底；再上传至亚马逊KDP平台。我记得自己甚至不好意思向任何人收费，于是将其设为慈善募捐项目，将全部收入悉数捐出。

时光快进两三年，步入职业生涯初期，我的目标开始承载更高风险。我开始对人生方向作出真实抉择，而这些抉择往往伴随不可逆的权衡取舍。

**开启职业征程**

2011年12月，我结束和平队（Peace Corps）海外服务回到美国，随即全身心投入旧金山求职：

“于2012年4月30日前，在科技或非营利领域找到一份工作，并迁居旧金山。”

几个月后，我已身处这座城市，面试两家机构发来的录用通知：一家是法国咨询公司Fabernovel的初级分析师岗位；另一家则是维基媒体基金会（Wikimedia Foundation，运营维基百科等项目）筹款团队的职位。

这两条路径截然不同：前者意味着果断转向商业与科技世界；后者则延续我既有的轨迹，更深扎进非营利领域。但我对非营利界已心存警惕——整个二十多岁，我大半时间都在其中度过，目睹了太多机能失调、政治倾轧与资源浪费。我决心尝试新路，接受了咨询公司的职位。

此时，我注意到自己持续更新的目标清单出现了一段空白。我想，那是因为我在咨询公司工作的十八个月里，无需自主设定目标——它们均由公司统一分配，与整体战略保持一致。这段经历很有价值：它让我真切体会到，当个人目标被纳入更宏大的使命时，是一种怎样的感受。

我注意到，自己的主体性在2013年初再度浮现——那时我已开始考虑离开咨询行业。我渴望更直接地投身技术领域，于是着手规划如何申请谷歌（Google）、可汗学院（Coursera）、印象笔记（Evernote）、优达学城（Udemy）、欧特克（Autodesk）、优步（Uber）、IDEO或特斯拉（Tesla）等科技公司。

然而，我并无真正的技术功底，深知若想获得一丝机会，简历必须极为出色。于是，我开通Squarespace账户，亲手搭建个人网站，以作品集形式展示自身资质与成就：

“于2013年7月1日前，上线一个设计精美的在线职业作品集，以翔实证据呈现我的各项成就。”

结果证明，这远远不够——我向上述所有公司投递的职位申请，竟连一次面试机会都未获得。

几个月后，2013年6月，又一个漫长夜晚在办公室对着键盘徒劳苦思后，我决定就此止步。我工作太拼命、报酬太微薄、对自己命运的掌控力也太弱。我宁愿孤注一掷，靠自己闯荡。

**踏上自由职业之路**

待两周离职通知期一满，我立刻将作品集网站转型为业务网站，全力以赴尝试一切可能的赚钱方式。

我承接各类零散活动，以分包身份协助朋友推进项目，还制作了首门线上课程——关于GTD（Getting Things Done）高效工作法。这门课意外走红，一度成为我的主要经济来源：

“于2014年1月1日前，在Skillshare平台推出GSD.LAB课程，并吸引1000名付费学员报名。”

手头稍有宽裕后，我获得了实验的自由空间，而我也的确放手尝试。回望过去，我清楚看到：许多未来事业的种子，正是在此刻以看似低风险的实验形式悄然播下。

我志愿为本地“量化自我”（Quantified Self）社群组织的一场聚会做分享，由此结识了一个亚文化群体——他们系统性地运用技术促进个人成长：

“于2013年11月18日‘量化自我硅谷分会’（QS SV Meetup）上，创作并发表一场鼓舞人心、触动心灵、前沿尖锐的演讲，主题为我的量化自我实验。”

“量化自我”社群最终成为我通往邻近领域——个人知识管理（Personal Knowledge Management）——的入口，而该领域随后数年成为我的核心专注方向。那场演讲恰在印象笔记（Evernote）位于红木城的总部举行，冥冥中预示了我与这一平台的深厚渊源——它将成为我构建“第二大脑”的首选工具。

首门线上课程意外走红后，我却发现难以持续维系这一收入流。要建立所需的关注者基础，同时开发更多课程与其他产品，还要兼顾经营企业的全部事务，实在力不从心。我看到了这条职业路径的潜力，但尚未准备好迎接它，于是应一位导师之邀，转而投身企业内训：

“于2014年4月1日前，签署一份关于生产力/工作流设计的重大企业内训合同。”

这些培训报酬丰厚，为我提供了数年的财务生命线。更重要的是，它们频次不高，留给我大量自由时间去学习新技能、探索新兴趣。

**设定个人成长目标**

自由职业者的生活充满不确定性与压力，彼时我转向冥想——只因某夜在某个网络论坛看到一本入门读物的介绍，便将其当作基本的生存手段：

“于2014年5月31日前，确立每日正念冥想习惯。”

这最终引导我参加了一场为期十天的静默冥想闭关：

“于2014年8月31日前，参加一场为期十天的冥想闭关。”

这次体验如此深刻，以至于我迫切想要记录下来，于是写下了我在新兴平台Medium上的第一篇博客文章。这场闭关免费，仅需我付出十天时间；然而，它却在我心理层面引发了一场地震，催生了两个至今仍定义我人生的核心主题：公开写作，以及对个人成长体验的持续探索。

我下一次类似探索，是参加一个名为“地标论坛”（Landmark Forum）的周末研讨会——此前多位朋友都曾提及：

“于2016年12月31日前，参加一场地标论坛项目。”

我至今记得，自掏700美元报名一场研讨会时那种忐忑不安。而回报之丰厚，却难以估量。受此影响，我随后两年沉浸于地标论坛的世界：陆续参加其系列课程与研讨会，最终与当时的女友劳伦（Lauren）一同完成了为期七个月的领导力项目。

我目睹无数例证：当时看似微不足道的一个“同意”，竟为我开启了全新的世界。再举一例：我答应为一家我长期关注的冷门博客Ribbonfarm撰写五篇客座文章。这次经历成为我撰写长篇思想评论的训练场，深化了我的写作与思考能力，吸引了我第一批稳定的读者群，并让我接触到一个充满活力的社群——那里恰是我新兴生产力理念最理想的试验田：

“于2017年1月31日前，在Ribbonfarm完成五篇客座文章系列，阐明我的生产力愿景，并借此招募一批聪慧的受众。”

有时，个人成长并非指向某种巅峰体验，而是关乎腾出时间，从事非目标导向的活动。例如，我确信自己投入的帆船时光，对心理健康至关重要：

“于2015年8月31日前，获得加州帆船俱乐部（Cal Sailing Club）颁发的初级船长认证。”

2015年，当我急需一个与工作完全无关的出口时，我选择了帆船运动——它既能锻炼身体、亲近自然，又能助我结交新友。位于伯克利码头（Berkeley Marina）的加州帆船俱乐部，在所有这些方面都成了我的生命线。我无比感激自己当年能从职业追求中抽身，专程前往那里学习帆船。

**“构建第二大脑”的崛起**

当我看到自己的想法逐渐获得关注，一个更早的愿景随之回归：打造一种独立的职业形态——像一名自由学者那样，专注于阅读、研究、写作与教学。

我记得2016年秋，一个与此相关的新目标鲜明浮现——它令人敬畏，却也完美融合了此前数年我所习得的一切：

“于2017年2月28日前，开发一门关于数字信息整理与创意执行的全新线上课程，并以高单价面向50人交付。”

当我重返线上课程领域，我希望摒弃此前未能奏效的“自主学习”（self-paced）模式。回看那段历程，我耗时之久才摸索出新路径，实在令人扼腕！

就像观看电影主角在迷宫中跌跌撞撞、屡屡错过所有明显线索，如今以全知视角回望，一切早已清晰可见。我当时含糊地提到要打造某种“训练营”（bootcamp），而这最终催生了“小组制课程”（cohort-based course）模式，乃至整个虚拟教育的新类别。

大约就在那时，我将原先单句式的SMART目标，转向更具叙事性的可视化描述——内容更丰富、细节更具体：

“我定期举办线上训练营，邀请我最活跃的追随者参与其中，提供高质量、引人入胜、强调责任的学习体验，融入最新理念与工具。我已建立一套系统，可最小化重复性投入，高效更新与交付训练营内容，从而将大部分精力用于为学员的项目与事业提供教练指导、反馈支持与个性化帮助。该训练营帮助我测试并精炼新生产力愿景的核心组件，生成客户证言、案例研究与实际范例，为著书立说积累素材。”

我的首个小型测试小组仅有15人，大多是朋友或熟人，他们免费加入，仅以提供反馈为交换。我们每周线上聚会一次，共持续四周；我至今记得，每次会议前，我都是根据上一次的反馈即时制作幻灯片。

幸运的是，这些反馈极为宝贵。此后，我陆续推出了多期训练营，每期人数稳步增长，定价亦逐步提高。我记得达成以下目标时，终于确认：这一模式于我而言确具可持续性：

“于2018年8月31日前，向80人交付‘构建第二大脑’第三版（BASB v3），创收4.8万美元。”

回望这一稚嫩起步，再看今日“构建第二大脑”（Building a Second Brain）已发展为何等规模，实在令人惊叹。七年来，它已通过书籍、课程、会员计划及海量内容（由我及无数他人共同创作）构成的生态系统，服务了数十万用户。而一切，皆始于纸上写下的一行目标。

同样令人惊讶的是，回望那些曾占据大量心力与焦虑的事项——例如为缴税预留资金、提交预估税款——如今已完全自动化，我甚至无需为此花费一秒心神。另一些曾看似遥不可及的忧虑，如退休储蓄，如今也变得切近起来——毕竟，如今我们的主要难题，反而是如何合理降低税负！

**从未实现或延迟达成的目标中学到什么**

我已细述了一系列顺利达成、并催生更大格局的目标，但我也清晰看到：还有众多目标或中途熄火、或初始便偏离靶心、或远比预期耗时漫长才得以实现。

2017年初，我渴望联结其他创作者与创业者——毕竟这种生活方式常常异常孤独。然而，我对此尚无任何具体构想，措辞中便流露出这份模糊：

“于2017年6月30日前，组建一个志同道合、雄心勃勃、积极投入的群体，以实现彼此学习、相互督促、社群联结与共同成长。”

这一渴望持续多年，且日益强烈，最终于六年后（2023年）落地为我创立的“全然大师圈”（Wholesome Mastermind）。事后反思，我意识到：直到自己具备一定财务稳定性、并建立起足够声誉，使其他创业者愿意加入，我才真正准备好主持此类活动。

另一些目标看似迅速成型，却需历经多次迭代方能厘清。2017年，“构建第二大脑”训练营蓬勃发展，我需要专人处理课程后勤，于是决定首次聘请员工：

“于2017年12月31日前，与一位合作者建立稳固的工作关系，显著提升我的工作效率与盈利能力。”

我至今记得那份恐惧感、那份沉甸甸的责任感，以及我自身期望的模糊与犹豫。我仓促聘用了第一个主动提出合作的人，全无广泛筛选的意识。八年后，历经中间约二十次雇佣，我终于开始感受到：在团队中实现了自主性与责任感的平衡。

彼时我采用的叙事式目标设定法，带来一个意想不到的副产品：它更清晰地揭示了我诸多错误假设——那些我以为能带来幸福与满足的事物。结果常令人莞尔或发人深省。

2014年初，我写道：

“我在一夜安眠后醒来，睡眠质量已由量化自我设备追踪，供后续分析。我使用智能体重秤、活动追踪手环及其他QS设备，记录健康数据。”

哈哈！彼时我深陷“量化自我”运动及相关思潮，笃信自我优化即通往自我实现之路。只要我的睡眠得分、体能得分、效率得分等指标足够高，幸福必然水到渠成！

这种优化心态甚至延伸至我的周遭环境与所购产品：

“我的椅子经深度调研后选定，完美支撑脊柱。我还深入研究并实践了最佳坐姿与人体工学定位，并坚持应用。”

幸而不久之后，我便意识到：自我优化行至某处，终成死胡同。我学会了“满意解”（satisfice）之道：允许生活中大部分领域保持松弛、甚至略显“平庸”，以便将有限精力聚焦于少数真正重要的事情。

2014年，我投入大量时间与金钱推出第二门线上课程——关于习惯养成——结果却惨遭滑铁卢：

“我正在讲授一门关于行为设计的线上课程，指导新手制定习惯养成与维持计划，并借助当下最前沿的理念、方法与工具执行该计划。”

选题本身精准无误——几年后《原子习惯》（Atomic Habits）现象级的成功便印证了这一点。但事后反思，我清楚看到失败根源：我所传授的内容并非源于真实自我。我本人从未真正践行习惯或任何固定日程；我甚至不太相信其效力，更倾向依据自身感受灵活调整选择。这种推荐内容与生活实践之间的张力，处处显现，从一开始就注定了项目的失败。

但即便这次失败，也教会我一个深刻教训：我不该追逐潮流，或盲目模仿热门趋势。我必须追随内心的真实，追随我真正热爱、天生好奇的事物——哪怕它看似漫无目的。直至今日，我依然恪守这一原则。

**目标作为自我认知的途径**

我注意到，在叙事式目标设定中，我曾抱有许多关于工作“应有模样”的信念，其中不少后来被证明是误解。

例如，“一对一教练辅导”这一概念反复出现——毕竟，在旧金山湾区的个人成长圈中，它始终是一股强劲潮流。但我最终领悟：教练辅导并不适合我；我更适合通过课程形式，将知识规模化地传递给众人。

公开演讲亦是如此。我曾写道：

“我定期在全球各地的活动与会议上发表演讲，以此为平台传播理念、直接影响他人。”

咨询工作亦然：

“我为全球最具影响力的组织提供咨询服务，塑造体现以人为本设计理念的产品与服务，融入我所领悟的人本工作智慧。”

我对商业中何为重要，亦存诸多误解。例如，早期我过度强调“福尔泰实验室”（Forte Labs）品牌调性的精致感，耗费大量时间打磨Logo、名片与精美幻灯片：

“福尔泰实验室品牌已完成全面重塑与焕新，视觉形象堪比全球最具创新力的品牌。该品牌形象统一呈现在官网、所有社交媒体平台及印刷物料中，确保每一次互动都传递一致而迷人的体验。”

得益于后见之明，我明白品牌在当时毫无意义；它唯有在“构建第二大脑”问世后，才真正变得重要且轮廓清晰。

我为自己设定的许多目标，其最大价值并非源于成功达成，而恰恰在于未能实现。它们教会我真正渴望与排斥之物。这种自我认知与觉察，远比盲目追逐目标直至终点所能获得的任何东西都更为珍贵。

我曾有过许多浮华幻想，例如“在超现代、极美的厨房里享用早餐”。事实证明，拥有“超现代”厨房意味着租住超现代公寓——而那租金高昂得令人咋舌。我最终发现，自己更偏爱居住在经济实惠、不会在房租到期日令我焦虑的住所。

另一个例子是“频繁商务差旅”——短暂时期内，它曾让我感到奢华与尊贵，但很快我便意识到：自己恨不得不惜一切代价避开它。

**跑道比你想象的更长，但飞机升空后能飞得比你想象的更高**

许多我设定的目标，看似遥不可及，而事实上，它们确实遥不可及！2015年设定目标时，我大胆写下：

“我年税前收入达20万美元。”

这数字当时听来简直狂妄，而我直到2019年才真正实现——整整四年之后。又过两年，疫情推动线上业务爆发式增长，我们家庭年收入突破七位数，这在2015年的我看来，简直是天方夜谭。若能穿越回那时，亲口告诉年轻的自己这个消息，我愿付出一切代价去看他脸上的表情。

这正印证了一种我事后可清晰辨识的普遍现象：许多目标的实现周期远超我最初的预估；而一旦达成，其潜在价值却常远超我狭隘的想象。换言之，我倾向于低估飞机起飞所需的“跑道长度”，但一旦腾空，它所能攀升的高度，却远超我所能企及的想象极限。

这一原则的另一例证是我的图书预付金。2015年，我写道：

“我已与出版社签约，预付金至少5万美元。”

字里行间透出一种恳求的语气——当时，任何规模的图书合约都足以令我欣喜若狂。

事实上，我足足等待六年才收到这笔预付金的第一部分；而当它真正到来时，金额高达32.5万美元，远超我前五年自由职业总收入之和。十年后的今天，这本书及关联产品的销售收入已达185万美元，预计其生命周期总收益将达680万美元。令人惊叹。

回望我最早期的一些目标，例如“实现财务自由”——我将其定义为被动收入足以覆盖全部开支：

“于33岁（2018年5月4日）前实现财务安全（被动收入 = 开支）。”

如今我明白，这一目标基于一个错误前提：即工作是亟待“逃离”的负担。站在今日的视角回望，即便拥有选择权，我也不愿停止工作——因为工作正是我学习与获得满足感的最重要源泉之一。我也深知，我曾苦苦追寻的安全感，并非来自银行账户的特定数字，而源于一种整体丰盈的人生：拥有深厚的人际关系，包括与风险和不确定性健康共处的能力。

个人目标亦然。几乎每年，我都会写下类似这样的句子：

“我每天进行某种形式的锻炼，并不断变换锻炼方式，以保持体魄强健、支持整体健康。”

多年来，我尝试过当时流行的多种健康健身理论：原始人饮食法（Paleo）、CrossFit训练、高蛋白早餐等等。但没有一种能长久坚持下来。

规律锻炼始终是我心中的“白鲸”，而直到十多年后的2025年，我才（基本）养成了这一习惯。我发现，如今之所以能够做到，是因为我已处于不同的人生阶段，拥有更契合、更内在的动机：不再是为了练出肌肉或取悦异性，而是为了远离酸痛、更活跃地陪伴孩子。

我看到太多例证，印证着许多愿望与意图的实现，需要漫长得惊人的周期。我们习惯以周、月、季度为单位思考，若算得长远，顶多延展至一年。但回望二十年的目标历程，我不得不承认：我最有意义的追求，大多需要数年光阴才能开花结果。

**关于企业的目标**

我的企业相关目标中，亦不乏各种理论与假说的试错痕迹。

2020年，我尝试推行“无会议日”：

“每一天都拥有明确统一的主题：要么完全不安排会议，以便专注产出；要么保持开放与自发，随时响应他人需求。”

但很快我发现，以“一天”为单位的时间块过大；更契合我精力节律的做法，是将所有上午时段留空，下午则集中处理电话与会议。

我亦能清晰看到，自己曾一贯低估经济与文化环境对企业增长的影响，误以为一切全凭一己之力，而非取决于在线教育与宏观经济的趋势：

“福尔泰实验室正迅猛扩张，并非因为我做了什么或强行推动，而在于我‘成为’了怎样的人。”

我还能看到其他天真之处，例如曾以为可以将所有内容“开源”，同时维持盈利性业务：

“我所知的一切均已开源，无偿帮助人们在其工作与生活中创造更多自由、愉悦与影响力——无论他们是否向我购买任何产品。”

一些目标代表了重大举措，最终却被证明完全误入歧途：

“我负责运营一个线上课程孵化器，定期孵化新课程及其他产品，为开发者创造收入与价值。我为其提供从创意构思到最终落地所需的全部知识、服务与指导，并从中抽取分成，同时为其提供覆盖全球的销售与营销平台。”

我们确实曾尝试构建一个容纳多位讲师课程的平台与市场，持续约一年，但最终惨败收场——因为它与我们的价值观及能力根本不相匹配。

我甚至笑叹自己对自身工作的表述，曾是多么模糊不清：

“我们的社群，是全球探讨工作未来这一议题的最佳对话场域，汇聚了精心甄选的资源、教程与实验。”

我想，即使到了今天，这个表述也没好多少！

回望自己设定的目标，我也再次意识到：我的兴趣多么多元、视野多么广阔：

“我统筹管理一系列实验项目，持续拓展本领域的边界，例如：群体知识管理、设计思维、科幻原型设计、行为改变、自我追踪、艺术与音乐、众包协作、全栈式自由职业、在线营销、项目管理、社群建设、创造力、涌现现象、历史、自我觉察、在线学习、时间追踪、视觉化思考及写作。”

这项回顾提醒我：是时候重新拾起那些过去因时间所限未能深入探索的兴趣，并或许以崭新形态将其复活。

**我今日所珍视之物**

纵观我长达二十年的目标设定史，婚姻与组建家庭无疑是人生中最重大的转折。它彻底改变了我生命的方方面面，包括目标设定本身。

早在20岁时，我写下的最早目标之一便是：

“于2020年12月31日前，迎娶一位美丽、深情、聪慧、灵性、敏感的女性，她将成为一位卓越的母亲；婚礼将在我们双方家族欢聚的美好时刻举行，为我们凝聚起一个温暖社群。”

这场婚礼最终于2019年4月举行，而我根本无法想象，这段婚姻竟会如此意义深远、复杂丰饶、充满满足。

不过，早在2016年，劳伦（Lauren）对我的目标设定便已产生影响，例如我曾写道：

“我尊重身体、心智、精神、家庭、朋友、社群、自然与国家的运行周期，将每个季节视为平衡生命新维度的契机。”
