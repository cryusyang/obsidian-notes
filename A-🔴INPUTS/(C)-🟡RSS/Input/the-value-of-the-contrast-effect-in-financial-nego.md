In financial [negotiations](https://www.pon.harvard.edu/daily/negotiation-skills-daily/what-is-negotiation/), it may seem obvious that having your offer accepted is always preferable to having it rejected. Yet acceptance isn’t the only path to agreement. In fact, a rejection can sometimes be the catalyst that moves both parties closer to “yes.” When a counterpart turns down your proposal, it can prompt you to gather more information, rethink your assumptions, and refine your offer in ways that ultimately lead to a stronger, more mutually beneficial deal. Rather than viewing rejection as a setback, skilled negotiators recognize it as an opportunity to learn, adapt, and move the conversation forward.

在金融[谈判](https://www.pon.harvard.edu/daily/negotiation-skills-daily/what-is-negotiation/)中，你的报价被接受似乎显然总比被拒绝更可取。然而，达成协议的路径并不仅限于接受。事实上，拒绝有时恰恰是推动双方更接近“同意”的催化剂。当对方拒绝你的提议时，这反而可能促使你收集更多信息、重新审视原有假设，并以最终促成更有力、更具互惠性的协议的方式优化你的报价。高明的谈判者不会将拒绝视为挫折，而是视其为学习、调整并推动对话向前发展的机会。

Here’s a story about consumer behavior in financial negotiations, as described by Itamar Simonson of Stanford’s Graduate School of Business and the late Amos Tversky in a pivotal 1992 article in the *Journal of Marketing Research.*

这是斯坦福大学商学院伊塔马尔·西蒙松（Itamar Simonson）与已故心理学家阿莫斯·特沃斯基（Amos Tversky）在1992年发表于《营销研究杂志》（*Journal of Marketing Research*）上的一篇开创性文章中所描述的、关于金融谈判中消费者行为的一个案例。

Upscale retailer Williams-Sonoma was selling a bread-making machine priced at $275. Eventually, the company also began selling a similar but larger bread-making machine, this one priced at $429.

高端零售商威廉姆斯-索诺玛（Williams-Sonoma）当时正以275美元的价格销售一款面包机。后来，该公司又推出了一款外观相似但体积更大的面包机，售价为429美元。

Williams-Sonoma sold few units of the more expensive machine, but after it was on the market, sales of the less-expensive machine almost doubled. Apparently, the $275 model didn’t seem like a bargain until it was sitting next to the $429 model.

威廉姆斯-索诺玛高价面包机销量寥寥，但该机型上市后，低价款（275美元）的销量却几乎翻倍。显然，只有当275美元的型号与429美元的型号并排陈列时，消费者才真正意识到前者是一笔划算的买卖。

Translated to the context of financial [negotiations](https://www.pon.harvard.edu/daily/negotiation-skills-daily/how-power-affects-negotiators/), the *contrast effect* suggests a strategic move: ask for more than you realistically expect, accept rejection, and then shade your offer downward. Your counterpart in the financial negotiations is likely to find a reasonable offer even more appealing after rejecting an offer that’s out of the question.

将这一现象迁移到金融[谈判](https://www.pon.harvard.edu/daily/negotiation-skills-daily/how-power-affects-negotiators/)语境中，“对比效应”（*contrast effect*）提示了一种策略性操作：先提出一个远超你实际预期的要价，坦然接受拒绝，再顺势调低报价。在金融谈判中，当对方刚刚拒绝了一个明显不切实际的报价后，往往会对一个合理得多的后续报价产生更强的认同感。

Real-estate agents sometimes play on this tendency by taking buyers to see overpriced, run-down homes before showing them ordinary homes that appear stunning by comparison, writes psychologist Robert Cialdini of Arizona State University.

亚利桑那州立大学心理学家罗伯特·西奥迪尼（Robert Cialdini）指出，房地产中介有时便利用这种心理倾向：先带买家去看一些价格虚高、破旧不堪的房屋，再展示普通住宅——后者在对比之下便显得格外惊艳。

Try putting forth several equivalent offers that aim higher than your counterpart is likely to [accept](https://www.pon.harvard.edu/daily/salary-negotiations/compensation-negotiation-tips-for-salary-bargaining/). His reaction to the offers will help you frame a subsequent set that, thanks in part to the contrast effect, are more likely to hit the mark.

不妨同时提出数个等效但要价更高的方案，其幅度超出对方可能[接受](https://www.pon.harvard.edu/daily/salary-negotiations/compensation-negotiation-tips-for-salary-bargaining/)的范围。对方对这些方案的反应，将帮助你设计出下一组报价——借助对比效应的部分作用，这组新报价更有可能命中目标。
