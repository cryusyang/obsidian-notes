<b>ALEXANDER KARP AND NICHOLAS ZAMISKA'S</b> The Technological Republic: Hard Power, Soft Belief, and the Future of the West may not be the worst book I have read this year, but it is by far the most disappointing. Karp is the rare CEO more famous for his intellect than his entrepreneurship. The overlap between the students of Jürgen Habermas and captains of industry is small. Among the technology brethren, Karp is regularly portrayed as a latter-day philosopher king. Karp leans into this image. I do not begrudge him this—founders must sell both themselves and their companies, and a company like Palantir is easier to sell when its founder is wreathed in mystique.

There are some downsides to mystique. If people believe you are some philosophic savant, they expect you to write a book with real intellectual heft. An important book. The sort that teaches men how to merge principle with practice. The sort of book that might be remembered.

## 中文译文

亚历山大·卡普与尼古拉斯·扎米斯卡所著  
《技术共和国：硬实力、软信念与西方的未来》或许并非我今年读过的最差的一本书，但无疑是最令人失望的一本。卡普是一位罕见的首席执行官——其声名更多源于其思想深度，而非创业成就。哈贝马斯的学生与商界领袖之间的交集本就稀少；而在科技圈内，卡普更常被描绘为一位当代“哲人王”。卡普本人也乐于强化这一形象。对此我并无异议——创业者既需推销自身，亦需推销公司；而当一家公司的创始人笼罩在神秘光环之中时，像帕兰提尔这样的企业自然更易获得认可。

然而，神秘感亦有其弊端。倘若人们将你视作一位哲思卓绝的智者，他们便会期待你写出一部真正具有思想分量的重要著作——一部能教导世人如何将原则与实践相融合的著作，一部有望被后世铭记的著作。
