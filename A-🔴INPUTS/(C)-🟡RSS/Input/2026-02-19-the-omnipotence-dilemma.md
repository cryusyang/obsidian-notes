> 文章提出“全能困境”（Omnipotence Dilemma）：AI工具大幅降低行动门槛，使人能轻松启动无数项目，却反而导致难以专注、完成度低、创作感弱化。作者指出，零成本启动消解了选择的严肃性，创作退化为选项筛选，“自动补全文化”削弱了作者性与身份认同。过去因时间、能力与资源稀缺而被迫做出的优先级选择，曾帮助个体形成清晰方向与自我定位；而如今稀缺性的消失，正引发“高产出潜力”与“低实质进展”的危险循环。

<h3 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>When being able to do everything prevents you from achieving anything</strong></h3>



<p>This morning, I sat in front of my laptop and asked myself a simple question: what should I work on today?</p>



<p>Looking at my projects in progress, I have three literature reviews I find interesting, an app idea that could be useful for the Ness Labs community, and a dashboard I’ve started building for my team.</p>



<p>With AI tools like Claude Cowork and OpenClaw, each one feels only a few prompts away from a solid first draft: research outlines appear instantly, interfaces prototype themselves, and code that once required weeks is now written in minutes.</p>



<p>Technically, I can do almost anything. Practically, I’ve noticed a worrying shift: I keep starting new things and finishing very few. It feels like being surrounded by infinite drafts.</p>



<p>What should you work on when everything is technically possible? This question is what I call the Omnipotence Dilemma.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Power without progress</h2>



<p>We’ve reached a point where, for the first time in humanity’s history, virtually anyone can work on anything. You have a team of infatigable skilled workers ready to do research, build apps, send emails, and answer all your questions along the way.</p>



<p>We tend to think more capability automatically leads to more progress, so that sounds great in theory. But what I’m noticing is that when the cost of taking action approaches zero, a strange dynamic seems to appear.</p>



<p>First, when starting something no longer requires much sacrifice, it stops functioning as a decision. You don’t choose one path instead of another – you can begin several at once, and nothing forces prioritization anymore. You can rapidly prototype ten versions of something without ever deciding what it is actually for.</p>



<p>And when a draft, plan, or prototype can be generated instantly, creation starts to feel like selection rather than authorship. This is the rise of what’s been called <a href="https://www.fastcompany.com/90126569/the-rise-of-autocomplete-culture">autocomplete culture</a>: instead of wrestling with an idea, you refine options already proposed to you. Yes, you can generate anything, yet less of it feels fully yours.</p>



<p>Limited time, skills, and resources once forced us to choose, and those choices accumulated into a direction. In my first year as a neuroscience student, I also took web development classes. I loved them, and I’m still glad I learned the basics which are useful to me every day. But after a few months, I had to decide where most of my time and attention would go.</p>



<p>This didn’t mean choosing just one thing (impossible for a hypercurious mind!), but it did mean prioritizing. I decided that I’d spend ~60% of my time on neuroscience, 20% on writing, and the remaining time on learning other skills (including webdev) as needed. And in that way, scarcity helped shape my identity. Today, that sense of scarcity is gone.</p>



<p>Taken together, these dynamics form a dangerous loop:</p>

</div>


<p><strong>1. Synthetic plausibility.</strong> AI can construct convincing plans for nearly anything. When almost every idea sounds viable and every path appears reasonable, prioritization becomes harder.</p>



<p><strong>2. Cheap starts.</strong> Beginning something used to require at least some commitment, even if just in the form of time investment. But starting has become effortless – just one prompt and you’re on your way – and the stakes are so low that it’s almost a form of consumption.</p>



<p><strong>3. Endless iteration.</strong> Projects are endlessly refined, abandoned or replaced, creating the sensation of progress even when nothing meaningful moves forward.</p>



<p>The deeper cost is that you outsource the hardest but most important part of creative work: forming a view. What becomes scarce is no longer skill or access. What becomes scarce is attention, conviction, taste, trust, time, and responsibility.</p>



<p>And what worries me the most is that this cost shows up slowly. Projects that don’t quite make sense. A subtle loss of our ability for strategic thinking and meaning-making. Being unable to articulate why in the first place you’re doing this work.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Sandboxing your curiosity</h2>



<p>Instead of thinking like a maximizer trying to pursue every promising direction, it helps to think like a scientist running <a href="https://nesslabs.com/personal-experiments">experiments</a>.</p>



<p>An experiment has a clear scope. It has a duration. It produces learning whether it succeeds or fails.</p>



<p>It allows you to shift from an escapist mindset to an experimental mindset by choosing projects at the intersection of curiosity and ambition – work that genuinely interests you while also stretching your capabilities and contributing something meaningful.</p>


<div class="wp-block-image">
<figure class="aligncenter size-large"><img alt="Solving the Omnipotence Dilemma with an Experimental Mindset - Ness Labs" class="wp-image-17901" height="720" src="https://nesslabs.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/experimentalist-mindset-matrix.png" width="1280" /></figure>
</div>


<p>Then, to apply that experimental mindset, turn your area of curiosity into a mini-protocol using this format:</p>



<blockquote class="wp-block-quote is-layout-flow wp-block-quote-is-layout-flow">
<p>I will [action] for [duration].</p>
</blockquote>



<p>Whether that’s a two-week prototype, a month of writing, ten published essays around a topic you truly care about, this kind of tiny experiment provides a clear sandbox for you to explore and actually learn and grow.</p>



<p>At the end of your experiment, <a href="https://nesslabs.com/plus-minus-next">take time to reflect</a>. What worked? What didn’t? Based on what you learned, what would you like to experiment with next? This allows you to replace the dangerous AI slop loop I described earlier with a <a href="https://nesslabs.com/metacognition">metacognitive loop</a>, where each iteration is an opportunity for self-discovery.</p>



<p>In an age of near-omnipotence, <a href="https://nesslabs.com/science-of-curiosity">curiosity</a> has never been easier to satisfy. And yet, meaningful progress still requires committing to your curiosity – not by pursuing everything at once, but by choosing an interesting path to explore deeply enough for something meaningful to emerge.</p>

## 中文译文

当无所不能反而让你一事无成  

今天早晨，我坐在笔记本电脑前，问了自己一个简单的问题：今天该做什么？  

浏览我正在推进的项目，眼前有三篇我颇感兴趣的文献综述、一个或许对Ness Labs社区有用的应用构想，以及一个我已着手为团队搭建的数据看板。  

借助Claude Cowork、OpenClaw等AI工具，每个项目似乎只需几个提示词就能产出扎实的初稿：研究提纲瞬间生成，界面原型自动浮现，而过去需要数周编写的代码，如今几分钟即可完成。  

从技术上讲，我几乎可以做任何事；但现实中，我却察觉到一种令人担忧的变化：我不断开启新项目，却极少真正完成任何一个。这种状态，仿佛被无穷无尽的未完成草稿所包围。  

当一切在技术上皆有可能时，你究竟该专注于什么？这个问题，我称之为“全能困境”（Omnipotence Dilemma）。  

拥有力量，却不带来进展  

人类历史上首次，几乎任何人都能投身于任何领域。你拥有一支不知疲倦、技艺娴熟的“虚拟团队”，随时准备为你开展研究、开发应用、发送邮件，并在每一步为你答疑解惑。  

我们往往认为，能力越强，自然进展越多——理论上这听起来无比美好。但我观察到的是：当行动成本趋近于零时，一种奇特的动态悄然浮现。  

首先，当启动某件事不再需要多少付出，它便不再构成真正的“选择”。你不必在不同路径之间做出取舍——你可以同时开启多个项目，再没有任何外力迫使你优先处理某一项。你甚至能快速生成十个版本的原型，却始终不明确它究竟为何而存在。  

其次，当一份草稿、一个计划或一个原型都能即时生成，创作便渐渐蜕变为“筛选”，而非“创造”。这正是所谓“自动补全文化”（autocomplete culture）的兴起：你不再与一个想法艰苦角力，而是反复打磨他人已为你预设的选项。是的，你能生成一切，但其中越来越少的部分真正属于你。  

过去，有限的时间、技能与资源迫使我们做出选择，而这些选择日积月累，最终塑造出我们的方向。在我神经科学专业的第一年，我也选修了网页开发课程。我很喜欢，至今仍庆幸掌握了这些基础知识，它们每天都在为我所用。但几个月后，我不得不决定：我的主要精力与注意力应投向何处？  

这并非意味着只专注一件事（对一个极度好奇的大脑而言，这根本不可能！），但它的确意味着必须有所侧重。我决定将约60%的时间投入神经科学，20%用于写作，其余时间则按需学习其他技能（包括网页开发）。正因如此，“稀缺性”曾帮助我塑造自我认同。而如今，这种稀缺感已然消失。  

综上所述，这些动态共同构成了一种危险的循环：  

1. **合成式可行性**（Synthetic plausibility）：AI几乎能为任何构想构建出极具说服力的执行方案。当几乎所有点子都听上去可行，每条路径都显得合理，优先排序便愈发困难。  

2. **廉价的启动**（Cheap starts）：过去，开启一件事至少需要某种承诺——哪怕仅是投入时间。而如今，启动变得毫不费力：只需一条提示，旅程便已启程；风险又如此之低，以至于它近乎一种“消费行为”。  

3. **无限迭代**（Endless iteration）：项目被持续优化、随意放弃或轻易替换，制造出一种“正在前进”的幻觉，实则并无任何实质进展。  

更深层的代价在于：你将创造性工作中最艰难却也最重要的部分——形成自己的观点——外包了出去。如今真正稀缺的，已不再是技能或资源获取渠道，而是注意力、信念、品位、信任、时间与责任。  

最令我忧虑的是，这种代价悄无声息地累积：那些逻辑略显牵强的项目；我们战略思维与意义建构能力的微妙退化；甚至无法清晰说出——自己最初为何要从事这项工作。  

为好奇心划定实验边界  

与其像一个“最大化者”（maximizer）那样，试图追逐每一个看似有潜力的方向，不如以科学家的姿态去设计实验。  

一项实验具有明确的范围、固定的周期，并且无论成败，都能带来切实的学习收获。  

它能助你从逃避现实的心态转向实验探索的心态——即主动挑选那些处于“好奇心”与“抱负”交汇点上的项目：既真正吸引你，又能拓展你的能力边界，同时产生有意义的贡献。  

接着，为践行这种实验心态，请把你感兴趣的领域转化为一套微型操作协议，格式如下：  

我将在[时长]内完成[具体行动]。  
我将在[时长]内完成[具体行动]。  

无论是为期两周的原型开发、一个月的持续写作，还是围绕你真正关切的主题发表十篇论文——这类微小实验，为你提供了清晰的“沙盒”，让你得以真实探索、切实学习并稳步成长。  

实验结束后，请留出时间进行反思：哪些做法有效？哪些无效？基于所学所得，你接下来希望尝试什么？如此一来，你便能用一种“元认知循环”（metacognitive loop）取代前文所述那种危险的AI“糊弄循环”（slop loop）：每一次迭代，都成为一次自我发现的机会。  

在这个近乎全能的时代，满足好奇心从未如此轻而易举；然而，真正有意义的进步，依然要求你忠于自己的好奇心——不是同时追逐所有可能，而是选择一条引人入胜的路径，深入探索，直至某种意义从中自然浮现。
