<p><em>Imagine saving hundreds of hours and thousands of dollars each year—just by owning less. These 20 stats show how.</em></p>



<p>Most people I know want more time and money. In most cases, I can see why.</p>



<p>We’re stretched thin—trying to meet deadlines, pay bills, care for our families, and still find space for rest, joy, purpose, and meaning. The days feel short. The expenses pile up. And it’s easy to believe that the only solution is to work harder, earn more, or somehow fit more into an already full life.</p>



<p>But maybe there’s a better answer. What if the solution isn’t found in earning more, but in owning less?</p>



<p>Every item we buy has a cost—and not just in dollars. Our possessions cost us time to earn, time to maintain, and time to manage. And the quickest way to get more time and money back into our lives is to pursue minimalism.</p>



<p>And it turns out, the numbers back this up. Here are 20 statistics that show just how much minimalism can change our everyday by giving us more time and more money.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Money</h3>



<p>1. Americans waste $18,000 on &#8220;non-essential&#8221; expenses each year (<a href="https://nypost.com/2019/05/02/americans-waste-18k-on-non-essential-expenses-a-year/">source</a>)</p>



<p>2. Despite owning enough clothing to form an average of 135 outfits, Americans spend an average of $1,445 yearly on clothes and shoes. (<a href="https://lukezionjewelry.com/blogs/news/the-economics-of-style-americans-spend-1445-yearly-on-fashion">source</a>)</p>



<p>3. On average, people spend $360 each year on jewelry, whether for gifting others or buying for themselves—despite already owning 34 pieces of jewelry. Men, on average, spend more on themselves than women do. (<a href="https://www.shaneco.com/theloupe/articles-and-news/the-most-jewelry-obsessed-states/">source</a>)</p>



<p>4. Families spent $24 billion on toys annually. Parents spend an average of $240 on toys and games every year while grandparents spend $500. All while it&#8217;s estimated that 20-30% of toys are never played with. (<a href="https://www.jcfs.org/blog/less-more-toys-and-their-impact-childrens-cognitive-and-neurological-development">source</a>)</p>



<p>5. Americans spend $14.6 billion every year on home organization. (<a href="https://www.marketresearchfuture.com/reports/home-organization-products-market-21533">source</a>)</p>



<p>6. In the United States, people throw away over $473 billion worth of food annually—38% of all the food in America. (<a href="https://www.feedingamerica.org/our-work/reduce-food-waste">source</a>)</p>



<p>7. Nearly $10 billion in electronic devices—comprising computer equipment and devices, screens, and small electronic appliances—is thrown away every year in the US alone. (<a href="https://www.bcg.com/publications/2023/seizing-opportunity-ewaste-recycling">source</a>)</p>



<p>8. On average, Americans spend approximately $1,100 per year on coffee from coffee shops. (<a href="https://policyengineer.com/how-cutting-coffee-costs-can-safeguard-your-familys-future/#:~:text=On%20average%2C%20Americans%20spend%20approximately,up%20to%20a%20significant%20sum.">source</a>)</p>



<p>9. Nearly 25% of grocery shopping dollars are spent on processed foods and sweets—that amounts to $125/month for the average American household. (<a href="https://www.creditdonkey.com/grocery-shopping-statistics.html">source</a>)</p>



<p>10. In the US, consumers spend an average of $150 per month on impulse purchases. (<a href="https://www.statista.com/statistics/1330467/per-month-spending-on-impulse-purchases-usa/">source</a>)</p>



<p>11. The average American spends over $1,000 a year on subscriptions—$200 of it on unnecessary or unused subscriptions. (<a href="https://www.usatoday.com/story/money/2025/06/22/how-to-cancel-subscription-trends/84284851007/">source</a>)</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Time</h3>



<p>12. On average we spend <em>two hours per day</em> buying things and taking care of the things we already own. (<a href="https://ourworldindata.org/time-use">source</a>)</p>



<p>13. Americans spend nearly two hours a day shopping online at work. (<a href="https://www.cnbc.com/2018/12/11/americans-spend-nearly-two-hours-a-day-shopping-online-at-work.html">source</a>)</p>



<p>14. The average woman makes 301 trips to the store annually, spending close to 400 hours a year shopping. This amounts to 8.5 years spent shopping during a typical lifespan. (<a href="http://www.nydailynews.com/life-style/average-women-spend-399-hours-shopping-year-survey-finds-article-1.116819">source</a>)</p>



<p>15. The median size of a new American home has grown to 2,338 square feet from 983 square feet in 1950, driven partly by the need to store excess possessions, increasing housing costs. (<a href="https://www.nahb.org/blog/2022/03/new-single-family-home-size-continues-to-grow">source</a>)</p>



<p>16. 54% of Americans feel overwhelmed by clutter, with managing possessions elevating stress hormone levels, particularly for mothers, costing mental health and time. (<a href="https://www.forbes.com/sites/emmajohnson/2015/01/15/the-real-cost-of-your-shopping-habits/">source</a>)</p>



<p>17. Americans spend more than two full days online shopping per year. (<a href="https://nypost.com/2024/08/07/lifestyle/americans-spend-more-than-two-days-online-shopping-per-year-study/">source</a>)</p>



<p>18. The average American now spends 2.5 days per year (60 hours) looking for lost items, costing households $2.7 billion annually to replace misplaced possessions. (<a href="https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/lost-and-found-the-average-american-spends-25-days-each-year-looking-for-lost-items-collectively-costing-us-households-27-billion-annually-in-replacement-costs-300449305.html">source</a>)</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">More</h3>



<p>19. 81% of shoppers are willing to increase their online spending purchase just to meet a retailer’s free shipping threshold. (<a href="https://newsroom.fedex.com/newsroom/global-english/fedex-data-highlights-that-consumers-view-free-shipping-as-a-non-negotiable-for-cart-conversion">source</a>)</p>



<p>20. The national average credit card debt among cardholders with unpaid balances in 2025 averaged $7,321 (with much of it tied to unnecessary purchases), which results in an extra $120 billion in credit card interest and fees every year. (<a href="https://www.lendingtree.com/credit-cards/study/credit-card-debt-statistics/">source</a>)</p>



<p>The data doesn’t lie: when we own less, we can save more—both time and money. And with that, we gain something even more valuable—margin to live a better, more intentional life.</p>

## 中文译文

想象一下，仅通过减少拥有之物，每年就能节省数百小时和数千美元——以下20项统计数据将揭示其中奥秘。

我所认识的大多数人，都渴望更多的时间与金钱。在大多数情况下，我能理解这种渴望。

我们常常被拉扯得筋疲力尽——既要赶截止日期、支付账单、照顾家人，又要努力为休息、快乐、目标与意义留出空间。日子仿佛转瞬即逝，开支却不断累积；我们很容易便相信：唯一的出路，就是更拼命地工作、赚取更多收入，或设法在本已排满的日程中再塞进更多事情。

但或许，还有更好的答案。如果解决之道并非来自赚得更多，而是源于拥有更少呢？

我们购买的每一件物品，都有其成本——而这一成本远不止于金钱。我们的物品耗费我们赚钱的时间、维护的时间，以及管理的时间。而将时间与金钱重新收归己有的最快途径，正是践行极简主义。

事实证明，数据恰恰印证了这一点。以下是20项统计数据，清晰展现极简主义如何通过赋予我们更多时间与金钱，切实改变日常生活。

金钱方面：

1．美国人每年在“非必要”开支上浪费18,000美元。（来源）

2．尽管平均每人已拥有足够搭配出135套造型的衣物，美国人每年仍平均花费1,445美元购置服装与鞋履。（来源）

3．平均而言，人们每年在珠宝（包括送礼及自购）上花费360美元——而事实上，每人已平均拥有34件珠宝。男性在自身珠宝消费上的支出，甚至高于女性。（来源）

4．美国家庭每年在玩具上的总支出达240亿美元。其中，父母平均每年为孩子花费240美元购买玩具与游戏，祖父母则高达500美元；然而据估算，20%至30%的玩具从未被孩子玩过。（来源）

5．美国人每年在家居收纳整理上的花费达146亿美元。（来源）

6．在美国，民众每年丢弃的食物价值逾4730亿美元，占全美食物总量的38%。（来源）

7．仅在美国，每年就有近100亿美元价值的电子设备被丢弃——包括电脑设备与配件、显示屏及小型家用电器。（来源）

8．美国人平均每年在咖啡店购买咖啡的花费约为1,100美元。（来源）

9．近25%的食品杂货支出用于加工食品与甜食——对普通美国家庭而言，相当于每月125美元。（来源）

10．在美国，消费者平均每月在冲动消费上的支出为150美元。（来源）

11．普通美国人每年在各类订阅服务上的支出超过1,000美元，其中200美元用于无用或从未使用的订阅项目。（来源）

时间方面：

12．平均而言，我们每天花费两小时用于购物，以及照料已拥有的物品。（来源）

13．美国人平均每天在工作期间花近两小时进行线上购物。（来源）

14．普通美国女性每年约前往实体店购物301次，全年购物耗时近400小时；按此推算，一生中平均将有8.5年光阴耗费在购物上。（来源）

15．美国新建住宅的中位面积，已从1950年的983平方英尺增长至如今的2,338平方英尺——部分原因正是为储存过剩物品，而这直接推高了住房成本。（来源）

16．54%的美国人因杂物堆积而感到不堪重负；打理物品带来的压力甚至会升高皮质醇等应激激素水平，尤其对母亲群体影响显著，既损害心理健康，也吞噬宝贵时间。（来源）

17．美国人每年在线购物所耗时间超过整整两天。（来源）

18．如今，普通美国人每年平均花费2.5天（即60小时）寻找遗失物品；家庭因此每年额外支出27亿美元，用于补购丢失的物品。（来源）

其他方面：

19．81%的消费者愿意为凑足零售商设定的免运费门槛，而刻意增加线上购物金额。（来源）

20．截至2025年，持有未结清余额的美国信用卡持卡人，人均信用卡债务达7,321美元（其中大量债务源于非必要消费），由此每年额外产生1200亿美元的信用卡利息与手续费。（来源）

数据不会说谎：当我们拥有更少，我们便能真正节省更多——不仅是金钱，更是时间。而由此收获的，是比二者更为珍贵的东西：生活的余裕——让我们得以活出更美好、更有意识的人生。
