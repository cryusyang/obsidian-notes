<p>In October 2023, I opened a bank account with the hope of uncovering any vulnerabilities. I had tested this bank program before, however I had no luck; it is a very well-known Bank, so it was very hard to find something unless I really put the hours. I was getting invited to other programs at that time, so I eventually halted my efforts and decided to focus on these new opportunities.</p><p>After my running routine on November 17th, I returned home and, as usual, checked my mailbox. There, I discovered an offer from a bank. It dawned on me quickly that this was an offer from the same bank I had tested last month.</p><figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/643/1*jLqYd3DQfoCPaICBGuUmew.png" /></figure><p>Banks usually sends these offers to their customers to open new accounts, which tends to offer some benefits like “no annual fee and/or 0% intro APR”</p><p>After taking a shower following that long run, I sat down at my PC to access the offer.</p><p>I entered both reservation code and access code:</p><figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/534/1*utJNYTjAfon4au14Opt1zA.png" /></figure><p>The server then redirected me with an access-encoded key, something like</p><pre>https://apply.bxmbnbank.com/?ridNumber=U2fsdGXkX1%2FD2t6FFmuvS7zVam%2Bvp9avVFbxQYrTM1Sa6e7y876LHulztDoJvxue</pre><p>I accessed my offer and all my personal information was stored there, So I was curious if I can somehow decode the <em>ridNumber </em>to maybe try to access another user’s offer, I quickly looked into the response and found a parameter called <em>ridNumber</em> too, same as the one I had in the URL, but this had a numeric value, it wasn’t encoded.</p><figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/526/1*cfLzaheAZI9fGNtieg4rSw.png" /></figure><p>I thought, oh, this may be the decoded version, will it work?</p><pre>https://apply.bxmbnbank.com/?ridNumber=0075514045460235</pre><p>It did worked!</p><p>Surprisingly, the server accepted the decoded version. I began to wonder if it was vulnerable to IDOR. I modified the last two numbers and gained access to offer <em>0075514045460243</em>, which belonged to a completely different user. And just to make sure, I then tried <em>0075514045460241 </em>and <em>0075514045460255 </em>revealing the PII of those as well, stopped there and reported it</p><figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/1024/1*Cng8_8-VtBR4Ro67tVxKMw.png" /></figure><p>This vulnerability could have allowed an attacker to access other user’s offers, revealing sensitive personally identifiable information such as complete names, residential addresses, emails, phone numbers, and dates of birth.</p><p>I was surprised this issue hadn’t been reported before, considering this Bank has a very active bounty program. It’s also one of the biggest Banks in America.
