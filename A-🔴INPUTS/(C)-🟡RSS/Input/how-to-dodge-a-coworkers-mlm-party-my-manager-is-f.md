It’s five answers to five questions. Here we go…

这是五个问题的五个答案。下面开始……

<strong>1. How can I politely dodge a coworker’s MLM product party?</strong>

<strong>1. 如何礼貌地婉拒同事举办的多层次营销（MLM）产品聚会？</strong>

<blockquote><p>How do you politely dodge coworkers’ MLM “parties&#8221;? I despise multi-level marketing schemes (MLMs). They&#8217;re predatory, cult-like organizations, and I refuse to support them in any way.</p>
<p>A coworker recently invited me to her cookware-hawking &#8220;party.&#8221; Putting aside the fact that I rarely cook anything more elaborate than spaghetti, I really just can&#8217;t bring myself to support this. The problem is, this is a colleague who I like a lot and collaborate with regularly. I don&#8217;t want to lecture her about the toxic nature of these companies but it feels rude to just blow it off. I&#8217;d claim to be busy, but it&#8217;s an online event. How do I politely turn it down?</p></blockquote>

<blockquote>如何礼貌地婉拒同事组织的MLM“聚会”？我极度反感多层次营销（MLM）模式。它们是掠夺性的、类邪教式组织，我坚决拒绝以任何形式支持它们。

最近一位同事邀请我参加她推销厨具的“聚会”。先抛开我平时做饭几乎只限于意大利面这一事实不谈，我实在无法说服自己去支持这种活动。问题是，这位同事是我非常欣赏、且日常频繁协作的同事。我不想长篇大论地向她批判这类公司的毒性本质，但直接无视又显得很失礼。我本想以“太忙”为由推脱，可这是一场线上活动——那又该怎么得体地拒绝呢？</blockquote>

“I don’t really buy cookware, but thank you!”

“我平时不太买厨具，不过还是谢谢你！”

If she responds that you don’t need to buy anything and it’ll be fun just to attend: “They’re not really my thing, but thanks anyway.” (Or you could just say that from the start.)

如果她回应说你不必购买任何东西，单纯出席就很有趣：“这类活动不太符合我的兴趣，不过还是很感谢！”（或者你从一开始就直接这么说也可以。）

If you were someone she knew to be an avid cook, you could also say, “I’m super picky about cookware and only have a couple of brands I buy” or “I’m trying to be disciplined about not buying any new kitchen things.” And if she pushed after that: “It’s not really my thing, but thank you.”

如果你在她印象中是个热衷烹饪的人，你还可以这样说：“我对厨具特别挑剔，只认准几个固定品牌”；或“我正努力克制自己，不再添置任何新的厨房用品。” 如果她继续劝说，你就再次温和而坚定地说：“这类活动真的不太符合我的兴趣，不过还是谢谢你。”

MLMs often train their salespeople in how to overcome objections so any of these answers could spur her to try to change your mind (which would be especially <a href="https://www.askamanager.org/2019/02/my-staff-is-selling-multi-level-marketing-products-at-work-my-coworker-makes-r-rated-noises-and-more.html">inappropriate to do with a coworker</a>, but that doesn’t mean it won’t happen) but falling back on “it’s not really my thing” will work as long as you’re firm about sticking to it. (In fact, that&#8217;s often the case with boundaries &#8212; it almost doesn&#8217;t matter what specific you land on, as long as you are willing to stick to it.)

MLM公司通常会培训销售人员如何应对各种异议，因此以上任一回应都可能激发她试图说服你改变主意（对同事这样做尤其<a href="https://www.askamanager.org/2019/02/my-staff-is-selling-multi-level-marketing-products-at-work-my-coworker-makes-r-rated-noises-and-more.html">不合时宜</a>，但这并不意味着它不会发生）。不过，只要态度坚定、始终如一地使用“这类活动不太符合我的兴趣”这一说法，就足以有效设界。（事实上，设立边界往往正是如此——具体措辞其实没那么重要，关键是你是否愿意并能够坚持它。）

<strong>2. Manager is fixated on very old mistakes</strong>

<strong>2. 上司总是纠结于非常久远的错误</strong>

<blockquote><p>I&#8217;ve been working at my current job in mechanical design for a little over a year and a half. An inherent part of the design process in my industry is very long lead times for client feedback and other departments doing their portion of the design, which means it can be months before a design I have finished actually starts being built.</p>
<p>My boss frequently calls me in to lecture me about errors in projects I worked on a year or more ago, when I was still brand new and had very little experience with how the company did things, but weren&#8217;t noticed until production began more recently. I know I&#8217;ve improved significantly since those early days, and would never make the obvious mistakes I did early on, but my boss talks about these errors in the present tense as if they are happening now, and dismisses any explanation I offer about how long its been and how much I have improved. My coworker who started the same day I did gets treated the same way, and the two of us have already gotten one email from our boss&#8217;s boss about the errors we &#8220;are&#8221; making and how it costs the company money to fix.</p>
<p>For the most part my job is very satisfying. I enjoy the work, the hours are very flexible and open to WFH if needed, and aside from this issue my boss isn&#8217;t bad; they answer questions and explain things when I ask, leaving me alone to work at my own pace otherwise. But it&#8217;s frustrating and demoralizing to feel like I&#8217;m being judged and evaluated based on an image that is very much not reflective of my current work and I&#8217;m constantly concerned about being warned or even fired because of those past errors. Aside from privately tracking my corrected errors, which suffers from the same long delay between design and production, how can I prepare myself in case the department manager continues to get an outdated impression of my performance?</p></blockquote>

<blockquote>我在目前这家机械设计岗位上已工作了一年半多。在我所处的行业，设计流程天然存在漫长的周期：客户反馈滞后、其他部门完成各自部分也需要时间，这意味着我完成的设计方案，往往要等数月之后才真正进入制造阶段。

而我的上司却频频把我叫去，就我一年甚至更早以前参与的项目中的错误对我进行训诫——那时我才刚入职不久，对公司的工作方式几乎毫无经验，而这些错误直到最近生产启动后才被发现。我很清楚自己早已取得显著进步，绝不会再犯当初那些明显低级的错误；但上司却总用现在时态谈论这些错误，仿佛它们正在当下发生，并且完全无视我提出的解释：这些已是很久以前的事，而我早已今非昔比。与我同日入职的同事也遭遇同样对待，我们俩甚至已收到过上司的上司发来的邮件，指出我们“正在犯”的这些错误，以及公司为此付出的修复成本。

总体而言，这份工作让我感到非常满足：我喜欢工作内容，工时灵活，必要时还可远程办公；除了这个问题，我的上司其实并不差——我提问时她会耐心解答、细致说明，其余时间则充分放手让我按自己的节奏推进。但令人沮丧又伤士气的是，我感觉自己正被一个完全不能反映我当前实际表现的形象所评判和衡量；我甚至持续担忧，会因那些早已翻篇的旧错而遭到警告，乃至解雇。除了私下记录自己已修正的错误（而这同样受限于设计到生产的漫长延迟），若部门经理持续对我形成过时的绩效印象，我还能做些什么来提前准备？</blockquote>

Can you name it for your manager? For example: “You’ve pointed out a few errors to me recently that were from back when I first started, like X and Y, and I want to make sure you know that that’s not something I’m still doing currently — it was back from when I was learning the job and still figuring things out. I’m always grateful to get feedback, but I also don’t want you to worry that those are errors I’m still making.”

你能否直接点明这个问题，与上司坦诚沟通？例如：“您最近几次提到的几个错误，比如X和Y，其实都发生在我刚入职那会儿。我想确保您了解：这些并非我现在仍在重复的问题——那是我初学上手、还在摸索公司流程的阶段。我始终非常感激您的反馈，但也希望您不必担心，这些错误我如今已不会再犯。”

Depending on how that goes, you could also say, “Is there a good way for me to communicate than an error was from a year or more ago when I was still learning? I don’t want to sound defensive when you’re giving me feedback — I definitely want any feedback you have for me — but ideally I’d like you to know if it’s something from a while back that is no longer happening.” She may not have a good answer to that, but the act of asking it should help get it on her radar as a thing that’s happening.

视她对此的反应而定，你还可以补充：“有没有什么好办法，能让我在您反馈时，清晰传达某项错误其实是一年多前我尚在学习阶段发生的？我不希望听起来像在辩解——我真心欢迎并重视您的一切反馈——但我更希望您能知道，有些问题早已成为过去，不再发生。” 她未必能立刻给出理想方案，但主动提出这个问题本身，就能让她意识到：这种“旧错新判”的现象确实存在，且需要被关注。

<strong>3. Child care and hotel rooms when two spouses are attending the same work conference</strong>

<strong>3. 夫妻双方同赴同一场工作会议时的育儿安排与酒店住宿问题</strong>

<blockquote><p>I wrote in last year about <a href="https://www.askamanager.org/2025/04/my-friend-hired-me-and-isnt-paying-what-we-agreed-we-get-free-food-as-appreciation-but-i-cant-eat-any-of-it-and-more.html">my spouse’s company suddenly competing with mine</a> (<a href="https://www.askamanager.org/2025/12/updates-reporting-a-coworker-i-get-possessive-over-my-work-and-more.html">update here</a>). My spouse and I still aren&#8217;t bidding on the same work (thank goodness!), but we do still work in similar roles for separate clients in different industries. Turns out, both of those clients use the same vendor who hosts an important annual conference. We now may both be asked to attend the same conference!</p>
<p>In our previous, child-free life, that would be no problem. But per my previous update, we now have a baby to consider! We can&#8217;t both travel to the same conference without a childcare option. Our options would be flying a relative out to take care of the baby while we are traveling or bringing the baby with us and seeking a childcare option during the day (and likely evening with busy conference schedules!). Do you think we would have any grounds to ask for our companies to pay for childcare during the travel days? I doubt it, but curious about your opinion of what&#8217;s normal in cases like this. I have nightmares of us bringing the baby to the conference and switching off care between sessions. I&#8217;m not serious about that one, but could you imagine how awful it would be to attempt nap time behind a booth or in some random conference room?</p>
<p>Separately, what would we do about a hotel? It would be weird for us to travel and get two separate hotel rooms, but I couldn&#8217;t ask my company to pay for half of a hotel room, right? Does anyone else attend the same conference with their spouse for different companies and run into issues like this?</p></blockquote>

<blockquote>我去年曾写信咨询过<a href="https://www.askamanager.org/2025/04/my-friend-hired-me-and-isnt-paying-what-we-agreed-we-get-free-food-as-appreciation-but-i-cant-eat-any-of-it-and-more.html">我配偶所在公司突然与我公司形成竞争关系</a>的问题（<a href="https://www.askamanager.org/2025/12/updates-reporting-a-coworker-i-get-possessive-over-my-work-and-more.html">后续更新在此</a>）。目前，我和配偶虽仍未竞标同一项目（谢天谢地！），但确实在不同行业的不同客户方，从事高度相似的岗位。结果发现，这两位客户竟都采用同一家供应商——该供应商每年主办一场极其重要的行业会议。我们两人如今都有可能被各自公司指派参加同一场会议！

在我们从前没有孩子的生活里，这完全不是问题。但根据我之前的更新，我们现在有了宝宝需要照看！若无可靠的育儿安排，我们无法同时出差参会。可行方案包括：请一位亲属飞来照顾宝宝，或带宝宝一同前往，并在白天（很可能还包括晚上，毕竟会议日程通常非常紧凑）寻找临时托育服务。请问，我们是否有合理依据，向各自公司申请报销差旅期间的育儿费用？我对此表示怀疑，但很想听听您对这类情况“常规做法”的看法。我甚至做过噩梦：带着宝宝参会，在各场次间隙轮班照看——虽然这纯属夸张想象，但试想一下：在展台后面或某个随机的会议室里给宝宝哄睡，那场面得多糟糕啊！

另外，酒店住宿怎么安排？我们一同出差却各自订房，显然很奇怪；但我也不能要求公司报销一半房费，对吧？还有其他人遇到过类似情况吗——夫妻双方分别代表不同公司，却共同出席同一场会议？</blockquote>

You can&#8217;t really ask your company to pay for child care in a case like this; in all but the most unusual situations (where you have an extremely hard-to-find skill set and are wildly in demand) that would come across as out of touch. You’re generally expected to figure out child care or explain you can’t go. Is the latter an option for one of you?

在这种情况下，你实际上很难向公司申请育儿费用报销；除非极特殊情形（例如你拥有极其稀缺、市场极度渴求的技能），否则此举很容易显得脱离现实。通常，公司默认员工应自行解决育儿问题，或直接说明自己无法参会。那么，你们其中一人放弃参会，是否可行？

But if you do both go, for the hotel one of you would just tell your company that you don’t need them to book a hotel room because your spouse will also be there and you’ll be sharing a room.

但如果你们两人都决定参会，那么其中一人只需告知公司：“无需为我预订酒店房间，因为我的配偶也会出席，我们将共住一间房。”

<strong>4. Backing out of a summer job if I get a better offer</strong>

<strong>4. 若获得更优机会，能否退出已接受的暑期工作？</strong>

<blockquote><p>I&#8217;m a college student who recently applied to several summer internships in my dream industry. I&#8217;m pretty confident in how I presented myself, but I also want to be realistic about this pretty competitive industry, so I also applied to some local businesses as back-up summer jobs. The problem is, many of these local places have responded to me expressing interest much faster than the internships. If I get into an internship, I&#8217;ll definitely take it, but I don&#8217;t want to turn down any of my back-ups before I know that for sure.</p>
<p>What do I say if I get a hiring offer from a back-up job while I still have a chance at the internships? If I accept and then get a better opportunity, is there a tactful way to back out of that job, without seeming disrespectful or damaging my credibility with the business?</p></blockquote>

<blockquote>我是一名大学生，最近向梦想行业内的多家公司申请了暑期实习。我对自己的求职表现相当有信心，但也清醒认识到这一行业竞争激烈，因此我也向一些本地企业投递了简历，作为保底的暑期工作备选。问题在于，许多本地企业回复我的速度远快于实习项目。如果我最终拿到实习offer，我肯定会选择它；但在结果未定之前，我又不愿贸然回绝任何保底机会。

那么，如果我在实习结果出炉前，先收到了保底工作的录用通知，我该如何回应？倘若我接受了保底工作，之后又获得更优机会，是否存在一种得体的方式退出，既不显得失礼，也不损害我在该企业的信誉？</blockquote>

This is a thing that happens with summer jobs. They won’t be thrilled, but they’re unlikely to be shocked or outraged either. You’d simply say something like, “Unfortunately I’ve had a conflict come up and I won’t be able to work with you for the summer. I really appreciate you offering me the opportunity, and I wanted to let you know as soon as possible. I apologize for any inconvenience this causes, and I wish you and the team all the best for the summer.” They might be loath to hire you in the future, but that’s just how this stuff goes.

暑期工作领域本就常有此类情况。对方当然不会高兴，但也不太可能震惊或愤怒。你只需坦诚而简洁地说明：“很抱歉，我近期出现了一些突发状况，将无法在今年暑期加入贵公司工作。非常感谢您给予我这个宝贵机会，我也希望尽早将此消息告知您。由此带来的任何不便，我深表歉意，并衷心祝愿您和团队整个夏天一切顺利。” 他们未来或许不愿再雇佣你，但这就是这类事务的常态。

<strong>5. Is networking required to get a job now?</strong>

<strong>5. 如今找工作，人脉网络是否已成为必备条件？</strong>

<blockquote><p>I&#8217;m seeing a lot of stuff online saying that because the job market is so bad right now, the best way to get a job is through networking. On some posts you say networking is nice but <a href="https://www.askamanager.org/2022/02/is-networking-overrated.html">not a requirement</a>; you can still get jobs without it. Is that still true, or is networking now a must-have?</p>
<p>And if it is a must, what are some good ways to start networking with strangers? I&#8217;m job searching now but I&#8217;m not sure if I can rely on my current/former coworker network for jobs.</p></blockquote>

<blockquote>我看到网上大量信息称：由于当前就业市场形势严峻，找工作的最佳途径就是依靠人脉网络。而您在某些文章中则指出，人脉固然有益，但<a href="https://www.askamanager.org/2022/02/is-networking-overrated.html">并非必需</a>——不靠人脉，同样可以找到工作。这一观点如今是否依然成立？还是说，人脉网络如今已变成求职的硬性门槛？

如果它确实已成为刚需，那么，有哪些好的方法可以开始与陌生人建立职业联系？我目前正在积极求职，但不确定能否依赖现有或前任同事的人脉来获取工作机会。</blockquote>

Networking is helpful but not a must-have. People get hired without networking all the time!

人脉网络很有帮助，但绝非求职的硬性门槛！人们完全可以在不依赖人脉的情况下成功获得录用！

That said, it can make your job search easier, so it’s a good thing to do to whatever extent you <em>can</em>, because it can get your application an additional look that will help you stand out among a slew of qualified candidates.

话虽如此，它确实能让求职过程更轻松，因此，只要你力所能及，不妨尽可能尝试——因为它能让你的申请材料获得额外的关注，从而在众多合格候选人中脱颖而出。
