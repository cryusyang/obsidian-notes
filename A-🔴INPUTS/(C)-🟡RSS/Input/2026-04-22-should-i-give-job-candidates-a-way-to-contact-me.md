<p>A reader writes:</p>
<blockquote><p>Some colleagues and I have a question on interview etiquette from the interviewer side that we can’t agree on. If you give someone a job interview, should you give them a way to contact you?</p>
<p>My thinking is, yes, if you interview a job candidate, you should give them either your work phone or work email so they can follow up if they need to. For example, what if they need to withdraw their application? Or if they have a change of phone number or email address they need to inform you about? Or if they would just like to send a thank-you/follow-up email after the interview?</p>
<p>The other two managers on our team don’t like providing this information. They have had negative experiences in the past (one candidate calling way too often to check in, and another incident where a candidate called and yelled at the interviewer) and prefer to conduct phone interviews from a conference room phone line so their office number or work email isn’t shared.</p>
<p>My worry is we never go back and check that conference line’s messages so if someone calls and leaves a message, they may be frustrated if no one returns their calls. Also, I think how candidates do or do not follow up can give us good information on if we should hire them (for example, the guy who called and yelled was not hired!).</p>
<p>So what is considered standard practice? And does it change depending on first-round interviews vs second-round or in-person vs over the phone?</p></blockquote>

## 中文译文

一位读者来信写道：

我和几位同事就面试官一方的面试礼仪问题存在分歧，始终无法达成一致。假如您为某人安排了职位面试，是否应当向其提供一种联系您的方式？我的看法是：应该提供。如果您对求职者进行了面试，就应向其提供您的工作电话或工作邮箱，以便他们在必要时与您联系。例如，若他们需要撤回应聘申请；若他们的联系电话或电子邮箱发生变更，需及时告知您；又或者，他们只是想在面试后给您发送一封致谢信或跟进邮件。

然而，我们团队另外两位经理却不愿提供此类联系方式。他们过去曾有过不愉快的经历（比如有求职者频繁致电询问进展，还有一次某求职者来电后对着面试官大声斥责），因此更倾向于使用会议室电话线路进行电话面试，以避免泄露自己的办公电话号码或工作邮箱地址。

我担心的是，我们从不回头查看该会议室电话线路的语音留言。一旦有人致电并留下信息，却无人回复，对方难免感到沮丧。此外，我认为求职者是否主动跟进、以及如何跟进，本身就能为我们提供有价值的参考信息，帮助判断是否应录用此人（例如，那位打电话吼叫的求职者最终并未被录用）。

那么，业界普遍认可的标准做法究竟是什么？这种做法是否会因面试轮次不同（如初试与复试）或面试形式差异（如现场面试与电话面试）而有所变化？
