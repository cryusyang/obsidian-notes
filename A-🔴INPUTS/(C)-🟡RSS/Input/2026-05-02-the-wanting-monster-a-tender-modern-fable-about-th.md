> 文章探讨了“渴望”（wanting）作为欲望与需求之间的危险误差，揭示其如何破坏内心的宁静与完整，并将人困于永无止境的匮乏叙事中。作者援引狄金森与冯内古特等人的思想，强调“足够”（enough）作为幸福核心却常被忽视。随后引入马丁·默里与安娜·里德创作的童书《渴望怪兽》，以诗意寓言展现当人们真正安住于“足够”时，外在的匮乏叙事便失去力量——怪兽无法撼动安然生活的人们，唯当遇见内心不安者（如Mr. Banks），才得以借其 insecurity 植入“想要更多”的焦虑。

<p><a href="https://www.amazon.com/Wanting-Monster-Martine-Murray/dp/1592704190/?tag=braipick-20" target="_blank"><img alt="The Wanting Monster: A Tender Modern Fable about the Difficult Art of Resting at the Still Point of Enough" class="cover alignright size-medium" height="415" src="https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/thewantingmonster_cover.jpg?fit=320%2C415&amp;ssl=1" width="320" /></a></p><p>Wanting is the menacing margin of error between desire and need. It is the blade that vivisects your serenity, the hammer that shatters your wholeness &#8212; to want anything is to deem your life incomplete without it. It is a perpetual motion machine that keeps you restlessly spinning around the still point of enough. &#8220;Enough is so vast a sweetness, I suppose it never occurs, only pathetic counterfeits,&#8221; Emily Dickinson lamented in <a href="https://www.themarginalian.org/2018/12/10/emily-dickinson-love-letters-susan-gilbert/">a love letter</a> a century before Kurt Vonnegut, in his <a href="https://www.themarginalian.org/2014/01/16/kurt-vonnegut-joe-heller-having-enough/">shortest and most poignant poem</a>, located the secret of happiness in the sense of enough. Wanting is a story of scarcity writing itself on the scroll of the mind, masquerading as an equation read from the blackboard of reality. That story is the history of the world. But it need not be its future, or yours. </p>
<p>An epoch after <a href="https://www.themarginalian.org/2023/07/29/john-j-plenty-fiddler-dan/"><em>John J. Plenty and Fiddler Dan</em></a> &#8212; John Ciardi&#8217;s magnificent 1963 spell against the cult of more &#8212; author Martine Murray and artist Anna Read, living parallel lives close to nature in rural Australia, offer a mighty new counter-myth in <a href="https://www.amazon.com/Wanting-Monster-Martine-Murray/dp/1592704190/?tag=braipick-20" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><strong><em>The Wanting Monster</em></strong></a> (<a href="https://search.worldcat.org/title/1467887179" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><em>public library</em></a>) &#8212; an almost unbearably wonderful modern fable about who we would be and what this world would be like if we finally arrived, exhausted and relieved, at the still point of enough. Having always felt that great children&#8217;s books are works of philosophy in disguise, speaking great truth in the language of tenderness, I hold this one among my all-time favorites. </p>
<p><a href="https://www.amazon.com/Wanting-Monster-Martine-Murray/dp/1592704190/?tag=braipick-20" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-85264" height="472" src="https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/thewantingmonster21.jpg?resize=680%2C472&#038;ssl=1" width="680" /></a></p>
<p><a href="https://www.amazon.com/Wanting-Monster-Martine-Murray/dp/1592704190/?tag=braipick-20" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-85237" height="457" src="https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/thewantingmonster1.jpg?resize=680%2C457&#038;ssl=1" width="680" /></a></p>
<p>The story begins in a town so tranquil and content that no one notices the Wanting Monster, who stands sulking on the edge of the scene, part ghost out of a Norse myth, part Sendakian Wild Thing. </p>
<p>And so the Wanting Monster stomps over to the next village, &#8220;bellowing and crashing about as monsters do,&#8221; but still the magpie keeps singing, the bees keep laboring at the flowers, and the children keep playing in the square. The Wanting Monster redoubles the growling and the howling, but not even Billie Ray, &#8220;the littlest child of the village,&#8221; pays heed. </p>
<p><a href="https://www.amazon.com/Wanting-Monster-Martine-Murray/dp/1592704190/?tag=braipick-20" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-85263" height="453" src="https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/thewantingmonster22.jpg?resize=680%2C453&#038;ssl=1" width="680" /></a></p>
<p><a href="https://www.amazon.com/Wanting-Monster-Martine-Murray/dp/1592704190/?tag=braipick-20" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-85239" height="457" src="https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/thewantingmonster3.jpg?resize=680%2C457&#038;ssl=1" width="680" /></a></p>
<p>This inflicts no small identity crisis:</p>
<blockquote><p>What good was a monster if it couldn&#8217;t raise any trouble? If it couldn&#8217;t even raise the eyebrow of a small, curly-headed child? The Wanting Monster had its head in shame.</p></blockquote>
<p>But then it comes upon Mr. Banks, napping serenely by the stream. With that &#8220;terrible compulsion&#8221; that turns the insecure monstrous, the Wanting Monster moans its siren growl of want into the sleeping man&#8217;s ear.</p>
<p><a href="https://www.amazon.com/Wanting-Monster-Martine-Murray/dp/1592704190/?tag=braipick-20" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-85238" height="457" src="https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/thewantingmonster2.jpg?resize=680%2C457&#038;ssl=1" width="680" /></a></p>
<p><a href="https://www.amazon.com/Wanting-Monster-Martine-Murray/dp/1592704190/?tag=braipick-20" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><img src="https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/thewantingmonster5.jpg?w=680&#038;ssl=1" /></a></p>
<p><a href="https://www.amazon.com/Wanting-Monster-Martine-Murray/dp/1592704190/?tag=braipick-20" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-85262" height="453" src="https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/thewantingmonster23.jpg?resize=680%2C453&#038;ssl=1" width="680" /></a></p>
<blockquote><p>Mr. Banks began to wriggle. His heart began to jiggle. </p>
<p>A little note of misery sounded in his mind. </p>
<p>What could possibly be wrong? </p>
<p>It was a perfect day for a snooze by the stream. But now he wanted something else, something more.</p></blockquote>
<p>Suddenly, he wants the stream itself, shimmering so seductively in the sunlight that it has to be had. </p>
<p>As soon as Mr. Banks builds a swimming pool at his house and fills it with the stream&#8217;s water, Mr. Bishop perches to peek over the fence and begins &#8220;to twitch and prickle and hop around&#8221; with the restless desire for a pool of his own. </p>
<p>So goes the cascade of envy, that handmaiden of wanting, until pool by pool the streams begins to run dry. </p>
<p><a href="https://www.amazon.com/Wanting-Monster-Martine-Murray/dp/1592704190/?tag=braipick-20" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-85242" height="457" src="https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/thewantingmonster6.jpg?resize=680%2C457&#038;ssl=1" width="680" /></a></p>
<blockquote><p>Soon it was only a trickle.</p>
<p>The fish gasped and flapped, the frogs jumped away, and the reeds withered and died.</p></blockquote>
<p><a href="https://www.amazon.com/Wanting-Monster-Martine-Murray/dp/1592704190/?tag=braipick-20" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-85243" height="457" src="https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/thewantingmonster7.jpg?resize=680%2C457&#038;ssl=1" width="680" /></a></p>
<p>Triumphant and drunk on its own power, the Wanting Monster now wonders how much more damage it can do to these peaceful people. So it turns to Mrs. Walton next, who is gathering flowers in the field for her dear friend Maria, and whispers into her ear.</p>
<p><a href="https://www.amazon.com/Wanting-Monster-Martine-Murray/dp/1592704190/?tag=braipick-20" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-85244" height="457" src="https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/thewantingmonster8.jpg?resize=680%2C457&#038;ssl=1" width="680" /></a></p>
<blockquote><p>Mrs. Walton began to frown and fret. </p>
<p>She was irritated. Why was she picking flowers for Maria when it was really she herself who deserved them? </p>
<p>She should fill her own house with flowers. </p>
<p>Yes, she should have the most fragrant, the most colorful, the most stylish house in the whole village.</p>
<p>Everyone would admire it. Everyone would envy her.</p></blockquote>
<p>The other women watch Mrs. Walton pick all the flowers she can carry, and suddenly they too are aflame with the mania for owning the flowers. Soon, no flowers are left and the bees are bereft of pollen, the butterflies fly away, and the wrens and finches have nowhere to nest. </p>
<p><a href="https://www.amazon.com/Wanting-Monster-Martine-Murray/dp/1592704190/?tag=braipick-20" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-85245" height="457" src="https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/thewantingmonster9.jpg?resize=680%2C457&#038;ssl=1" width="680" /></a></p>
<p><a href="https://www.amazon.com/Wanting-Monster-Martine-Murray/dp/1592704190/?tag=braipick-20" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-85261" height="453" src="https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/thewantingmonster24.jpg?resize=680%2C453&#038;ssl=1" width="680" /></a></p>
<p>The Wanting Monster stomps across the flowerless fields, gloating. </p>
<p>That night, it visits Mr. Newton &#8212; the town&#8217;s most passionate stargazer &#8212; and whispers into Mr. Newton&#8217;s ear. </p>
<p><a href="https://www.amazon.com/Wanting-Monster-Martine-Murray/dp/1592704190/?tag=braipick-20" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-85246" height="457" src="https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/thewantingmonster10.jpg?resize=680%2C457&#038;ssl=1" width="680" /></a></p>
<p><a href="https://www.amazon.com/Wanting-Monster-Martine-Murray/dp/1592704190/?tag=braipick-20" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><img src="https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/thewantingmonster27.jpg?w=680&#038;ssl=1" /></a></p>
<p>Suddenly possessed with the desire to own the stars, he heads to the forest and cuts down a great old tree to build himself a ladder, then climbs into the night and takes a star.</p>
<p><a href="https://www.amazon.com/Wanting-Monster-Martine-Murray/dp/1592704190/?tag=braipick-20" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-85247" height="457" src="https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/thewantingmonster11.jpg?resize=680%2C457&#038;ssl=1" width="680" /></a></p>
<p>I am reminded here of this miniature etching by William Blake, which I suspect might have inspired Read&#8217;s art:</p>
<figure class="wp-caption aligncenter" id="attachment_83225"><a href="https://www.amazon.com/Wanting-Monster-Martine-Murray/dp/1592704190/?tag=braipick-20" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><img alt="" class="size-full wp-image-83225" height="988" src="https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Iwant_blake.jpg?resize=680%2C988&#038;ssl=1" width="680" /></a><figcaption class="wp-caption-text"></a> <em>I Want! I Want!</em> by William Blake, 1793. (Available as <a href="https://society6.com/product/i-want-i-want-by-william-blake-1793_print?curator=brainpicker" rel="noopener" target="_blank">a print</a> and as <a href="https://society6.com/product/i-want-i-want-by-william-blake-1793_cards?curator=brainpicker" rel="noopener" target="_blank">stationery cards</a>.)</figcaption></figure>
<p>Ms. Grimehart watches Mr. Newton and, unable to bear possessing no stars herself, she cuts down not one tree but two to make an even bigger ladder and snatches not one star but five. </p>
<p><a href="https://www.amazon.com/Wanting-Monster-Martine-Murray/dp/1592704190/?tag=braipick-20" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-85248" height="457" src="https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/thewantingmonster12.jpg?resize=680%2C457&#038;ssl=1" width="680" /></a></p>
<p>More and more ladders rise up and the sky soon grows starless. With the stream gone and the flowers gone and the forest gone, with the birds silent and the bees still, this tranquil little world finds itself unworlded.</p>
<p><a href="https://www.amazon.com/Wanting-Monster-Martine-Murray/dp/1592704190/?tag=braipick-20" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-85249" height="457" src="https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/thewantingmonster13.jpg?resize=680%2C457&#038;ssl=1" width="680" /></a></p>
<blockquote><p>The village was quiet and colorless and gloomy. The children wept. They had loved their forest and their little stream. They missed the singing birds, the sunlit flowers, the shining stars.</p></blockquote>
<p>People, unable to console the children, begin to leave. The Wanting Monster roars with self-congratulation. </p>
<p><a href="https://www.amazon.com/Wanting-Monster-Martine-Murray/dp/1592704190/?tag=braipick-20" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-85260" height="453" src="https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/thewantingmonster25.jpg?resize=680%2C453&#038;ssl=1" width="680" /></a></p>
<p><a href="https://www.amazon.com/Wanting-Monster-Martine-Murray/dp/1592704190/?tag=braipick-20" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-85250" height="457" src="https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/thewantingmonster14.jpg?resize=680%2C457&#038;ssl=1" width="680" /></a></p>
<p>This time, everyone hears the roar and begins to wonder about the menacing presence. It is Billie Ray who first sees it and, pointing, tells the townsfolk that there is a monster in their midst. Naming a hurt has a way of opening up the space for healing &#8212; as soon as the little girl names the menace, everyone sees it clear as daylight. Suddenly, the Wanting Monster grows &#8220;no bigger than a beetle.&#8221; It is only those things of which we are not fully conscious that have the power to possess us. </p>
<p>But when the grownups lurch to stomp the tiny monster, Billie Ray stops them, leans down and asks the suddenly helpless creature if it needs a cuddle. </p>
<p><a href="https://www.amazon.com/Wanting-Monster-Martine-Murray/dp/1592704190/?tag=braipick-20" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><img alt="" class="aligncenter size-full wp-image-85251" height="457" src="https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/thewantingmonster15.jpg?resize=680%2C457&#038;ssl=1" width="680" /></a></p>
<p><a href="https://www.amazon.com/Wanting-Monster-Martine-Murray/dp/1592704190/?tag=braipick-20" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><img src="https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/thewantingmonster16.jpg?w=680&#038;ssl=1" /></a></p>
<blockquote><p>The Wanting Monster climbed into the palm of her hand. It was tired, after all, and the hand was soft and warm. It lay down. Billie Ray cupped her other hand to make a roof, and then she wandered toward the dry river bed, where she sat on its banks and began to rock her hand and sing the lullaby her mother had once sung to her. </p>
<p>No one had ever sung to the Wanting Monster before. Nor had it ever been cared for. And the Wanting Monster didn&#8217;t know quite how those things felt &#8212; not really.</p></blockquote>
<p>Listening to the lullaby, the Wanting Monster begins to weep. &#8220;There, there,&#8221; Billie Ray comforts it, &#8220;Oh, dearest heart.&#8221; The Wanting Monster doesn&#8217;t know how to bear all this tenderness &#8212; how many of us really do &#8212; and so it goes on weeping &#8220;sorrowful, endless tears&#8221; that begin replenishing the stream. </p>
<p>Everyone else, listening and watching, begins to weep too.</p>
<p><a href="https://www.amazon.com/Wanting-Monster-Martine-Murray/dp/1592704190/?tag=braipick-20" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><img src="https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/thewantingmonster17.jpg?w=680&#038;ssl=1" /></a></p>
<blockquote><p>A great mournful lament filled the valley.</p>
<p>Tears swelled the little stream, and it rushed like a river&#8230; </p>
<p>What had been withheld was released; what had dried up, flowed. </p>
<p>What had hardened was becoming soft again.</p></blockquote>
<p>People unpack their suitcases, take the stars out of their pockets, and set about collecting seeds, tilling the ground, and filling watering cans to replant the trees and flowers. </p>
<p><a href="https://www.amazon.com/Wanting-Monster-Martine-Murray/dp/1592704190/?tag=braipick-20" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><img src="https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/thewantingmonster18.jpg?w=680&#038;ssl=1" /></a></p>
<p><a href="https://www.amazon.com/Wanting-Monster-Martine-Murray/dp/1592704190/?tag=braipick-20" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><img src="https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/thewantingmonster19.jpg?w=680&#038;ssl=1" /></a></p>
<p><a href="https://www.amazon.com/Wanting-Monster-Martine-Murray/dp/1592704190/?tag=braipick-20" rel="noopener" target="_blank"><img src="https://i0.wp.com/www.themarginalian.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/thewantingmonster20.jpg?w=680&#038;ssl=1" /></a></p>
<p>As the birds return and the night reconstellates, the Wanting Monster finally stops weeping and, looking up wonder-smitten at the stars lavishing the world with all that abundant beauty, feels, finally, slaked of want. </p>

## 中文译文

“想要”是欲望与需求之间那道充满威胁的误差边界。它是一把解剖刀，将你的宁静活体解剖；它是一柄铁锤，将你的圆满击得粉碎——只要有所欲求，便等于判定自己的生命若缺此物便残缺不全。“足够”本应如浩瀚甘泉般丰沛，我猜想它却从未真正降临，人们所能触及的，不过是一些可怜的赝品而已。艾米莉·狄金森在一个世纪前的一封情书中如此哀叹；而库尔特·冯内古特则在他最短也最动人的诗作中指出：幸福的秘密，正在于“足够”的感受。  
“想要”是一种匮乏叙事，在心灵的卷轴上自行书写，却伪装成从现实黑板上抄录下来的等式。这一叙事，正是人类世界的历史。但它不必成为它的未来，也不必成为你的未来。

在约翰·J·普伦蒂与小提琴手丹——约翰·契弗于1963年创作的、那部气势恢宏的反“越多越好”崇拜的咒语——问世一个时代之后，澳大利亚乡村作家玛蒂娜·默里与插画家安娜·里德，两位亲近自然、生活轨迹彼此平行的创作者，以《想要怪兽》（公共图书馆藏书）推出一则强劲有力的新反神话——这则几乎令人心碎的现代寓言，描绘了当我们终于精疲力竭却又如释重负地抵达“足够”的静止之点时，我们将会成为怎样的人，这个世界又将变成何等模样。我向来深信，伟大的儿童读物实为披着温柔外衣的哲学著作，以柔情的语言诉说至深的真理；而这本书，是我毕生挚爱之一。

故事始于一座安宁而满足的小镇，镇上的人们如此恬然自足，竟无人留意到那头“想要怪兽”——它正闷闷不乐地伫立在画面边缘，一半像北欧神话中飘忽的幽灵，一半似桑达克笔下狂野不羁的生灵。

于是，“想要怪兽”便大步迈向下一个村庄，“像所有怪兽那样咆哮嘶吼、横冲直撞”，可喜鹊依旧婉转歌唱，蜜蜂依旧在花间辛勤劳作，孩子们依旧在广场上嬉戏玩耍。“想要怪兽”加倍发出低吼与哀嚎，可就连比莉·雷——“村里最小的孩子”——也毫不理会。

这令它陷入一场不小的认同危机：

倘若一头怪兽连一点麻烦都惹不出，它还有什么用处？倘若它甚至无法让一个卷发小童挑起半根眉毛，它还有什么价值？“想要怪兽”羞愧地垂下了头。

倘若一头怪兽连一点麻烦都惹不出，它还有什么用处？倘若它甚至无法让一个卷发小童挑起半根眉毛，它还有什么价值？“想要怪兽”羞愧地垂下了头。

然而，它随后遇见了班克斯先生——他正安然酣睡于溪畔。出于那种令不安者走向狰狞的“可怕冲动”，“想要怪兽”朝着熟睡男子的耳朵，发出它那魅惑人心的、充满渴望的低吼。

班克斯先生开始扭动身体。他的心开始怦怦乱跳。一丝悲苦的音符悄然在他脑海中响起。究竟出了什么差错？这明明是溪边小憩的完美日子啊。可此刻，他却想要别的东西，更多东西。

刹那间，他渴望起那条溪流本身——它在阳光下粼粼闪烁，诱惑得令人非拥有不可。

班克斯先生刚在家院里修好一座泳池，并灌满溪水，主教先生便攀上篱笆探头张望，随即“焦躁地抽搐、刺痒、蹦跳”，被一股同样焦灼的拥有欲攫住，渴望拥有属于自己的泳池。

“想要怪兽”陶醉于自身力量，洋洋得意，不禁思忖：还能对这些平和的人们造成多大破坏？于是，它转向下一目标——沃尔顿太太。她正于田野中采摘鲜花，准备送给挚友玛丽亚；怪兽悄悄凑近她的耳畔，低语起来。

其他妇女目睹沃尔顿太太采走所有能携带的鲜花，霎时间也燃起疯狂的占有欲，争相抢夺。很快，田野再无一朵鲜花，蜜蜂失去花粉，蝴蝶飞离，鹪鹩与金翅雀亦无处筑巢。

“想要怪兽”踏过光秃秃的田野，洋洋自得。

当夜，它造访牛顿先生——镇上最热忱的观星者——并在他耳畔低语。

牛顿先生突然萌生占有星辰的强烈渴望，立刻奔入森林，砍倒一棵古老巨树，搭起一架长梯，攀上夜空，摘下一颗星星。

此时，我不禁想起威廉·布莱克这幅微型蚀刻画，我怀疑里德的插画或许正受其启发：

《我想要！我想要！》，威廉·布莱克，1793年。（可购印刷品及贺卡。）

格里姆哈特女士目睹牛顿先生之举，无法忍受自己手中空空如也，便不仅砍倒一棵树，而是两棵，搭起更巨大的梯子，不仅摘取一颗星，而是五颗。

越来越多的梯子拔地而起，夜空迅速变得星光尽失。溪流枯竭，鲜花凋尽，森林消逝，鸟鸣沉寂，蜂翼停歇——这个安宁的小世界，终至“失却世界”。

大人们无力抚慰孩子，纷纷离去。“想要怪兽”则得意地咆哮起来。

这一次，所有人都听见了咆哮声，开始揣测这股迫近的威胁。最先发现它的，是比莉·雷——她指着怪兽，告诉乡邻：“我们中间有一头怪兽！”为伤痛命名，往往开启疗愈的空间；小女孩刚一说出这威胁，众人便如见天光般清晰看见它。刹那间，“想要怪兽”骤然缩小，“只如甲虫般大小”。唯有那些我们尚未完全觉察之物，才真正拥有主宰我们的力量。

但当大人们踉跄扑来，欲踩死这渺小怪兽时，比莉·雷却拦住了他们，俯身轻问这忽然无助的生灵：“你需要一个拥抱吗？”

“想要怪兽”爬进她摊开的掌心。它毕竟已疲惫不堪，而那只手掌柔软温热。它躺了下来。比莉·雷用另一只手轻轻覆在它身上，为它搭起一座小屋。接着，她缓步走向干涸的河床，在岸边坐下，轻轻摇晃手掌，哼唱起母亲曾为自己吟唱过的摇篮曲。从来没有人给“想要怪兽”唱过歌，也从未有人照料过它。它根本不知晓这些感受究竟是什么——真的不知晓。
