<img alt="Mediation" height="133" src="https://www.pon.harvard.edu/wp-content/uploads/images/posts/mediation-1.jpg" title="Mediation" width="200" /><br /><p>What makes a good mediator? And how is it that mediators—despite having no authority to impose a solution—are nonetheless able to guide even bitterly divided parties toward agreement? These questions sit at the heart of effective dispute resolution and point to the subtle skills, strategies, and judgment that allow mediators to help adversaries find common ground where none seemed possible.</p>
<p>Of course, serious <a href="https://www.pon.harvard.edu/courses-and-training/harvard-negotiation-institute-5-day/mediation-workshop/">mediation training</a> and substantive expertise are critical, as is keen analytic skill. But according to a survey by Northwestern University law professor Stephen Goldberg, veteran mediators believe that establishing rapport is more important to effective <a href="https://www.pon.harvard.edu/category/daily/mediation/">mediation</a> than employing specific <a href="https://www.pon.harvard.edu/tag/mediation-techniques/">mediation techniques</a> and tactics.</p>
<p>To gain parties’ trust and confidence, rapport must be genuine: “You can’t fake it,” one respondent said. Before people are willing to settle, they must feel that their interests are truly understood. Only then can a mediator reframe problems and float creative solutions.</p>
<p>Goldberg’s respondents could report only their own perceptions about why they succeed, of course. A detached observer or the parties themselves might have very different explanations. Indeed, one of the tenets of <a href="https://www.pon.harvard.edu/daily/mediation/make-the-most-of-mediation/">mediation practice</a> is to work subtly so that parties leave feeling as if they have reached accord largely on their own, a strategy that is meant to deepen their commitment to honor the agreement.</p>
<p>In an earlier study by mediator Peter Adler, his colleagues explained their success by discussing “the breakdowns, breakthroughs, and the windows of opportunities lost or found.” By contrast, participants in the same cases remembered the mediators only as “opening the room, making coffee, and getting everyone introduced.”</p>
<p>This research offers two lessons for <a href="https://www.pon.harvard.edu/tag/negotiators/">negotiators</a>—including those who must resolve disputes and make deals without the help of a third party. One is the importance of relationship building, especially in contentious situations. Some measure of trust is required before people will open up and reveal their true interests. The other is that a hallmark of an artful process is that others do not feel maneuvered or manipulated.</p>

## 中文译文

一名优秀的调解员应具备哪些素质？为何调解员——尽管并无强制达成解决方案的权力——却依然能够引导彼此敌对、分歧严重的各方走向共识？这些问题直指高效纠纷解决的核心，也揭示了调解员所依赖的精微技能、策略与判断力：正是凭借这些能力，他们才能帮助对立双方在看似毫无共同点之处找到共识。

当然，扎实的调解培训、深厚的专业知识以及敏锐的分析能力都至关重要。然而，据西北大学法学院教授斯蒂芬·戈德伯格（Stephen Goldberg）开展的一项调查表明，经验丰富的调解员认为，建立融洽关系比运用特定的调解技巧与策略更为重要。

要赢得当事各方的信任与信心，这种融洽关系必须是真诚的：“你无法假装出来。”一位受访者如是说。在人们愿意达成和解之前，他们必须真切感受到自己的利益已被充分理解。唯有如此，调解员才能重新界定问题，并提出富于创意的解决方案。

当然，戈德伯格的受访者所能报告的，仅限于他们自身对成功原因的认知；旁观者或当事人本人或许会给出截然不同的解释。事实上，调解实践的一项基本原则，恰恰在于以潜移默化的方式开展工作，使各方最终感觉协议主要是靠自己达成的——这一策略旨在增强他们信守协议的意愿与责任感。

在调解员彼得·阿德勒（Peter Adler）早先的一项研究中，其同行将自身成功归因于“那些陷入僵局的时刻、实现突破的契机，以及那些错失或抓住的转瞬即逝的机遇”；而同一案件的当事人却只记得调解员“打开会议室门、煮好咖啡、并帮大家相互介绍”。

这项研究为谈判者——包括那些必须在无第三方协助的情况下自行解决纠纷、达成交易的人——提供了两点启示：其一，在充满争议的情境中，构建人际关系尤为关键；人们只有在建立起一定程度的信任之后，才会敞开心扉，坦陈自身的真实利益。其二，一项高明流程的显著标志，正在于他人不会感到被操控或被操纵。
