<p>A reader writes:</p>
<blockquote><p>My position was recently eliminated.</p>
<p>Now former colleagues are texting me to ask questions. I don&#8217;t feel obligated to answer. What are some response options when I want to politely say no?</p></blockquote>
<p>You’re not obligated to answer questions when you’re no longer working there. That said, it’s also true that if you want to keep good relationships with these colleagues and might need them for job leads or references (informal or otherwise) in the future, you might not want to take a completely black-and-white line on this.</p>
<p>You definitely shouldn’t do work of any real substance when you’re no longer getting paid — like a detailed update on the history of a project or a rundown of the best way to approach a client — but if it’s a very simple question like “where is the key for the X filing cabinet?” that you could answer in a single sentence, it can be to your benefit to answer, because you want to maintain those relationships. Even then, there are limits; if you’re getting multiple questions like that, it’s reasonable to stop helping. But one or two very simple questions? Those are usually in your best interests to answer.</p>
<p>If you’re being asked for more then that, though, then any of these are reasonable to say:</p>
<p>* “I don’t think I can help since I’m no longer working there — I’m sorry about that!”</p>
<p>* &#8220;I don’t have access to that anymore now that I’ve left.”</p>
<p>* “I’m not sure off the top of my head — sorry!”</p>
<p>* “I’m not sure off the top of my head, but check the files I left behind.”</p>
<p>* “I can’t keep answering questions now that I’m gone, but try checking the manual.”</p>
<p>If it’s a colleague you particularly like or have good rapport with, you could say, “I know you’re in a tough spot since you’re trying to get this done, but since I’m not being paid anymore, I’m not comfortable continuing to help with the work.&#8221;</p>
<p>And if it’s a really large number of questions on substantive things <i>and</i> you’d be willing to help if they paid you, you could say, “I’m getting a lot of requests for help with things like this. I’d be willing to set up a consulting arrangement for a set number of hours of time over the next month or two if you want to do that.&#8221; (I&#8217;ve noticed people like to suggest quoting an outrageously fee for that, but that&#8217;s not in your interests either. A fair rate, yes, but not an obscene one just because you want to stick it to them; that&#8217;ll just make your judgment look really off. If you want stick it to them, you&#8217;re better off skipping this altogether.)</p>
<p>But if you’re not particularly interested in maintaining these relationships and don’t think you’ll want to call on them for any sort of help in the future, you can also just ignore the messages. You’re not obligated to respond.</p>

## 中文译文

一位读者来信写道：

我的职位最近被裁撤了。如今，一些前同事仍通过短信向我提问。我并不觉得自己有义务回答这些问题。那么，当我想要礼貌地拒绝时，有哪些合适的回应方式呢？

我的职位最近被裁撤了。

如今，一些前同事仍通过短信向我提问。我并不觉得自己有义务回答这些问题。那么，当我想要礼貌地拒绝时，有哪些合适的回应方式呢？

你已不再为该公司工作，因此确实没有义务回答任何问题。不过话说回来，如果你希望与这些前同事保持良好关系，未来可能还需要他们为你提供求职线索或推荐信（无论是正式还是非正式的），那么对这个问题也未必需要采取非黑即白、完全拒绝的态度。

你当然不应再承担任何实质性的、本应由在职员工完成的工作——例如，详细梳理某个项目的来龙去脉，或系统说明如何最佳地对接某位客户——毕竟你已不再领取薪水。但若只是非常简单的问题，比如“X号文件柜的钥匙放在哪儿？”，只需一句话就能作答，那不妨予以回复。这样做有助于维系彼此关系。即便如此，帮助也须有度：倘若类似问题接二连三，你完全有权停止协助。但偶尔一两件极简单的小事，通常回答一下反而更符合你的长远利益。

倘若对方所提问题已超出上述范畴，那么以下任何一种回应都是合情合理的：

* “很抱歉，我现在已不在公司任职，恐怕帮不上忙了！”  
* “离职后，我已无法访问相关资料了。”  
* “一时想不起来，抱歉！”  
* “一时想不起来，但你可以查阅我留下的那些文件。”  
* “我已离职，不便继续回答各类问题，建议你参考操作手册。”

如果你和某位同事关系特别融洽、彼此信任，也可坦诚表示：“我知道你现在处境不易，正急着把事情做完；但既然我已不再领薪，继续帮忙处理工作就不太合适了。”

倘若对方提出大量实质性问题，而你又愿意在付费前提下提供协助，也可以这样回应：“我最近收到不少类似的求助请求。如果你们有需要，我很乐意在接下来一两个月内，以顾问形式提供固定时长的支持服务。”（有人喜欢借此机会报出一个高得离谱的报价，但这对你并无益处。合理收费当然可以，但刻意漫天要价，只为‘出口气’，只会让人觉得你缺乏判断力。真想‘出口气’，不如干脆跳过这一步，直接不予理会。）

当然，如果你压根儿就不打算维系这些关系，也不认为将来会需要他们的任何帮助，那么选择忽略这些消息也完全没问题——你本就没有义务回复。
