<p>I recently <a href="https://www.overcomingbias.com/p/finish-the-industrial-revolution">said</a> our civ will fall if we do not finish the industrial revolution, and apply the industry trio of math, big orgs, and capitalism to more areas of life. Especially our fast activism-driven evolution of values, morals, and norms.</p><p>But watching a <a href="https://www.pbs.org/show/henry-david-thoreau/">documentary</a> on early activist H.D. Thoreau brought home to me just how huge an ask this seems. Our modern world has come to deeply adore and revere changing its morals fast via <a href="https://www.overcomingbias.com/p/is-modernism-due-to-youth-culture">youth movements</a>, and a great many features of our modern world support this new pattern.</p><p>For example, youths are generally more risk-taking, emotionally expressive, eager to impress potential mates, less invested in prior arrangements, and better able to bond together into groups. Which attracts youths to the chance to skip the usual dues to rise fast in status as leaders of new tightly-bonded emotional youth movements.</p><p>Helping further, we legitimized fashions, seeing those who first adopt new popular changes as more virtuous. And we put kids together in high school and college, where they have more time for activism, bond into their own youth cultures, and are taught to see the world more abstractly and thus morality more simply and universally. Also, better communication tech has let them coordinate faster across wider distances.</p><p>Finally, the modern world has widely adopted the views (a) that morality is a whole separate realm where the usual adult knowledge and experience are less relevant, (b) that moral opinions should from come <a href="https://www.overcomingbias.com/p/authenticity-as-grace">authentically</a> from within, and (c) that youthful opinions on morals tend to be less corrupted by habit and self-interest.</p><p>All of this has created a perfect storm encouraging youth to repeatedly make and join new internal-feelings-driven moral crusades, movements maximally suspicious of opposing older adults with ties of interest and habits to the existing order.</p><p>Could we apply industry to more strongly to manage this process? For example, by <a href="https://www.overcomingbias.com/p/culture-guiding-futarchy">paying</a> big orgs to create, suppress, and influence such movements to achieve key <a href="https://www.overcomingbias.com/p/toward-adaption-futures">metrics</a>. Yes, big orgs do substantially influence youth movements today, but mostly from behind the scenes. And these are mostly not for-profit orgs, and our world is pretty hostile to for-profit orgs operating outside their usual scopes, especially in sacred areas like moral activism. Social media feed algorithms seem to be the main form of this now, but I doubt they could do that much more than they do now.  </p><p>We should do our best to try, but damn does this look hard.</p>

## 中文译文

我最近曾指出：倘若我们未能完成工业革命，并将数学、大型组织与资本主义这“产业三要素”更广泛地应用于社会生活的更多领域，我们的文明终将衰落。尤其在价值观、道德观与行为规范这一领域——当前它正由激进主义驱动而加速演进。

然而，观看一部关于早期行动主义者亨利·戴维·梭罗（H.D. Thoreau）的纪录片，却让我真切意识到：实现上述目标究竟有多艰难。当今世界已深深迷恋并尊崇一种模式——即通过青年运动快速改变道德观念；而现代世界的诸多特征，恰恰都在支撑并强化这一新模式。

例如，年轻人通常更敢于冒险、情感表达更外显、更渴望向潜在伴侣展现自我价值、对既往约定投入较少，也更善于凝聚成紧密的群体。这些特质，自然吸引着年轻人投身于新型情绪化青年运动——他们得以绕过常规晋升路径，迅速跃升为这类高度内聚型运动的领导者。

此外，我们还赋予了“时尚”以正当性，视那些率先采纳新兴流行趋势者为更具德性之人。同时，我们将青少年集中于高中与大学校园之中：他们在此拥有更多时间参与行动主义活动，自发形成属于自己的青年文化，并被教导以更抽象的方式看待世界，从而将道德理解得更为简明、更具普适性。再者，通信技术的进步，亦使他们得以跨越更广地域、更迅捷地协调行动。

最后，现代社会普遍接受了三种观念：（a）道德是一个完全独立的领域，在此领域中，成年人所积累的常识与经验相关性较低；（b）道德观点必须发自内心、真实自然；（c）青年人的道德判断往往较少受到陈规陋习与私利考量的腐蚀。

所有这些因素共同酿成了一场“完美风暴”，不断激励青年反复发起并加入由内在感受驱动的道德 crusade（道德讨伐运动）——而这些运动对一切持异议的年长者抱有最大警惕，尤其怀疑那些与既有秩序存在利益牵连或习惯性依附的成年人。

那么，我们能否将“产业逻辑”更有力地引入这一进程的管理之中？譬如，由大型组织受托创设、压制或引导此类运动，以达成关键绩效指标？事实上，大型组织如今确已在相当程度上影响青年运动，但大多隐于幕后；且其中多数并非营利性机构。而我们的社会整体上对营利性组织涉足其常规业务范围之外的领域——尤其是道德行动主义这类被视为神圣的领域——抱持强烈敌意。目前，社交媒体的信息流算法似乎是这一逻辑最主要的体现形式，但我怀疑，其作用空间恐怕已接近极限，难有显著拓展。

我们理应竭尽全力尝试，但天哪，这看起来实在困难至极。
